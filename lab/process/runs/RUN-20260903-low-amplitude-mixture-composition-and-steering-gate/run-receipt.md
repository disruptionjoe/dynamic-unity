`HC-DU-228`. A complete operational state, ordinary classical randomization,
and one fixed unconditional response jointly force convex affinity. The
literal cutoff cannot satisfy all three.

For `P=|1><1|` and `c=1/4`, the equal `Z` and `X` pure-state ensembles both
average to `I/2`. Every fixed quantum effect gives their mean responses equal.
The cutoff gives `3/8` and `1/4`, an exact `1/8` contrast. Applying the rule to
the density operator gives one decomposition-independent value but conflicts
with randomized pure-state calibration. Applying it to pure ensemble members
lets a remote Bell partner's `Z/X` measurement choice change the local
unconditional response by `1/8`.

Adding a preparation-route label repairs mathematical factorization, but only
by enlarging the state. DU additionally requires that provenance to be a
physically formed, locally accessible process record before it can influence
the detector. An inaccessible remote route cannot do so without signalling.
