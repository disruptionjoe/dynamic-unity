## Frozen theorem target

Let \(X\) be a finite history set. Freeze:

1. a formation/provenance target \(s:X\to S\);
2. a relational-character target \(c:X\to C_\chi\);
3. a stage archive \(a_\tau:X\to A_\tau\);
4. a horizon evolution \(d:A_\tau\to A_H\), with
   \(a_H=d\circ a_\tau\); and
5. the joint target \(t=(s,c)\).

Prove or kill:

1. \(a_H\) exactly certifies the joint target iff
   \[
   \ker a_H\subseteq\ker s\cap\ker c;
   \]
2. when \(a_\tau\) already certifies \(t\), persistence to the horizon is
   equivalent to \(d\) being injective on the target-bearing quotient of
   \(a_\tau(X)\);
3. the marked-stage, character-resource, and persistence obligations are
   logically independent through exact finite positive/hostile controls;
4. the three previously banked physical lemmas compose only after their
   different assumptions and target predicates are typed; and
5. a higher-order or hyperbolic response structure may supply causal
   response/resource structure without thereby selecting a stage or archive.

## Exact controls and cheapest kill

Construct finite controls for:

1. stage plus persistence without the nontrivial character;
2. character resource plus persistence without formation provenance;
3. stage plus character at an intermediate time followed by erasure at the
   declared horizon;
4. all three obligations satisfied in one explicitly supplied architecture;
5. a persistent preloaded value that does not certify a write occurrence;
6. a reversible embodied relation that supports one bounded action while
   retaining no historical archive; and
7. an ordinary classical marked archive showing that symmetry reduction is
   not a universal record requirement.

The **cheapest kill** of the scoped theorem is a finite history pair with the
same horizon archive and different joint target despite the kernel
criterion, or a noninjective horizon map that nevertheless preserves every
target-bearing distinction. The **cheapest kill** of the proposed universal
ladder is expected and counts as a positive return: one exact record outside
the nontrivial-character scope.
