# Stochastic multiplexing observability gate

## Purpose and North-Star relation

`HC-DU-095--097` show that a compact target-separating physical capability
surface can be finite-reduced, but only a uniform physical observability
modulus makes the packet quantitative.

This run tests whether one stochastic broadband source and one stochastic
readout can supply that modulus without replacing a finite record by an
infinite-bandwidth source, an infinite-time correlation limit, or a supplied
target-coded probability law.

## Frozen typed objects

Let:

- \((\Theta,d)\) be the compact physical completion class;
- \(T:\Theta\to Z\) be the held-out target and \(K_\delta\) its
  target-distinct pair space;
- \(H_{\rm in}\) and \(H_{\rm out}\) be separable source and readout Hilbert
  spaces;
- \(R_\theta:H_{\rm in}\to H_{\rm out}\) be the physical response operator;
- \(Q\) and \(S\) be source and readout covariance operators;
- \(W\sim N(0,Q)\) and \(V\sim N(0,S)\) be independent physical random source
  and readout weights; and
- \(m_{W,V}(\theta)=\langle V,R_\theta W\rangle\) be the retained scalar
  response.

Keep distinct:

1. pairwise almost-sure separation;
2. one draw simultaneously separating an entire completion class;
3. a uniform positive response margin;
4. finite source energy and bandwidth;
5. finite observation duration and retained record dimension;
6. source/readout realization provenance;
7. complete attempted-process acquisition; and
8. physical selection of \(Q,S\) rather than mathematical choice.

## Exact questions

1. Which Gaussian moment condition yields an explicit small-ball modulus for
   \(m_{W,V}\)?
2. Does pairwise almost-sure separation imply one stochastic draw is
   injective on an uncountable completion class?
3. Can an infinite-dimensional source space carry a nondegenerate isotropic
   finite-energy Gaussian law?
4. What do the white-noise single-measurement inverse theorems actually
   retain, and where do they use infinite time or distribution-valued data?
5. Which extra assumption makes published finite-measurement inverse
   theorems quantitative?
6. Does any audited active-wave theorem supply a uniform
   noise-weighted response gap, bounded scalar readout, and complete finite
   acquisition?

## Assumption and warrant discipline

- `STANDARD`: Hilbert-space Gaussian moments, Paley--Zygmund, trace-class
  covariance, compactness, and finite-dimensional inverse-problem controls.
- `IMPORTED`: white-noise inverse-wave uniqueness, finite-measurement

- `PROJECT_NATIVE`: typed source/readout provenance, complete acquisition,
  finite certified record, target-independent interface, no-refit transfer,

  Hilbert--Schmidt gap, response Lipschitz bound, bounded-resource sampling,
  and finite retained acquisition.

## Strongest absorbers

- Gaussian random design and small-ball methods;
- observability inequalities;
- finite-measurement inverse problems on finite-dimensional model classes;
  and
- compressed sensing and random tomography.

## Cheapest positives and kills

Cheapest constructive positive:

- derive a universal pairwise small-ball probability for the bilinear
  Gaussian scalar response from a uniform lower bound on
  \(\|S^{1/2}(R_\theta-R_{\theta'})Q^{1/2}\|_{\rm HS}\).

Cheapest quantifier kill:

- random scalar projection of the unit circle separates every fixed pair
  almost surely while every realized projection remains globally
  noninjective.

Cheapest finite-energy kill:

- prove that a fully unitary-invariant Hilbert-space Gaussian covariance must
  be \(cI\), which is trace class only for \(c=0\) in infinite dimension.

Cheapest physical-arena kill:

- show that “one white-noise measurement” requires the complete incident and
  returned distribution-valued traces and an infinite-time correlation
  limit, without a finite-time uniform target margin.

## Stops

- do not interchange pairwise probability-one events with one uniform event;
- do not treat mathematical white noise as bounded physical source energy;
- do not use a Gaussian law without typing who selects its covariance;

- do not activate an active-wave successor without a finite physical
