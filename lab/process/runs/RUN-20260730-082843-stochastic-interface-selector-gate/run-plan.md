## Exact question

Let a physical antecedent space \(X\) and candidate-interface fibres
\(\mathcal I_x\) carry a symmetry action by \(G\).

1. When does a deterministic equivariant selector
   \(s:X\to\mathcal I\) exist?
2. When does an equivariant Markov kernel
   \(K:X\to\mathcal P(\mathcal I)\) exist even though no point selector does?
3. When does even a normalized stochastic selector fail?
4. Does selection of \(K\) select a realized interface, occurrence,
   provenance-bearing record, archive, or action capability?
5. Can two physically different labelled instruments have the same
   unlabelled channel and therefore defeat any endpoint-only inference from
   the selected distribution?

## Pre-registered theorem targets

### Theorem A — deterministic/stochastic stabilizer criterion

For an orbit representative \(x\) with stabilizer \(G_x\):

- deterministic equivariant selectors on \(Gx\) correspond to points of
  \(\mathcal I_x\) fixed by \(G_x\);
- equivariant Markov kernels on \(Gx\) correspond to probability measures on
  \(\mathcal I_x\) invariant under \(G_x\); and
- deterministic selection is the special case of an invariant Dirac
  measure.

Thus stochastic selection can exist when deterministic selection does not.

### Theorem B — existence is not universal

- On a nonempty finite fibre with a finite stabilizer, orbit averaging always
  supplies an invariant probability.
- For a compact stabilizer acting continuously on a compact fibre, Haar
  averaging supplies an invariant probability.
- A noncompact homogeneous orbit need not admit one. In particular, the
  proper-orthochronous Lorentz orbit of future-unit timelike directions
  \(H^3\) admits no normalized Lorentz-invariant Borel probability.

This should preserve the prior Minkowski observer-direction obstruction while
opening a stochastic-only class on compact quantum-interface orbits.

### Theorem C — law, sample, and record do not collapse

Even when the antecedent uniquely selects an equivariant kernel \(K\), it
selects only a conditional law. A realized interface requires a physical
sampling instrument. A formed record additionally requires a retained,
accessible carrier whose later response depends on the sampled value.

An unlabelled channel cannot recover this structure when two labelled
instruments have the same coarse-grained channel.

## Exact controls

1. **Finite orbit:** \(C_3\) acts transitively on three interfaces. No point
   is fixed; the uniform stochastic selector is unique.
2. **Symmetry-breaking repair:** adding one physical orientation token
   point-selects the corresponding interface.
3. **Noncompact hostile control:** integer translations on \(\mathbb Z\)
   admit no normalized translation-invariant probability; the Lorentz
   \(H^3\) argument is the continuous physical analogue.
4. **Quantum instrument A:** uniformly select \(X,Y,Z\) dephasing, retain the
   selected axis in a classical archive, and verify that the unlabelled
   channel shrinks every Bloch coordinate by \(1/3\).
5. **Quantum instrument B:** a different labelled Pauli-error instrument
   realizes the same unlabelled channel.
6. **First leak:** conditional future responses differ when the archive is
   accessible, while erasing the archive makes the two realizations
   endpoint-equivalent.
7. **Representation control:** jointly permuting axes, archive labels, state,
   and readout preserves every statistic.
