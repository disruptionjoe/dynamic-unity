# Local-QFT realizability and record-algebra selection gate

## Cold-start contract

Dynamic Unity asks whether independently selected, observer-indexed certified
causal records reconstruct all observer-accessible time, geometry, fields, and
capability, or expose a finite physical remainder. `HC-DU-117` admitted Araki
relative entropy as a type-III-safe diagnostic only after the record algebra
and channel have been selected. It did not identify what physics selects.

This run tests the strongest local-QFT candidate for that missing selector:

> Do local quantum-field measurement schemes and generalized-symmetry net
> structure physically select a record carrier algebra and channel strongly
> enough to determine a held-out observer target, or do the probe, coupling,
> state, readout, region, algebra completion, and access class remain supplied?

The question deliberately combines two mature results without treating their
composition as earned:

1. Fewster--Verch-type schemes distinguish causal/localizable instruments from
   arbitrary formal quantum operations; and
2. generalized-symmetry AQFT distinguishes additive local algebras from larger
   algebras containing nonlocally generated operators.

The run asks whether these are physical selectors, physical filters, or merely
typed inputs to a record theorem.

## Primary-source boundary

- Fewster and Verch's measurement scheme derives an induced system observable
  only from a specified system, probe, compact coupling, probe preparation,
  and measured probe observable.
- Mandrysch and Navascués prove a movable Fewster--Verch cut for a substantial
  class of smeared-field measurements, but still define realizability through
  a probe theory, probe state, scattering morphism, and probe POVM.
- Mandrysch, Simmons, and Navascués show in the free scalar field that
  causality and Fewster--Verch realizability are not equivalent: some causal
  channels cannot even be approximated by those schemes, while a wide class
  of random-displacement instruments is realizable. Realizability is
  therefore a nontrivial filter, not a uniqueness theorem.
- Casini, Huerta, Magán, and Pontello show that generalized symmetries may
  appear as failures of additivity or Haag duality and that more than one
  algebra can be assigned to the same region. Their relative-entropy order
  parameter compares declared additive and enlarged algebras; it does not
  select which algebra is the observer's record carrier.

## Frozen finite controls

### A. Local measurement-scheme freedom

Use one system bit and one probe bit with the same finite interaction arena.
Compare:

1. identity coupling and probe readout, yielding a constant record;
2. controlled-not coupling and probe readout, yielding a computational-basis
   record; and
3. a basis-changing system operation followed by controlled-not, yielding a
   different record contract.

This is not a QFT model. It is a necessary-control specimen showing that
physical admissibility of multiple local couplings cannot select one induced
record channel without an additional law or boundary.

### B. Additive-versus-extended algebra target

On three binary sites define full-support distributions

\[
P_\pm(x)
=
\frac{1\pm\frac12(-1)^{x_1+x_2+x_3}}{8}.
\]

Every proper-subsystem marginal is uniform and identical under \(P_+\) and
\(P_-\), while the global parity target has expectation \(+1/2\) and \(-1/2\).
The full divergence is

\[
D(P_+\Vert P_-)=\frac12\log 3,
\]

whereas every proper-local restriction has divergence zero.

This is a finite classical shadow of a nonlocally generated operator. It may
prove a local-algebra target remainder, but it does not prove QFT net
nonuniqueness or physical observer access to the extended operator.

### C. Conditional-expectation dependence

Construct two distinct \(\mathbb Z_2\) actions on the same finite algebra and
verify that group averaging is idempotent and invariant for each action but
produces different fixed-point quotients. A canonical expectation is
therefore canonical relative to a supplied group action, not a selector of
that action.

## Exact questions

1. Does local QFT causality or realizability select one measurement
   instrument, record channel, or observer boundary?
2. Can two physically admissible measurement schemes in one arena induce
   different record quotients?
3. Can every proper-local record be identical while an extended target
   differs?
4. Is that extended difference a physical remainder for an observer whose
   frozen action algebra excludes the extended measurement?
5. Does granting the extended operator reveal a pre-existing record remainder
   or retype the observer's access/action contract?
6. Does generalized-symmetry algebra nonuniqueness select a record algebra?
7. Does a conditional expectation or relative-entropy order parameter select
   the group action, algebra inclusion, channel, formation, or access?
8. Do these results identify a ready Dynamic Unity successor?

## Strongest absorbers

- Davies--Lewis instruments and Stinespring measurement dilations;
- Sorkin-causality, causal-transparency, and local-QFT realizability results;
- AQFT additivity, Haag duality, maximal nets, and generalized symmetries;

## Cheapest kills

1. Derive one unique instrument or induced record channel from local QFT
   causality and covariance without choosing probe, coupling, state, readout,
   region, or outcome algebra.
2. Show that Fewster--Verch realizability uniquely selects one such scheme.
3. Prove that every target admitted by a fixed extended net factors through
   every proper additive local restriction.
4. Derive the physical generalized-symmetry group, algebra inclusion, and
   conditional expectation without supplying them.
5. Show that relative entropy selects the algebra or channel over which it is
   evaluated.
6. Exhibit a concrete QFT measurement packet in which the same physically
   selected scheme forms, preserves, and exposes a certified record under an
   independently frozen observer action contract.
