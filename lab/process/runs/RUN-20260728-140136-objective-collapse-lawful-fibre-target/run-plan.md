# Objective-collapse lawful-fibre target gate

## Purpose

Execute Swing 3 of the frozen objective-collapse campaign:

> Does the bounded accessible collapse outcome restrict a nonempty lawful
> completion fibre enough to improve prediction of a separately fixed
> downstream response beyond what the collapse law and supplied experiment
> already determine?

This is a conditional foundations audit. It does not assume GRW is true and
does not test GRW against quantum mechanics.

## Frozen antecedents

From `HC-DU-080` and `HC-DU-081`:

- complete theory is either GRWf or GRWm;
- apparatus, ready state, interaction, readout horizon, calibration, and
  bounded access are supplied and unchanged;
- the operational outcome is \(Z\);
- \(Z\) is not treated as a collapse-history or write-provenance certificate;
- the two primitive ontologies share only an operational outcome quotient.

## Escrowed target

The target is the distribution of a later binary apparatus response \(T\)
after a fixed, nontrivial intervention on the post-outcome system.

It is not:

- a copy of \(Z\);
- a collapse/flash detector;
- a provenance predicate;
- part of the first apparatus calibration; or
- allowed to redefine the record after seeing the result.

## Pre-registered decisions

1. Reconstruct the GRW law of superoperators for two consecutive experiments.
2. Compare the unconditional target kernel with the outcome-conditioned
   target kernel.
3. Use an exact two-state positive control with a non-copying noisy response.
4. Keep distributional prediction, realized-history determination, and
   universal sufficiency distinct.
5. Search for the smallest same-outcome/different-future witness under an
   enlarged but explicitly charged preparation or access contract.
6. Stop the campaign if standard GRW instruments plus ordinary conditioning
   already supply the entire positive result.

## Formal packet

Let the first fixed experiment have completely positive outcome maps
\(\{\mathcal C_z\}\), let \(\mathcal S\) be the fixed evolution/intervention
between experiments, and let the later experiment have outcome maps
\(\{\mathcal D_t\}\). For fixed input density matrix \(\rho\), test:

\[
p(z,t)
=
\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
\mathcal D_t\mathcal S\mathcal C_z(\rho)
\right],
\]

\[
p(t\mid z)
=
\frac{
\operatorname{tr}[
\mathcal D_t\mathcal S\mathcal C_z(\rho)]
}{
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal C_z(\rho)
}.
\]

The positive control freezes:

\[
\rho=|+\rangle\langle+|,
\qquad
\mathcal C_z(\rho)=P_z\rho P_z,
\qquad
\mathcal S(X)=R_y(\pi/3)XR_y(\pi/3)^\dagger,
\]

followed by a \(Z\)-basis response measurement.

- The GRW formalism already fixes the complete joint and conditional target
  kernels once the experiment is supplied.

  values.

  \(Z\) leave operationally distinct conditional states.

- Grade 4: exact necessity/sufficiency boundary or counterexample.
- Grade 5: only a physical remainder or prediction not already present in GRW
  or generic instrument theory.
