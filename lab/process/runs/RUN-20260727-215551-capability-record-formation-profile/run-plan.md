# Capability-record closure to physical formation profile

## Question

Does the capability--record lattice from `HC-DU-064` determine which of its
closed record requirements can be physically formed under one resource
contract, or must physical formation be represented by an additional
resource-sensitive structure?

## Scope and controls

- **Owning lanes:** Lane 3 and Lane 4, with Lanes 1, 5, and 7 supporting.
- **Channels:** formal proof, synthesis, and literature collision.
- **Maximum return:** scoped necessity plus conditional physical-realization
  theorem for one frozen process family.
- **Strongest absorber:** the Admissible Record Envelope, quantum-channel
  discrimination, dense coding, Bell-state discrimination, and resource
  theory.
- **Cheapest kill:** an already banked DU result with the same closure-to-cost
  map, or a one-use unassisted strategy that perfectly identifies all four
  Pauli channels.
- **Local-computation disposition:** no model. Dimension, orthogonality, and
  kernel proofs decide the question exactly.
- **Hardware disposition:** unavailable, unnecessary, and irrelevant.

The semantic closure lattice, physical interface, joint-realizability
complex, and resource receipt remain different objects. A supplied formation
protocol cannot be promoted to endogenous interface selection.

## Required outputs

1. a formation-profile definition over closed record relations;
2. a proof that semantic joins do not imply joint physical formation;
3. an exact one-use unassisted lower bound for the four Pauli channels;
4. exact two-use product and one-use entanglement-assisted realizations;
5. ancilla, entanglement, measurement, and archive lower bounds;
6. a classical control proving that the same closure lattice can carry a
   different formation profile;
7. novelty collision and explicit non-promotions; and
8. live-state transition, validation, commit, push, and session completion.

## Stop

Stop when the closure--formation separation and resource frontier are exact.
Do not build a hardware circuit, infer a universal resource law, or claim that
standard Pauli discrimination selects its own apparatus.
