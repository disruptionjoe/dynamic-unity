## The theorem package to prove or kill

1. **Mean-signal absorption.** Mean clock phase or frequency can be
   reproduced by a fixed semiclassical average proper time and does not
   certify nonclassical history.
2. **Reduced-dephasing no-go.** Every two-level single-time coherence factor
   \(\Gamma\) with \(|\Gamma|\le1\), including visibility loss generated by a
   quantum motional label, is a classical random-proper-time mixture.
3. **History-relative channel certificate.** If a conditioned single-Kraus
   operation obeys \(K_m^\dagger K_m=p_mI\) and
   \(K_m/\sqrt{p_m}\not\propto V_h\) for every frozen \(h\in\mathcal H\), its
   Choi operator lies outside the classical specified-history cone.
4. **Minimum Ramsey witness.** Reproduce the exact two-history bright- and
   dark-port predictions and every declared null: identical histories,
   removed intermediate control, unrelated erasure label, and incoherent
   averaging.
5. **Two-axis evidence theorem.** Nonclassical-history certification and
   physical-source attribution are independent. Leaving
   \(\mathsf{CPTH}(\mathcal H)\) does not identify the generator of the same
   conditioned channel.
6. **Residual source null.** If proper-time and engineered coherent
   contributions enter only through
   \(\theta=\theta_\tau+\theta_\chi\), every output observable of the frozen
   process has local sensitivity proportional to \((1,1)\). The exact
   target-changing null is \((1,-1)\).
7. **Minimum repair.** No additional measurement on an identical output
   channel can close mechanism attribution. A second, independently
   calibrated intervention sensitivity not parallel to \((1,1)\) can close
   the local parameter null, provided controls and nuisance models are frozen
   before the result and transfer without refitting.
8. **Interpretation ceiling.** Even full parameter attribution to the
   relativistic clock-motion Hamiltonian cannot experimentally distinguish
   mathematically equivalent “proper-time evolution” and “mass-energy
   coupling” interpretations of that same Hamiltonian.
