# Operational completion-closure trichotomy

## Question

For an explicitly declared class of completed histories, exactly when can a
finite certified record prove productive escape, and when can a realized
history remain outside the completion class while every finite record is
still compatible with some member of it?

The swing must also determine whether a fixed resource bound retains finite
empirical content while the union over every finite bound becomes
finite-prefix indistinguishable.

## Frozen objects

Let:

- \(\Sigma\) be a finite outcome alphabet with at least two symbols;
- \(X=\Sigma^\omega\) be the complete-history space;
- \(u\preceq x\) mean that finite word \(u\) is a prefix of history \(x\);
- \([u]=\{x\in X:u\preceq x\}\) be the operational cylinder generated by the
  finite record \(u\);
- \(C\subseteq X\) be a declared completion class; and
- \(\operatorname{Pref}(C)\) be its finite-prefix language.

The passive record contract observes one finite cylinder at a time. The
adaptive extension replaces coordinates by a finitely branching
intervention-response tree and uses cylinders generated by finitely many
intervention transcripts.

## Pre-registered theorem targets

### T1 — Completion-closure trichotomy

For every \(x\in X\), exactly one holds:

```text
ABSORBED:                 x in C
LIMIT-UNDERDETERMINED:    x in closure(C) \ C
FINITE-PRODUCTIVE-ESCAPE: x not in closure(C)
```

### T2 — Finite-certificate criterion

A finite prefix of \(x\) excludes every completion in \(C\) exactly when
\(x\notin\overline C\). Equivalently, finite escape is possible exactly when
some observed prefix is absent from \(\operatorname{Pref}(C)\).

### T3 — Bounded/unbounded squeeze

Construct a nested bounded generator family \(C_k\) such that every \(C_k\)
is closed and has finite bad-prefix witnesses, while
\(\bigcup_k C_k\) is dense and therefore admits none uniformly. The intended
minimal witness is bounded eventually-periodic binary histories.

### T4 — Fixed-oracle and computability corollary

Computable histories are dense in \(X\). Therefore no finite record can prove
that its realized infinite continuation is noncomputable, even though
noncomputable histories exist and lie outside that class.

### T5 — Intervention-tree extension

The same closure criterion holds after finite adaptive interventions when a
completion is a full response tree. A completed branch-family oracle is not
defeated merely by observing finitely many branches.

## Required distinctions

Keep separate:

```text
exact finite exclusion
    != statistical evidence
    != identification in the limit
    != global class nonmembership
    != physical source issuance
```

A dense support can still carry statistically distinguishable probability
measures. This theorem addresses zero-error finite certification from a bare
completion class, not likelihood or asymptotic testing.
