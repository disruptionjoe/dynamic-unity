# Regional-Finality Excess Audit: Rate, Mergeability, and Criticality

## Result in plain English

The regional-consensus picture remains useful as an **architecture for asking
better questions**, but its three most tempting physics claims do not survive
exact typing:

1. A global tick and distributed-consensus theorems do not produce a new
   physical rate of finality. The same causal speed and tick can support
   arbitrarily different record-formation and decision times.
2. Quantum complementarity is not the same thing as failure to merge records.
   Source observables, measurement instruments, disturbance, joint physical
   formation, and already-classical archive merge are different objects.
3. A sharp-looking finite finality threshold is not yet a phase transition,
   and a critical exponent is undefined until physics selects the order
   parameter and scaling family.

One important correction did survive the hostile audit. The prepared
“finality-rate trilemma” was incomplete. A measurable rate could be:

```text
ordinary dynamics or protocol
observable preferred-frame leakage
operationally inaccessible hidden structure
genuinely new Lorentz-covariant dynamics
```

The fourth branch matters. Objective-collapse research shows that a proposed
observer-accessible rate need not be a preferred-frame leak. But Dynamic Unity
has not derived that branch or its coefficient. Its existing conditional
`PRED-DU-001/002` seams already hold the corresponding beyond-unitary
possibility, so no new prediction was minted or promoted here.

## 2. Candidate 1 — a finality-rate bound distinct from causal reach

### 2.1 Native theorem collision

FLP proves a qualitative possibility of nontermination for deterministic
consensus in a fully asynchronous message-passing model with one faulty
process. It does not derive a physical rate. See
[Fischer, Lynch, and Paterson (1985)](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/tds/papers/Lynch/jacm85.pdf).

Partial-synchrony results explicitly add bounds on message delay or process
speed, with the bounds or stabilization time known or unknown according to
the model. A hidden global clock does not give participants those bounds. See
[Dwork, Lynch, and Stockmeyer (1988)](https://research.ibm.com/publications/consensus-in-the-presence-of-partial-synchrony).

Randomized asynchronous agreement changes termination properties by adding a
randomized protocol and its probability law. It does not turn FLP into a
universal physical clock. See
[Ben-Or (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1145/800221.806707).

The adapter therefore has to preserve at least:

```text
task
participants
message semantics
synchrony knowledge
failure model
adversary
randomness
decision rule
error tolerance
resource horizon
```

A preferred foliation supplies none of those fields by itself.

### 2.2 Exact same-tick counterexample

Let a formed local record occur independently on each global update with
probability \(p\). At tolerated failure probability
\(\epsilon=1/8\), the first safe decision time is:

\[
n(p,\epsilon)
=
\min\{n:(1-p)^n\leq\epsilon\}.
\]

With the same global tick and causal speed:

\[
n(1/2,1/8)=3,
\qquad
n(3/4,1/8)=2.
\]

The first process has residual failure \(1/8\); the second already has
\(1/16\). The difference is an ordinary local formation parameter, not a
second universal speed.

After formation, a protocol may also wait any declared number \(w\) of ticks
before calling the record final:

\[
T_{\mathrm{decision}}=n(p,\epsilon)+w.
\]

Changing \(w\) changes finality latency without changing propagation,
Hamiltonian/open-system dynamics, or the hidden foliation. Conversely,
changing the local coupling changes \(n\) while the global tick stays fixed.

### 2.3 Corrected four-way classification

The scaffold's `PF-C4` trilemma said that a distinct finality rate must be:

1. ordinary dynamics/protocol;
2. an observable preferred-frame leak; or
3. operationally unidentifiable.

That disjunction is not exhaustive. The missing class is:

4. a genuinely new observer-accessible **Lorentz-covariant** dynamics.

Relativistic objective-collapse models are the decisive logical control:
they attempt new stochastic event/reduction dynamics without making
observer-readable preferred simultaneity the signature. Examples include
[Tumulka's relativistic GRW-flash construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00482).
The difficulty of making a simple mass-coupled CSL law both Lorentz covariant
and physically acceptable is itself documented by
[Jones, Gasbarri, and Bassi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02627).

This correction does **not** validate a DU rate. It says only that:

```text
measurable distinct rate
    does not imply
preferred-foliation leak.
```

A covariant rate would require a new generator, selected coefficient,
conservation/no-signalling controls, and a held-out experimental consequence.
Those are exactly the kinds of blockers already attached to
`PRED-DU-001/002`.

## 3. Candidate 2 — complementarity from physical-record mergeability

### 3.1 The five type walls

The initial formulation compressed at least five different questions:

1. Are two source POVMs jointly measurable?
2. Are two measurement instruments jointly implementable?
3. Is one instrument nondisturbing for the other?
4. Can one physical interaction form both records under the frozen resource
   contract?
5. Can two already formed classical records be merged downstream?

These do not share one general yes/no predicate. The distinctions among POVM
joint measurability, instrument compatibility, and nondisturbance are standard;
see [Heinosaari and Wolf](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.5659) and
[Leppäjärvi and Sedlák](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11225).

### 3.2 Sharp case — known theorem

For projection-valued measures \(P=\{P_i\}\) and \(Q=\{Q_j\}\), joint
measurability is equivalent to commutation.

If \(G_{ij}\) is a joint POVM, then:

\[
0\leq G_{ij}\leq P_i
\quad\text{and}\quad
0\leq G_{ij}\leq Q_j.
\]

Sharpness gives:

\[
P_iG_{ij}=G_{ij}=Q_jG_{ij}.
\]

Summing the appropriate marginal yields:

\[
P_iQ_j=G_{ij}=Q_jP_i.
\]

Conversely, if all \(P_i,Q_j\) commute, then
\(G_{ij}=P_iQ_j\) is a joint POVM.

This narrowed statement is exact, but it is a
`KNOWN_THEOREM_SPECIALIZATION`, not a DU derivation of complementarity.

### 3.3 Broad case — exact unsharp counterexample

Let:

\[
E^x_a=\frac{I+a\eta X}{2},
\qquad
E^z_b=\frac{I+b\eta Z}{2},
\qquad
a,b\in\{-1,+1\},
\]

with \(\eta=1/2\). The effects \(E^x_+\) and \(E^z_+\) do not commute.

Nevertheless define:

\[
G_{ab}
=
\frac14(I+a\eta X+b\eta Z).
\]

The marginals are exact:

\[
\sum_bG_{ab}=E^x_a,
\qquad
\sum_aG_{ab}=E^z_b.
\]

For every \(a,b\):

\[
\operatorname{tr}G_{ab}=\frac12,
\qquad
\det G_{ab}=\frac1{32}>0.
\]

So every joint effect is positive and the two noncommuting unsharp
observables are jointly measurable. This is the minimum exact counterexample
to “noncommuting means nonmergeable.” The broader phenomenon is standard;
see [Busch and Heinosaari](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1415).

### 3.4 Archive merge is downstream

Suppose two separate contexts have already produced provenance-tagged
classical records:

```text
{context:X, outcome:+, provenance:lab-a}
{context:Z, outcome:-, provenance:lab-b}
```

Their set union is associative, commutative, and idempotent. A CRDT can merge
them perfectly while preserving the fact that they came from incompatible
source contexts. This merge does not retroactively make the source
measurements jointly implementable.

Conversely, source effects may be compatible while selected instruments
disturb one another or exceed a frozen joint resource budget.

### 3.5 Verdict

| proposition | return | reason |
|---|---|---|
| noncommutation exactly equals record nonmergeability | `EXACT_COUNTEREXAMPLE` | Noncommuting unsharp POVMs can be jointly measurable. |
| sharp PVM joint measurability iff commutation | `KNOWN_THEOREM_SPECIALIZATION` | Exact but standard. |
| already formed classical records cannot merge when source contexts conflict | `EXACT_COUNTEREXAMPLE` | Provenance-tagged set union merges them without erasing context. |
| one physical source instrument is selected by merge algebra | `RELABEL_ONLY` | A supplied archive operation does not select the source interface. |

**Retreat cost.** Replace “record merge derives complementarity” with a typed
ledger of source compatibility, instrument compatibility, nondisturbance,
joint formation, provenance, and downstream archive merge.

## 4. Candidate 3 — universal critical exponents for finality

### 4.1 The order-parameter problem

Let a proposed finality score near a threshold satisfy:

\[
F(s)\sim s^\beta.
\]

If the physical contract has not independently selected the score, the
monotone reparameterization:

\[
G=F^p
\]

preserves every finite ordering and threshold crossing but gives:

\[
G(s)\sim s^{p\beta}.
\]

For example, between \(s=1/4\) and \(s=1/2\):

\[
\frac{F(1/4)}{F(1/2)}=\frac12,
\qquad
\frac{G(1/4)}{G(1/2)}=\frac14
\]

when \(F(s)=s\) and \(G=F^2\).

In standard critical-phenomena work, arbitrary reparameterizations with
vanishing derivative at the critical point are not treated as equivalent
order-parameter coordinates. That is precisely the point: DU has not yet
selected the physically normalized coordinate whose admissible
reparameterizations are restricted. Until it does, the exponent can be
authored by the analyst.

### 4.2 A finite knee is not a critical point

The finite analytic family:

\[
F_N(x)=\frac{x^N}{x^N+(1-x)^N}
\]

becomes visually sharp as \(N\) grows. At
\(x=1/4,1/2,3/4\):

\[
F_2=(1/10,1/2,9/10),
\]

while:

\[
F_8=(1/6562,1/2,6561/6562).
\]

For every finite \(N\), the denominator is positive on \([0,1]\) and the
family is analytic. Sharp appearance alone establishes neither a singular
limit nor an exponent.

### 4.3 Standard universality is already plural

Once a physical order parameter, system-size family, dimension, symmetry,
conservation laws, interaction range, bath, and generator are fixed, standard
statistical/open-system physics already supplies the relevant universality
question.

Examples:

- monitored quantum circuits can have measurement-induced transitions; a
  percolation toy limit and the full quantum transition need not share
  exponents
  ([Skinner, Ruhman, and Nahum](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05953));
- open and closed light-matter critical models can have different exponents
  while open models share a declared dissipative universality class
  ([Hwang, Rabl, and Plenio](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08175)); and
- symmetry class can change monitored-circuit critical behavior
  ([Klocke et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02171)).

Thresholds in codes and noisy systems likewise depend on a frozen code/noise
family rather than “finality” in the abstract; see
[Chubb and Flammia](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10704).

### 4.4 Verdict

| proposition | return | reason |
|---|---|---|
| every sharp finite finality knee is a phase transition | `EXACT_COUNTEREXAMPLE` | Finite analytic families can be arbitrarily sharp. |
| a finality score carries a universal exponent before physical normalization | `EXACT_COUNTEREXAMPLE` | Monotone score powers change the apparent exponent. |
| one exponent spans quantum records, QEC, percolation, and consensus | `RELABEL_ONLY` | The “same” order parameter and scaling family have not been defined across arenas. |
| a frozen physical family has a critical exponent | `KNOWN_THEOREM_SPECIALIZATION` or ordinary open problem | Standard universality machinery applies first. |

**Retreat cost.** Keep only platform-specific protected phases and finite
operational thresholds under `CONCEPT-DU-009`. Require a selected order
parameter, growing family, finite-size scaling, standard-class collision, and
no-refit cross-platform transfer before reviving a universal claim.
