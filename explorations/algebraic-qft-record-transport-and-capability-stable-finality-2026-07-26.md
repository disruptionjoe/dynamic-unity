# Algebraic-QFT record transport and capability-stable finality

## Result in plain English

The five-swing center/finality picture survives the move beyond finite
matrices, but not in the optimistic way.

The exact center theorem extends to any von Neumann action algebra:

> A finite sharp record can be internal to an action algebra and leave every
> admitted action undisturbed exactly when its record projections are central.

For a factor, the center contains only scalar multiples of the identity.
Therefore:

> A factor has no nontrivial finite sharp record that is both internal and
> nondisturbing for its full action algebra.

This matters because local observable algebras in well-behaved relativistic
AQFT models are commonly type-III factors. The type-III structure does not
provide a richer internal public-record center. It makes the exact internal
center trivial.

That does not refute physical records. It says where they must come from.
A nontrivial QFT record must use at least one additional structure:

1. a physically justified restriction to an effective action algebra;
2. an enlarged source--probe--pointer--environment interface;
3. a superselection, global, boundary, or asymptotic algebra;
4. a split/type-I interpolation under additional phase-space assumptions; or
5. an approximate, scale- and horizon-indexed record.

None of those structures is selected merely by saying “the local algebra is
type III.”

The approximate-finality result is equally important. A record that leaks
only a little under each present action need not remain approximately final
under repeated or newly acquired actions. Exact screening composes
indefinitely. Approximate screening earns only a declared capability envelope
and future horizon unless leakage is summable, contractive, or reset.

The smallest exact control starts with one coherent continuation whose public
leakage is only

\[
\frac{20}{101}\approx0.198.
\]

After eight repetitions, the same hidden distinction changes the later public
outcome by

\[
\frac{10825473963759840}{10828567056280801}
\approx0.999714.
\]

So “small one-step leakage” is not “durable finality.”

The resulting program correction is:

> Dynamic Unity should seek a physically formed record interface between
> algebras, not a nontrivial center hiding inside the full local QFT factor;
> and it should state finality relative to an admissible capability-growth
> envelope, scale, error, and horizon.

## Frozen objects

Let \(\mathcal A\subseteq B(\mathcal H)\) be a unital von Neumann algebra
representing the independently frozen admitted action algebra.

Let

\[
\{P_q\}_{q=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal A
\]

be a finite sharp PVM and let

\[
\Lambda^*(X)=\sum_qP_qXP_q
\]

be its nonselective Lüders adjoint.

“Internal” means \(P_q\in\mathcal A\). A pointer projection in a commutant,
probe, environment, boundary algebra, or enlarged dilation is a separately
typed interface.

For capability growth, freeze an increasing family of admitted action sets

\[
\mathcal S_0\subseteq\mathcal S_1\subseteq\cdots
\]

and do not add a new coherent route after declaring finality without recording
the action-contract change.

## Theorem 1 — von Neumann action-center transport

For any finite internal PVM,

\[
\Lambda^*|_{\mathcal A}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal A}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P_q\in Z(\mathcal A)\ \text{for every }q.
\]

### Proof

If \(\Lambda^*(X)=X\) for every \(X\in\mathcal A\), then for \(q\neq r\),

\[
P_qXP_r
=
P_q\Lambda^*(X)P_r
=0.
\]

Thus every \(X\in\mathcal A\) is block diagonal in the PVM and commutes with
every \(P_q\). Since \(P_q\in\mathcal A\), it lies in \(Z(\mathcal A)\).

Conversely, if all \(P_q\) are central, then

\[
\Lambda^*(X)
=
\sum_qXP_q
=X.
\]

No trace, density matrix, minimal projection, or finite-dimensional
decomposition is used. \(\square\)

### What transports and what does not

The center characterization transports. The finite phrase “the unique
finest PVM of minimal central projections” does not automatically transport.
An infinite-dimensional center can be diffuse and need not possess minimal
projections.

## Corollary 1 — factor-internal record no-go

If \(\mathcal A\) is a factor, then

\[
Z(\mathcal A)=\mathbb CI.
\]

Every projection in the center is therefore \(0\) or \(I\). A finite internal
PVM satisfying exact full-action nondisturbance has only one nonzero outcome.
It carries no nontrivial record.

The result is independent of whether the factor is type I, II, or III.
Type III adds important physical and structural distinctions, but it does not
create a nontrivial center.

## Corollary 2 — no nontrivial local central-record subnet in a factor net

Let a local AQFT net assign a factor \(\mathcal A(O)\) to every bounded
region \(O\). If a proposed internal record assignment obeys

\[
\mathcal R(O)\subseteq Z(\mathcal A(O)),
\]

then

\[
\mathcal R(O)=\mathbb CI
\]

for each such region.

So a nontrivial local record net cannot be constructed solely as a subnet of
the exact local centers.

This is not a claim that AQFT has no records. It is a routing theorem: records
must be probe-induced, interface-relative, sectoral/global/asymptotic, or
approximately classical rather than exact nontrivial elements of each full
local factor center.

It supplies arena structure, not Dynamic Unity's missing record engine.

## Theorem 2 — finality is anti-monotone under capability growth

For a fixed binary record projection \(P\), let \(Q=I-P\) and define

\[
\Lambda_P^*(X)=PXP+QXQ.
\]

For an admitted action set \(\mathcal S\), define

\[
d_P(\mathcal S)
=
\sup_{\substack{X\in\mathcal S\\\|X\|\leq1}}
\|X-\Lambda_P^*(X)\|.
\]

If

\[
\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal T,
\]

then

\[
d_P(\mathcal S)\leq d_P(\mathcal T).
\]

Thus a fixed record that is exact or approximate for a smaller capability
class need not remain so after adding a new action.

The exact \(2\times2\) control is decisive:

- \(\mathcal S_0=\operatorname{span}\{I,Z\}\);
- \(P=(I+Z)/2\);
- \(d_P(\mathcal S_0)=0\);
- adding \(X\) generates the full matrix factor proxy;
- \(\Lambda_P^*(X)=0\); and
- \(d_P(\mathcal S_1)=1\).

The states

\[
\rho_\pm=\frac{I\pm X}{2}
\]

have identical \(P\)-record probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\), while the newly
admitted effect \((I+X)/2\) returns probabilities \(1\) and \(0\).

Capability-stable finality must therefore be defined relative to a declared
envelope of admissible present and future generators. Adding an action
outside that envelope is contract retyping; omitting an already available
action manufactures finality.

## Theorem 3 — binary approximate disturbance is a commutator bound

Let

\[
U=2P-I=P-Q.
\]

Then for every \(X\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
X-\Lambda_P^*(X)
&=PXQ+QXP,\\
[P,X]
&=PXQ-QXP\\
&=U\bigl(X-\Lambda_P^*(X)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(U\) is unitary,

\[
\boxed{
\|[P,X]\|
=
\|X-\Lambda_P^*(X)\|
}.
\]

For a word \(W=X_1\cdots X_k\) of unit-norm action generators,

\[
[P,W]
=
\sum_{j=1}^{k}
X_1\cdots X_{j-1}[P,X_j]X_{j+1}\cdots X_k,
\]

so

\[
\|[P,W]\|
\leq
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|[P,X_j]\|.
\]

If each generator has defect at most \(\varepsilon\),

\[
\|[P,W]\|\leq k\varepsilon.
\]

Exact zero is composition-stable. A nonzero per-generator tolerance earns a
finite-horizon bound, not indefinite finality.

## Theorem 4 — approximate regional-screening defects add

Let \(\mathcal H_i\) be Hilbert operator spaces, let \(E_i\) be the orthogonal
projection onto the declared public subspace at layer \(i\), and let

\[
T_i:\mathcal H_i\to\mathcal H_{i-1}
\]

be contraction pullbacks. Define

\[
\delta_i
=
\|(I-E_{i-1})T_iE_i\|.
\]

For two layers,

\[
\begin{aligned}
(I-E_0)T_1T_2E_2
={}&
(I-E_0)T_1E_1T_2E_2\\
&+(I-E_0)T_1(I-E_1)T_2E_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Contractivity gives

\[
\delta(T_1T_2)\leq\delta_1+\delta_2.
\]

Induction yields

\[
\boxed{
\delta(T_1\cdots T_k)
\leq
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_i
}.
\]

This is a normed finite-horizon assurance result. A physical use must declare
the operator space, norm, route normalization, selective strata, scale, and
horizon. It does not license an aggregate-only replacement for
`HC-DU-035D`.

## Exact coherent-accumulation control

Consider a public \(Z\) record and a coherent continuation whose Heisenberg
action rotates

\[
Z\longmapsto
\cos\theta\,Z+\sin\theta\,X
\]

with

\[
\cos\theta=\frac{99}{101},
\qquad
\sin\theta=\frac{20}{101}.
\]

The distance of the pulled-back public effect from the diagonal public
algebra is \(|\sin\theta|=20/101\). The same quantity is the later public
response gap between \(\rho_+\) and \(\rho_-\).

After eight repetitions,

\[
(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^8
=
\frac{-258800989811999
+10825473963759840\,i}
{10828567056280801}.
\]

The public leakage is therefore

\[
\left|\sin(8\theta)\right|
=
\frac{10825473963759840}{10828567056280801}
>0.999.
\]

The local defect was not a hidden stable finality margin. Coherent
composition made the hidden distinction almost perfectly public.

This does not say every nonzero leak grows. It proves that no universal
infinite-horizon conclusion follows from one small local number alone.

## Symmetry and orbit selection

The assumption-breaker does not overturn `HC-DU-033C`.

A symmetric law may select an invariant interface orbit while selecting no
point in that orbit. In the exact three-axis control:

- the full permutation group has no fixed axis;
- the orbit of any axis is the full three-axis family; and
- an oriented reference selecting axis \(0\) reduces the stabilizer, after
  which axis \(0\) is the unique fixed point.

The correct physical target can therefore be:

```text
covariantly selected interface orbit
    + physically supplied or dynamically formed reference section
    -> observer-indexed record instrument.
```

Predictions must either descend to the orbit quotient or receipt the physical
reference resource. Requiring a unique frame-independent point can be an
overconstraint; treating the orbit as an already realized observer is an
overclaim.
