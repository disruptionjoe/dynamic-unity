# Operational duality and ontological no-priority

## Executive result

Dynamic Unity's central question cannot always be forced into:

```text
records create physical reality
    versus
records merely describe an independently prior physical reality.
```

There is a third exact outcome.

If a physical operational presentation and a certified-record presentation
can simulate one another by target-independent maps for a frozen action
envelope, they support exactly the same downstream decision laws and risks.
No experiment confined to that envelope can identify which presentation is
ontologically prior.

This is **operational duality**, not ontological identity:

```text
mutual operational sufficiency
    -> no empirical priority verdict inside the frozen envelope

new action that splits an old record fibre
    -> duality breaks at that capability boundary

physical selection of one interface or formation path
    -> possible asymmetric explanation, still requiring separate evidence.
```

The same theorem transfers without semantic refitting to:

1. a stabilizer record versus the frozen correction quotient; and
2. an authenticated public certificate versus the frozen public-action
   quotient.

It breaks in the same way:

- a logical action separates same-syndrome quantum histories; and
- a physical-source audit separates same-certificate distributed histories.

A same-alphabet, same-marginal preloaded record is the hostile control. It
fails before duality because it is not source-bound to the held-out target.

The component theorem is Blackwell equivalence and finite quotient
mathematics. The Dynamic Unity advance is the typed cross-platform boundary:
operational equivalence, physical formation, antecedent selection, native
identity, and ontological priority are five different claims.

## 1. Frozen objects

Let:

- \(H\) be a finite physical history/completion class;
- \(P:H\to X\) be a **physical operational presentation**;
- \(R:H\to Q\) be a certified-record presentation;
- \(A\) be a frozen family of actions or decisions; and
- \(B_A:H\to Y_A\) be the complete response vector for those actions.

The word **operational** is load-bearing. \(P\) is not automatically the
microscopic physical state. It is the physical history quotient visible to
the declared action envelope:

\[
h\sim_A h'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B_A(h)=B_A(h').
\]

If the full native state is substituted for this quotient, a strict record
coarse-graining will usually be less informative and operational duality will
correctly fail.

## 2. Deterministic duality theorem

### Theorem 1 — equal-kernel duality

Suppose

\[
\ker P=\ker R=\ker B_A.
\]

Then:

1. there is a unique bijection
   \[
   \iota:\operatorname{im}P\longrightarrow\operatorname{im}R
   \]
   satisfying \(R=\iota\circ P\);
2. \(\iota^{-1}\) satisfies \(P=\iota^{-1}\circ R\); and
3. for every target \(T:H\to Z\),
   \[
   T \text{ factors through }P
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   T \text{ factors through }R.
   \]

#### Proof

Define \(\iota(P(h))=R(h)\). Equality of kernels makes this definition
well-defined and injective; every record value in \(\operatorname{im}R\)
comes from a history, so it is surjective. Its inverse is defined
symmetrically.

If \(T=f\circ P\), then

\[
T=f\circ\iota^{-1}\circ R.
\]

The reverse direction is identical. \(\square\)

### Meaning

The two presentations can use different labels, data structures, or apparent
objects. Inside the frozen action contract they are the same quotient of
physical histories.

This proves neither:

- that the native physical state is a record;
- that a record is the native physical state;
- that the record was physically formed or selected;
- that the action envelope is exhaustive; nor
- that one presentation causes the other.

## 3. Stochastic no-priority theorem

Let \(\mathsf P(x\mid h)\) and \(\mathsf R(q\mid h)\) be finite statistical
experiments over the same history class.

### Definition — mutual operational sufficiency

They are mutually sufficient when target-independent Markov kernels \(K\)
and \(L\) satisfy

\[
\mathsf R=\mathsf P K,
\qquad
\mathsf P=\mathsf R L.
\]

### Theorem 2 — frozen-contract no-priority

Mutually sufficient experiments have identical attainable response laws and
risk sets for every downstream finite randomized decision rule and bounded
loss in the frozen contract.

#### Proof

Let \(D_P\) be any decision kernel following \(\mathsf P\). The record-side
rule \(L D_P\) gives

\[
\mathsf R L D_P=\mathsf P D_P.
\]

Thus every physical-presentation response law is attainable from the record
presentation. Conversely, every record-side decision \(D_R\) is reproduced
from the physical presentation by \(K D_R\):

\[
\mathsf P K D_R=\mathsf R D_R.
\]

The induced response distributions are equal history by history, so every
bounded-loss risk is equal. \(\square\)

### Corollary — no internal ontological adjudication

No decision experiment whose interventions, observations, losses, and
resources remain inside the frozen contract can determine whether
\(\mathsf P\) or \(\mathsf R\) is ontologically prior.

This is an identifiability result, not a metaphysical proof that priority
does not exist. A wider physical action, an independently selected formation
mechanism, or a new law may break the equivalence.

## 4. First-leak theorem

Let \(A\subseteq A'\), with enlarged response vector \(B_{A'}\).

### Theorem 3 — capability-relative break

If \(R\) is operationally dual to \(B_A\), then it remains sufficient for the
enlarged envelope exactly when

\[
\ker R\subseteq\ker B_{A'}.
\]

Equivalently, duality first fails when there exist \(h_0,h_1\in H\) such
that

\[
R(h_0)=R(h_1),
\qquad
B_{A'}(h_0)\ne B_{A'}(h_1).
\]

#### Proof

This is the finite factorization criterion. A decoder
\(\widehat B:Q\to Y_{A'}\) exists exactly when \(B_{A'}\) is constant on
every record fibre. \(\square\)

This gives an exact experimental target. Do not ask vaguely whether two
descriptions are “really the same.” Add one independently motivated action
and look for a same-record/different-response pair.

## 5. Unchanged quantum transfer

Use the three-qubit repetition code with \(Z_1Z_2\) and \(Z_2Z_3\) checks.
Within the Pauli-\(X\) sector, write a history as

\[
h=(e,\ell),
\]

where \(e\in\{I,X_1,X_2,X_3\}\) is the declared correctable representative
and \(\ell\in\{0,1\}\) records multiplication by logical
\(\overline X=X_1X_2X_3\).

Multiplication by \(\overline X\) preserves both check syndromes. Therefore:

- physical operational presentation for the frozen correction action:
  correction sector;
- record presentation: two-bit syndrome;
- complete frozen response: correction label.

All three induce the same four-block history partition. The equal-kernel
theorem applies.

The minimum enlarged-action witness is:

\[
I
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\overline X.
\]

They have the same syndrome \((0,0)\) and require the same frozen syndrome
processing, but a logical-sensitive action separates them. Operational
duality was correct for the recovery envelope and false for the enlarged
logical envelope.

The code, checks, decoder, and allowed Pauli sector remain supplied. This is
not endogenous physical interface selection.

## 6. Unchanged distributed transfer

Let histories carry:

\[
h=(v,f),
\]

where \(v\in\{0,1\}\) is a public execution value and
\(f\in\{\text{source-formed},\text{preloaded}\}\) is its physical formation
mode.

The terminal certificate is a deterministic authenticated representation of
\(v\). Under the frozen public action envelope:

- the physical operational presentation is the safe read/execute response
  class determined by \(v\);
- the record presentation is the valid certificate; and
- the complete public response is read \(v\) and execute iff \(v=1\).

All three induce the same two-block history partition. The same theorem
applies without changing object roles.

The minimum enlarged-action witness is:

\[
(v,\text{source-formed})
\quad\text{versus}\quad
(v,\text{preloaded}).
\]

The certificate and public action are identical; a physical-source audit
separates the histories.

Authentication preserves the declared value and signer relation. It does not
by itself establish causal physical source binding.

## 7. Hostile same-alphabet control

Let target \(t\) and record value \(r\) be independent uniform bits. Sign
\(r\) with the same certificate function used above.

The signed-record marginal is the same uniform alphabet as a source-bound
case, but

\[
\ker R\not\subseteq\ker t.
\]

No decoder reconstructs the physical target. Shared labels, matched
marginals, valid signatures, or a common theorem schema cannot repair the
missing source relation.

This control prevents the cross-platform result from becoming an analogy
engine.
