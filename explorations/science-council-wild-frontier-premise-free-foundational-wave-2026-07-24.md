## 6. The bounded swing

### 6.1 Exact equivalence theorem

Let `T` and `U` be positive physical post-era coupling sequences with
`T_1,U_1>0`. Let `A(T)` be all standard originary transition probabilities at
every future parent size and allowed nonempty precursor summary.

Then

\[
\mathcal A(T)=\mathcal A(U)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[T]=[U].
\]

**Sufficiency.** If `U=cT` for a positive `c`, every numerator and denominator
is multiplied by `c`, so every probability is unchanged.

**Necessity.** In every originary parent, the post/origin alone is an allowed
singleton precursor. Define

\[
s_n
=
\Pr_T(n;1,1)
=
\frac{T_1}
{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}T_k}.
\]

Normalize `q_k=T_k/T_1`, so `q_1=1`. Then

\[
\frac{1}{s_n}
=
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}q_k.
\]

Starting from `q_1=1`, recover recursively

\[
q_n
=
\frac{1}{s_n}
-n
-\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}q_k.
\]

Thus equality of the complete transition algebras implies equality of all
`s_n`, which implies equality of every projective coupling ratio. The
projective law is a minimal sufficient statistic up to invertible recoding.

The exact rational fixture recovers

```text
[1, 35/33, 14/39, 119/57, 49/69, 203/93, 77/111, 287/129]
```

from singleton probabilities alone and verifies all `120` transition
summaries through parent size `8` are unchanged by rescaling the source vector
by `17/5`.

### 6.3 Arbitrary finite-horizon counterfamily

Fix a finite horizon `r>=1`, a post depth `P>=0`, a positive `a`, and a
positive `d != 1`. Define a strictly positive bare sequence by

\[
t_n=a^n
\quad\text{through }n=r+P+1,
\]

put one defect at

\[
t_{r+P+2}=d\,a^{r+P+2},
\]

and keep all other entries positive; the probe returns them to `a^n`.

For every `n<=r`, neither `M^P(t)_n` nor `M^(P+1)(t)_n` sees the defect:

\[
M^P(t)_n=a^n(1+a)^P,
\]

\[
M^{P+1}(t)_n=a^n(1+a)^{P+1}.
\]

After normalization by component `1`, both laws equal `a^(n-1)` through
component `r`. Since every transition at parent size `n` uses only
`T_1,...,T_n`, all transitions through horizon `r` agree.

At component `r+1`,

\[
\frac{M^P(t)_{r+1}}{M^P(t)_1}=a^r,
\]

while

\[
\frac{M^{P+1}(t)_{r+1}}{M^{P+1}(t)_1}
=
a^r
+
\frac{(d-1)a^{r+P+1}}{(1+a)^{P+1}},
\]

which differs. The singleton-origin transition is available at every parent
size, so it detects the difference at `n=r+1`.

This is not a numerical near-degeneracy. It is exact for arbitrary finite
`r`, uses strictly positive nonnegative-CSG-admissible couplings, and locates
the precise agreeing and diverging horizons.

### 6.4 Replay and source fibre
