# Wide-source preparation and the local-energy spectral boundary

## Executive return

`HC-DU-189` found that the high-ceiling part of the Chen--Giacomini proposal
is the phase generated by broad source energy-density eigenprofiles. This
swing asks the missing prior question: does a physically familiar wide,
stationary quantum source actually possess such a deterministic local-energy
profile?

In the smallest explicit low-energy source, the answer is **not
automatically**. A wide stationary wavefunction is a global-Hamiltonian
eigenstate, not generally a simultaneous eigenstate of every local
energy-density cell.

The exact two-cell result is a trilemma:

1. with hopping/kinetic transport, positive local energy cells do not commute
   and have no common eigenvector;
2. when hopping is removed so that local profiles are exact, the one-particle
   profiles are localized and the induced cross-source phase is exactly the
   lattice Newton potential; and
3. coarse-graining can make total energy deterministic only by erasing the
   spatial profile whose functional shape was supposed to distinguish the
   proposal.

This does not refute the published calculation or prove a QFT no-go. It finds
a more basic missing object than an apparatus design: the paper's
non-Newtonian effect requires an explicit physical source theory with
normalizable, preparable, sufficiently joint-sharp local stress profiles.
Neither a wide wavefunction, a global energy eigenstate, zero expected
momentum current, nor an expectation-value density supplies that object.

The umbrella remains profound but is not locally executable. It should now be
held behind a **spectral-realizability reopener**, before relational readout,
controller completion, magnitude, or hardware.

## 1. Published claim being tested

Chen and Giacomini's final published
[*Quantum Effects in Gravity Beyond the Newton Potential from a Delocalized
Quantum Source*](https://doi.org/10.1103/hl1c-t8z9), *Physical Review X*
**15**, 031063 (2025), takes a basis satisfying

\[
\widehat T_{00}(x)|E\rangle
=E(x)|E\rangle
\]

and obtains the source-dependent phase

\[
\Theta_{AB}
=
-\frac{\kappa t}{4\pi\hbar}
\int d^3x\,d^3y\,
\frac{E_A(x)E_B(y)}{|x-y|}.
\tag{1}
\]

The paper explicitly proposes varying the functional form of \(E(x)\),
possibly by changing source potentials. Its abstract and figures use wide
Gaussian states as the motivating example. It also states that, for its
static sources, the stress-tensor Schwinger term is negligible and hence
local \(T_{00}\) operators commute in the considered cases.

The physical inference therefore depends on a spectral lift:

```text
laboratory potential/control
  -> preparable static quantum state
  -> joint local-energy eigenprofile E(x)
  -> source-conditioned gravitational phase
  -> relationally acquired joint outcome
```

The published result supplies the phase conditional on the middle object. It
does not construct this entire lift for a concrete source.

The 2026 follow-on,
[*Quantum Reference Fields Transformations in Linearized Quantum
Gravity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09344), supplies dynamical reference
fields and a relational von Neumann-type measurement scheme. It also states
that the full \(T^{0\mu}\) family cannot generally be diagonalized on an
equal-time hypersurface because of Schwinger terms, while an approximate
leading-order basis can be used under its restrictions. It does not construct
the 2025 wide-source preparation or transport Eq. (1) into a complete
relational probability.

## 2. The profile-realizability contract

Because a point stress tensor is an operator-valued distribution, a physical
profile must first declare a finite family of smeared cells

\[
T_r=T_{00}(f_r),
\qquad r=1,\ldots,n,
\]

at a stated spatial and temporal resolution.

An **exact deterministic profile** \(e=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)\) on state
\(|\psi\rangle\) means

\[
T_r|\psi\rangle=e_r|\psi\rangle
\qquad\text{for every }r.
\tag{2}
\]

Necessary consequences are

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\psi(T_r)=0,
\qquad
[T_r,T_s]|\psi\rangle=0
\quad\text{for every }r,s.
\tag{3}
\]

A profile of expectation values

\[
\bar e_r=\langle\psi|T_r|\psi\rangle
\]

does not satisfy Eq. (2) when the variances are nonzero. Replacing the stress
operator by its expectation profile \(\bar e\) is a semiclassical
expectation-value closure, not the
profile-eigenvalue construction used in Eq. (1).

An approximate version is legitimate, but it must state the joint resolution,
all profile variances and commutator errors, and propagate them into the
phase distribution and visibility. “Static” or “wide” does not provide those
bounds by itself.

## 3. Exact two-cell theorem

Let the one-particle source Hilbert space be spanned by spatial cells
\(|L\rangle,|R\rangle\), with projectors \(P_L,P_R\). Split one positive
source Hamiltonian into local energy cells

\[
T_L=aP_L+\frac J2X,
\qquad
T_R=bP_R+\frac J2X,
\qquad
a,b>0, J\ge0.
\tag{4}

Their sum is the complete Hamiltonian

\[
H=T_L+T_R=aP_L+bP_R+JX.
\tag{5}

The hopping term is divided symmetrically across the two local cells. Direct
calculation gives

\[
[T_L,T_R]
=
\frac{iJ(a+b)}2Y.
\tag{6}

### Theorem 1: transport obstructs a joint local profile

If \(J>0\), Eq. (6) is invertible. Therefore no nonzero vector is annihilated
by the commutator, and \(T_L,T_R\) have no common eigenvector.

For \(a=b=m\), the spatially wide state

\[
|+\rangle=\frac{|L\rangle+|R\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\]

is an exact global energy eigenstate,

\[
H|+\rangle=(m+J)|+\rangle.
\]

It has zero expected \(Y\)-current, yet

\[
[T_L,T_R]|+\rangle\ne0
\]

and both local-energy variances are nonzero. Thus:

> A wide stationary state and zero mean current do not imply a deterministic
> local energy-density eigenprofile.

The paper's staticity assumption may be valid in a more restricted QFT
subspace, but it must be shown as an operator/profile statement. Vanishing
mean flux is insufficient.

### Theorem 2: the commuting limit restores Newton

When \(J=0\), the exact one-particle joint profiles are

\[
|L\rangle:(m,0),
\qquad
|R\rangle:(0,m).
\]

The wide state has expectation profile \((m/2,m/2)\), but each cell has
variance \(m^2/4\). It is a superposition of localized profiles, not an
extended deterministic profile.

For two such sources on sites \((i,j)\), substituting the exact profile
eigenvalues into Eq. (1) gives

\[
\Theta_{ij}
=
-\frac{Gtm_Am_B}{\hbar r_{ij}},
\]

the diagonal lattice Newton-potential operator on every branch. Linearity then
extends equality to arbitrary superpositions.

Hence a broad wavefunction alone does not produce the advertised
non-Newtonian functional shape in this source class. The extra effect requires
a nonlocalized *eigenprofile*, not merely a nonlocalized state.

### Theorem 3: coarse determinacy loses shape

Coarsening the two cells to total mass gives

\[
T_{\mathrm{tot}}=T_L+T_R=mI
\qquad(J=0,a=b=m),
\]

which is deterministic on every state. It also assigns the same outcome to
\(|L\rangle\) and \(|R\rangle\). The spatial profile is gone.

The finite lesson is not that smearing is illegitimate. It is that every
proposed repair must preserve enough spatial resolution for the held-out
phase shape while making the corresponding stress family jointly sharp.

## 4. Profile uncertainty changes the observable

If unresolved profile branches acquire phases \(\phi_r\), the retained
coherence is the characteristic function

\[
\Gamma=\sum_r p_r e^{i\phi_r},
\tag{7}

\]

not simply

\[
e^{i\sum_r p_r\phi_r}.
\tag{8}

\]

For two equal branches with phases \(0\) and \(\pi/2\), Eq. (7) has visibility
\(1/\sqrt2\), while Eq. (8) has visibility one. A preparation audit must
therefore predict both phase and visibility after the actual profile
distribution, rather than inserting an expectation density into Eq. (1).

This may eventually produce an additional experimental discriminator. At
present it is a standard dephasing consequence, not a new Dynamic Unity law.

## 6. What remains genuinely open

The finite result does not exclude:

- a many-body or field-theoretic source with extended joint stress
  eigenprofiles;
- a special static constraint sector on which the smeared stress family
  commutes;
- approximate joint profiles with sufficiently small operational variance;
- solitonic, condensate, number-squeezed, or other collective sources; or
- a full operator-valued calculation whose phase/visibility still differs
  from Newton without requiring deterministic profiles.

Each route must construct the source rather than name it.
