# Wild-frontier dual primitive-record-clock wave

## Result first

The user's two premises are both scientifically live, but they divide more
sharply than “local clock versus global clock” suggests.

> A single global update scheduler can underlie relativistic-looking observer
> clocks, but its tick count cannot also be the observers' elapsed time.
> Relativistic elapsed time appears only after record admission becomes
> path-dependent (`G-gated`) or the scheduler is removed from the physical
> observable algebra (`G-hidden`). `G-full` and distance-delay alone fail at
> reunion.

The bounded probe establishes four exact finite facts:

1. Two observers spanning the same global ticks receive equal `G-full`
   counts, even when the target proper times differ.
2. In a conditional `1+1` null-count construction,
   `tau_hash=2 sqrt(R L)` gives `100` records for the rest history and `80`
   for an out-and-back history at `|v|=3/5`, exactly the special-relativistic
   ratio `sqrt(1-v^2)=4/5`.
3. The pure null reslicing `(R,L)->(2R,L/2)` preserves `R L` and
   `tau_hash`, but changes the global layer count `K=R+L` from `100` to
   `125`. A readable `K` is therefore preferred-frame structure.
4. Three natural labelings of one finite event graph have one common
   order-only record signature but three different clock-tag signatures.
   Calling the scheduler gauge requires an explicit no-tag quotient; it does
   not follow from ordinary observers merely failing to notice it.

The same probe's echo fixture gives send/receive/return counts `10,17,24`.
The raw offset `7` becomes exactly `0` after subtracting the independently
counted seven-edge latency. Radar distance is `7` and radar `c=1`, but that is
definitional if distance is made from the round trip. “One edge per tick” is
a separate circuit law, and dimensional `c` still needs length and time
calibrations.

[Arrighi, Facchini, and Forets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.4499) are the
decisive positive antecedent, but not a derivation of the user's proposal.
Their Clock QCA proves exact covariance for a specially constructed `1+1`
lightlike circuit under an encoded, generally rescaled discrete Lorentz
transform. The paper itself notes that nontrivial integral `1+1` Lorentz
transforms require global rescaling and can be viewed as biased zooms. Its
Clock QW/QCA uses counters, idle steps, quiescent wires, and encodings—the
very gating/quotient structure missing from `G-full`. It proves that the
construction class is nonempty; it does not show that distance-induced
message delay generates proper-time differences or that a bare global clock
selects the light cone.

The wild-frontier opportunity is consequently a **clocked causal circuit with
an observable-algebra quotient**, not “global tick equals time.” The next
scientific task is to earn the record-admission law and prove that no global
clock tag leaks. No `Lambda`, gravitational dynamics, dimensional signal
speed, or DU physical identity is established.

### Delay-only/radar null

Let A send at `n_s=10`; B receives/reflects at `m_b=17`; A receives at
`n_r=24`. With independently counted graph distance `D=7` and one edge per
update:

```text
radar time     = (10+24)/2 = 17
radar distance = (24-10)/2 = 7
raw offset     = 17-10 = 7
corrected      = 17-10-D = 0.
```

The echo defines radar coordinates and round-trip latency. If radar distance
is the only distance, `c=1` is a unit convention. If graph distance is
independent, the agreement verifies the separately assumed circuit law “one
edge per tick”; it still does not derive dimensional `c`.

Asymmetric one-way latency would make clock synchronization conventional
unless additional reciprocal/isotropy structure is supplied. Round-trip
latency alone does not fix that split.


This kills the strong claim that distance-induced message latency by itself
produces differential accumulated time. Gated/path response is indispensable.
