## 2. Antecedent-guaranteed operational quotient

Interface nonselection does not mean nothing is common across the
antecedent fibre. It means the common object must be computed rather than
silently choosing one implementation.

Fix an antecedent value \(\alpha\) and suppose every implementation

\[

\]

\[

=
\bigvee_{m\in a^{-1}(\alpha)}\ker r_m
\]

\[

\]

be the finest common quotient.

### Theorem — antecedent-fibre dynamic sufficiency

A target \(t:X\to T\) factors through every native record on the antecedent
fibre exactly when

\[
K_\alpha\subseteq\ker t.
\]

Equivalently, \(t\) factors through \(q_\alpha\).

### Proof

If \(t\) factors through every \(r_m\), then
\(\ker r_m\subseteq\ker t\) for every implementation. Since \(\ker t\) is
an equivalence relation, it contains their join \(K_\alpha\).

Conversely, \(q_\alpha\) factors through every \(r_m\). If
\(K_\alpha\subseteq\ker t\), then \(t\) factors through \(q_\alpha\), and
therefore through every native record. \(\square\)

This is the `HC-DU-045` completion-common quotient theorem composed with the
ordinary transfer criterion. Its mathematics is known.

Most importantly, \(q_\alpha\) is an **operational quotient**. It is not
automatically a material record existing at one location. Calling it a
formed record would repeat the selection error.

### Constant-interface corollary

If the antecedent fibre admits one implementation whose accessible record is
constant, then \(K_\alpha=X\times X\). Only a target already constant on the
lawful class can be guaranteed from the antecedent alone.

Thus a supplied record may do real work conditionally while doing no
antecedent-robust work across the unselected interface family.
