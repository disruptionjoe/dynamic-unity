# Action-center record formation and capability boundary

## Result in plain English

The most important new answer is not that “the center is classical.” Dynamic
Unity already knew that. The new answer is exactly what a formed central
record can and cannot do.

Suppose the physics has independently given us an algebra of actions that can
actually matter to the system. Then:

1. the finest sharp record that can be read without disturbing any of those
   actions is the center of that algebra;
2. an explicit pointer and archive can copy that central sector label without
   disturbing the action algebra;
3. that label is an autonomous record state only when every admitted
   event-and-next-record operation also acts centrally; and
4. the central record captures every admitted action exactly only when the
   action algebra is commutative.

If the algebra has a noncommutative block, two states can have exactly the
same formed central record and still respond differently to one permitted
action. That is a finite, exact, capability-relative physical remainder. If
the algebra is a factor, its center is trivial: there is no nontrivial sharp
record that is both internal to the algebra and nondisturbing for all of it.

So the quantum-to-classical seam is not merely “unmeasured versus measured.”
It has an exact algebraic form:

> The center is the maximal nondisturbing classical sector record. Quantum
> structure survives inside its noncommutative blocks, and whether that
> structure is a physical remainder depends on what actions the observer can
> actually perform.

This does not show that records create reality. It gives a clean operational
duality in a frozen finite class:

- for a center-limited observer, the central record is complete;
- for an observer with coherent within-sector actions, it is incomplete; and
- changing the action class can change the sufficiency verdict without
  changing the record.

The mathematics is established operator-algebra terrain. The Dynamic Unity
advance is the exact bridge from physical formation through `HC-DU-036H`
autonomy to observer capability and a finite remainder witness.

## Frozen object

Let \(\mathcal H\) be finite dimensional and let

\[
\mathcal A\subseteq B(\mathcal H)
\]

be a unital finite-dimensional C*-algebra. Interpret \(\mathcal A\) as the
independently frozen algebra generated by the physical actions and effects
that the declared observer/system interface admits.

By the finite-dimensional structure theorem, after a unitary change of
representation,

\[
\mathcal A
\cong
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}
\left(M_{n_j}(\mathbb C)\otimes I_{m_j}\right).
\]

Its center is

\[
Z(\mathcal A)
\cong
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb C z_j,
\]

where the \(z_j\) are the minimal central projections.

The multiplicity spaces \(\mathbb C^{m_j}\) belong to the commutant of the
represented action algebra. The matrix factors \(M_{n_j}\) are the
potentially noncommutative within-sector action structure.

The action algebra is load-bearing and is not derived in this swing.
Changing it changes the observer capability contract.

## Theorem 1 — the finest internal nondisturbing sharp record

Let \(\{P_q\}\subseteq\mathcal A\) be a sharp PVM:

\[
P_qP_{q'}=\delta_{qq'}P_q,
\qquad
\sum_qP_q=I.
\]

Its nonselective Lüders adjoint is

\[
\Lambda^*(A)=\sum_qP_qAP_q.
\]

Then

\[
\Lambda^*|_{\mathcal A}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal A}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P_q\in Z(\mathcal A)\ \text{for every }q.
\]

Consequently, the minimal central projections \(\{z_j\}\) form the unique
finest action-internal sharp PVM that is nondisturbing for all of
\(\mathcal A\), up to relabeling.

### Proof

Suppose \(\Lambda^*(A)=A\) for every \(A\in\mathcal A\). For \(q\neq q'\),

\[
P_qAP_{q'}
=
P_q\Lambda^*(A)P_{q'}
=0.
\]

Thus every \(A\in\mathcal A\) is block diagonal under the PVM and commutes
with every \(P_q\). Because \(P_q\in\mathcal A\), it follows that
\(P_q\in Z(\mathcal A)\).

Conversely, if every \(P_q\) is central, then

\[
\sum_qP_qAP_q
=
\sum_qAP_q
=A.
\]

Every central projection is a sum of minimal central projections. Therefore
every central PVM is a coarse-graining of \(\{z_j\}\), which proves the
finest-record statement. \(\square\)

### What the internality condition prevents

A projector may commute with \(\mathcal A\) while belonging only to the
commutant \(\mathcal A'\), not to \(\mathcal A\). Such a projector can
describe a separate pointer or multiplicity degree of freedom. It is not a
record *inside* the source action algebra.

This distinction matters most for a factor. If

\[
\mathcal A=M_n(\mathbb C)\otimes I_m,
\]

then \(Z(\mathcal A)=\mathbb CI\). There is no nontrivial
action-internal sharp record preserving the whole algebra. A nontrivial
nondisturbing pointer must live in the commutant, arise after an enlarged
system/pointer split, restrict the action algebra, or abandon exact
nondisturbance.

## Theorem 2 — explicit formation is permitted, not selected

Let \(\{|j\rangle_P\}\) and \(\{|j\rangle_R\}\) be blank pointer and archive
registers large enough to carry the central labels. Define

\[
V
=
\sum_j z_j\otimes|j\rangle_P\otimes|j\rangle_R.
\]

Then

\[
V^\dagger V=I
\]

and for every \(A\in\mathcal A\),

\[
(A\otimes I_{PR})V=VA.
\]

Therefore this isometry writes two matching copies of the central label and
intertwines the complete action algebra.

The statement is an existence and compatibility result. It does not prove
that the source law selects:

- this source/pointer/archive boundary;
- this decoder;

- public access; or
- a unique microscopic dilation.

Those remain physical formation and interface-selection burdens. The theorem
only identifies the canonical sharp value that such a nondisturbing internal
formation can carry once \(\mathcal A\) is fixed.

## Theorem 3 — exact autonomy criterion on the center

Let an admitted action \(a\) have selective Heisenberg maps
\(\mathcal J^*_{a,s,y}\), including every acquisition stratum \(s\) and
response \(y\). For next central record \(j'\), define the joint effect

\[
E^a_{s,y,j'}
=
\mathcal J^*_{a,s,y}(z_{j'}).
\]

Assume these effects lie in \(\mathcal A\), and retain every rejected,
invalid, missing, or reset-relevant stratum required by `HC-DU-036H`.

For a state \(\rho\) supported in current sector \(j\),

\[
\Pr(s,y,j'\mid \rho,a)
=
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\rho E^a_{s,y,j'}\right).
\]

The complete joint row depends on \(\rho\) only through the current central
label \(j\) iff

\[
z_jE^a_{s,y,j'}z_j
=
k^a_{s,y,j'|j}z_j
\]

for every \(a,s,y,j',j\). Since the effects lie in \(\mathcal A\), this is
equivalent to

\[
E^a_{s,y,j'}\in Z(\mathcal A)
\]

for every complete joint cell.

### Proof

If the compression is scalar, every normalized state supported in sector
\(j\) assigns that cell the same probability
\(k^a_{s,y,j'|j}\). The common values form a controlled quotient kernel on
the central labels.

Conversely, if every state supported in \(z_j\mathcal H\) gives the same
expectation, the compression of the Hermitian effect to that block must be
scalar. Otherwise two eigenstates with different eigenvalues give a
same-record/different-response witness. Because the \(z_j\) are the minimal
central blocks of \(\mathcal A\), being scalar on every block is exactly
membership in \(Z(\mathcal A)\). \(\square\)

When this condition holds for the closed admitted action family, induction
gives every finite central-record trace law and every central-record-adaptive
policy. This is the operator-algebra specialization of `HC-DU-036H`, not a
new alternative to it.

### Complete selective continuation remains typed

The central quotient determines central event/next-record statistics. It does

Thus “the central record is autonomous” is always relative to the closed

## Theorem 4 — the commutative completeness boundary

The minimal central record determines the expectation of every effect in
\(\mathcal A\), for every physical state, iff \(\mathcal A\) is commutative.

Equivalently:

\[
\text{all admitted action statistics factor through the central record}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal A=Z(\mathcal A).
\]

### Proof

If \(\mathcal A\) is commutative, every effect has the form

\[
E=\sum_j e_jz_j.
\]

Its expectation is determined by the central weights
\(p_j=\operatorname{tr}(\rho z_j)\).

If \(\mathcal A\) is noncommutative, some block has \(n_j>1\). Choose a
non-scalar effect \(E\) in that matrix block and two normalized eigenstates
\(\rho_0,\rho_1\) with different eigenvalues. Both states have the same
central record \(j\), while

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\rho_0E)
\neq
\operatorname{tr}(\rho_1E).
\]

One admitted action therefore exposes the within-sector remainder.
\(\square\)

### Important qualification

Even in the commutative case, two density operators may differ in degrees of
freedom outside the declared algebra while agreeing on every
\(\mathcal A\)-effect. The theorem earns operational sufficiency for the
frozen action class, not identity of microscopic state or ontology.

## What this teaches about the distributed/quantum delta

The result identifies a precise point where a distributed-systems certificate
and a quantum record can look architecturally similar while remaining
physically different.

In an ordinary classical certified state machine, the admitted event algebra
is commutative. If its complete transition row lumps through the certificate,
the certificate can be a complete autonomous state for those actions.

In a quantum action algebra, a hardened public label may likewise be central,
but noncommutative blocks can remain behind that label. Coherent actions
inside a block can expose differences that the public record does not carry.
The central certificate is therefore:

- complete for the central/classical layer;
- incomplete for a richer coherent action layer; and
- complete again only if the action contract is restricted to the center.

This gives the layered-finality program a better object than a loose consensus
analogy:

```text

    = candidate hardened/public classical fact

noncommutative block over that sector
    = retained coherent/local capability state

admitted action algebra
    = the exact boundary deciding whether the retained state is operational
      remainder or inaccessible representation freedom.
```

No physical identity between a BFT protocol and a quantum channel is inferred.
The common structure is the quotient/autonomy test. The quantum-specific
residue is noncommutativity.
