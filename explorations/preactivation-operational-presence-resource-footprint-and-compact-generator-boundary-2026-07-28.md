# A footprint is an intervention response, and type count alone has no universal cost

## Executive result

`HC-DU-088` left two apparently different ways to distinguish a dormant fixed
type from genuine preactivation absence:

1. intervene on the source before activation; or
2. measure a storage, energy, stress, spatial, latency, or other physical
   footprint.

`HC-DU-089` shows that these are one route, not two. A footprint measurement is
itself an admitted preactivation query. Both are governed by the same complete
response signature already formalized in `HC-DU-056`.

The strongest valid positive is:

> If a predeclared physical action or resource measurement has different
> response laws in the dormant-fixed and absent source classes, it certifies
> operational preexistence relative to that action, source boundary, accuracy,
> and resource contract.

The converse fails:

> Equal preactivation response/resource signatures establish operational
> equivalence for the admitted actions. They do not establish physical absence,
> because a hidden state, compact generator, completed history, external
> boundary input, or slower activation route may remain in the same fibre.

There is also no universal positive energy cost “per dormant type.”

- Landauer's cost applies to logically irreversible information loss, not
  passive possession of a label or type.
- Orthogonal memory states can be energy-degenerate.
- A short generator can produce many later types without storing them as
  independently addressable preactivation entries.
- A speed-energy bound becomes informative only after a response deadline is
  frozen.
- A finite-region entropy bound constrains a model that claims many
  independently distinguishable resident states; it does not turn the number
  of possible future outputs into resident entropy.

Thus a physical footprint can kill a particular explicit-reservoir model. It
cannot, by itself, kill every fixed-generator or completed-history disclosure
model. The remaining issuance burden is now singular:

> independently select a source boundary and a complete preactivation
> response/resource class whose fibres coincide with the claimed fixed
> generative closure—or prove a source-completion theorem that makes them
> coincide.

## 2. Complete preactivation signature

Let:

- \(H\) be the frozen physical-history/completion class;
- \(t_a\) be the activation event;
- \(P_\tau:H\to\{0,1\}\) state whether type \(\tau\) lies in the admitted
  fixed source/generator closure before \(t_a\);
- \(\mathcal A^-\) be the independently declared family of interventions
  entirely before \(t_a\);
- \(\mathcal Q^-\) be the declared preactivation measurements of energy,
  stress-energy, charge, support, storage, reliability, latency, entropy, and
  other resource variables;
- \(Y_u\) be the output space for \(u\in\mathcal A^-\cup\mathcal Q^-\); and
- \(\mathsf P(Y_u)\) be its response-law space.

Define the complete preactivation signature:

\[
\Sigma^-(h)
=
\left(
\operatorname{Law}(Y_u\mid h,u)
\right)_{u\in\mathcal A^-\cup\mathcal Q^-}.
\]

This signature includes:

- passive measurements;
- controlled probes;
- counterfactual source perturbations;
- their noise and error laws;
- resource-accounted access; and
- the complete declared observer boundary.

A “footprint” is simply one coordinate of \(\Sigma^-\). It is not a second
logical kind of evidence.

## 3. `HC-DU-089A` — operational-preexistence criterion

### Theorem

The preactivation source target \(P_\tau\) is exactly reconstructible from the
complete admitted preactivation signature iff:

\[
\ker\Sigma^-
\subseteq
\ker P_\tau.
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, every dormant-fixed and absent pair must be separated by at
least one declared intervention or resource query.

### Proof

This is the fibre-factorization theorem. If
\(P_\tau=d\circ\Sigma^-\), equal signatures imply equal target values.
Conversely, if \(P_\tau\) is constant on every signature fibre, define
\(d(\Sigma^-(h))=P_\tau(h)\); condition (1) makes the decoder
well-defined. \(\square\)

### Attribution

This is an exact specialization of `HC-DU-056`, not a new observability
theorem. Its additive role is to collapse the proposed intervention and
footprint reopeners into one typed object.

### What a positive proves

If an action \(u\) produces different response laws in the two source
classes, then the classes are operationally distinct relative to:

- \(u\);
- its physical coupling;
- the observer/source boundary;
- the measurement accuracy and noise model;
- the resource envelope; and
- the declared meaning of \(P_\tau\).

The result can rule out a specified “absent” or “dormant but inert” model. It
does not establish a context-free ontology of type identity.

### What a null proves

If every admitted preactivation query agrees, the two models are
operationally equivalent under that contract. Enlarging the action family may
split the class:

\[
\mathcal A^-_0\subseteq\mathcal A^-_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ker\Sigma^-_1\subseteq\ker\Sigma^-_0.
\]

Therefore a null result certifies absence only when the query family is
independently complete for the claimed source target. Declaring it complete
because it returned a null would be target refitting.

## 4. A minimum separating positive

Suppose a declared dormant-reservoir model contains a two-state carrier
\(\{|0\rangle,|\tau\rangle\}\) before activation, and a predeclared QND probe
measures:

\[
Q_\tau=|\tau\rangle\langle\tau|.
\]

If:

\[
\Pr(Q_\tau=1\mid P_\tau=1)=1,
\qquad
\Pr(Q_\tau=1\mid P_\tau=0)=0,
\]

then one probe certifies operational preexistence in that model class.

But every load-bearing item was supplied:

- the carrier;
- the \(\tau\)-projector;
- the physical source-to-projector semantics;
- access to it before activation;
- QND implementability;
- zero error; and
- exclusion of hidden generators that do not occupy \(|\tau\rangle\).

This is a valid positive control. It is not a universal issuance assay.

## 5. `HC-DU-089B` — no type-count-only energy floor

### Proposition

The number of semantic type labels available in a model does not, by itself,
imply a positive incremental preactivation energy, heat, or work cost.

### Degenerate-state control

Let:

\[
\mathcal H_N=\operatorname{span}
\{|1\rangle,\ldots,|N\rangle\}
\]

and choose:

\[
H=E_0 I.
\]

The \(N\) orthogonal label states have the same energy. Changing which basis
state denotes a dormant type changes no energy expectation:

\[
\langle i|H|i\rangle=E_0
\quad\text{for every }i.
\]

This does not claim that an arbitrarily large reliable memory has zero total
physical cost. It disproves the narrower inference:

```text
one additional semantic type
  -> one universal positive energy increment.
```

Spatial support, barriers, reliability, preparation, readout, and state-space
capacity remain physical costs only after their contracts are fixed.

### Landauer correction

Landauer applies when a logically irreversible operation compresses several
distinguishable logical states into fewer states while disposing of entropy
to an environment. Merely retaining a dormant type or reversibly evolving a
register is not an erasure event.

Therefore this inference is invalid:

\[
\text{dormant type count }N
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Nk_BT\ln2
\text{ preactivation heat}.
\]

An update that overwrites an unknown registry, resets a workspace, or erases
history may incur a Landauer-style lower bound under its thermodynamic
contract. That cost attaches to the irreversible operation and discarded
information, not to the ontological status of a type.

## 6. The compact-generator absorber

Let \(p\) be a fixed program or physical rule that emits a sequence of types:

\[
\tau_j=p(j).
\]

The source need not store one independently addressable register per
\(\tau_j\). Its preactivation description burden is bounded by the generator,
input, and state:

\[
L_{\mathrm{source}}
\lesssim
|p|+|j|+\text{working state},
\]

not by the number of distinct outputs already produced or still possible.

For a finite completed history \(w\), the shortest-description burden is
related to its algorithmic description length, not its raw type count.
Low-description, high-logical-depth histories can be cheap to specify and
slow to produce.

Consequences:

1. A footprint proportional to an explicitly enumerated registry can rule out
   that registry implementation.
2. It does not rule out a compressed grammar, seed, boundary source, or fixed
   generator.
3. A runtime bound can pressure the generator only after a deadline and
   computational class are frozen.
4. A completed history can relocate description support outside the declared
   source unless the boundary is physically closed.

This is the same fixed-completion adversary that absorbed type arrival,
forecast novelty, and commitment nonmembership. Resource language does not
remove it automatically.

## 7. When finite-region capacity genuinely bites

Suppose a candidate dormant-reservoir model makes the stronger claim:

> Before activation, \(N\) alternatives are simultaneously resident,
> independently addressable, and reliably distinguishable inside one complete
> bounded system of radius \(R\) and total gravitating energy \(E\).

For an equiprobable \(N\)-state memory, the information entropy is:

\[
S=k_B\ln N.
\]

Under the Bekenstein-bound scope:

\[
S
\le
\frac{2\pi k_BER}{\hbar c},
\]

so:

\[
\ln N
\le
\frac{2\pi ER}{\hbar c}.
\tag{2}
\]

Equation (2) can exclude a declared combination of resident state count,
region, and energy.

It does not apply merely because a fixed generator has \(N\) possible future
outputs. Those outputs are not automatically \(N\) simultaneously resident,
equiprobable, independently addressable memory states.

The result is therefore:

```text
finite-region capacity
  -> conditional explicit-reservoir bound
  -/-> source issuance
  -/-> enumeration of every possible future type.
```

## 8. When speed genuinely bites

For a system with average energy \(E\) above its ground state, the
Margolus--Levitin bound gives the orthogonalization-time scale:

\[
\Delta t
\ge
\frac{\pi\hbar}{2E}.
\tag{3}
\]

Related counting bounds constrain how many mutually distinct state transitions
can occur in a fixed interval.

If a dormant-generator model must:

- accept an activation query by a frozen deadline;
- traverse a declared number of distinguishable intermediate states;
- use a fixed energy budget; and
- remain inside the declared source boundary,

then (3) or a sharper model-specific computational lower bound can exclude
part of its parameter space.

If activation may take arbitrarily long, an absolute “too expensive” argument
fails. The resource discriminator is a scaling/deadline claim, not an appeal
to an outside implementer's patience.

## 10. Matched-signature counterexample

Let two source histories share:

- the same observable preactivation state on the declared source algebra;
- the same energy, stress-energy, charges, spatial support, and entropy;
- the same response law for every \(u\in\mathcal A^-\cup\mathcal Q^-\);
- the same activation time and formed later type record; and
- the same post-activation observer capabilities.

Interpret the fixed hidden rule in:

- \(h_D\) as a dormant generator for \(\tau\); and
- \(h_I\) as a rule whose realized transition first constitutes \(\tau\).

If no independently fixed physical criterion distinguishes those
interpretations before activation, then:

\[
\Sigma^-(h_D)=\Sigma^-(h_I),
\qquad
P_\tau(h_D)\ne P_\tau(h_I).
\]

No preactivation physical decoder exists. Either:

1. \(P_\tau\) contains extra source structure omitted from the signature; or
2. the two labels describe one physical process and the claimed distinction is
   semantic rather than empirical.

This is why the physical adapter cannot be replaced by increasingly detailed
resource bookkeeping.

## 11. What an experiment could honestly establish

### Positive source-sensitive experiment

Predeclare:

- two physically specified source classes;
- one intervention or footprint observable with different predicted laws;
- complete acquisition and error model;
- frozen source boundary;
- no post-result decoder refit; and
- later type occurrence/capability target.

A measured response can reject one source class and establish operational
preexistence relative to the surviving class.

### Negative experiment

A null excludes only dormant-source models predicting a detectable response
above the assay's sensitivity. It does not establish issuance against:

- inaccessible hidden state;
- compact generators;
- completed histories;
- boundary relocation;
- slower computation;
- models below the resolution floor; or
- physically equivalent relabelings.

### Hardware boundary

No hardware is warranted now because no physical theory has supplied a
specific dormant carrier and preactivation observable whose result would
survive the completion rivals. The local proof identifies what a future
physical proposal must provide before an experiment becomes informative.
