# Physically formed sharp instruments

## Result in plain English

The finite circuit gives a qualified positive result and two decisive
no-go results.

The positive result is:

> If a source interaction has already selected a nondegenerate two-sector
> nondemolition algebra, an explicit pointer/archive coupling is required to
> form a record, the coupling is required not to disturb that frozen source
> algebra, and the resulting measurement is exactly repeatable, then the
> aligned outcome observable is a sharp PVM in the executed binary QND
> family.

This is a real derivation *inside the frozen family*. It is stronger than
calling the source commutant a record. The executable builds the system,
pointer, archive, pointer-to-archive transfer, final readout, provenance, and
repeatability sequence. A sharp outcome instrument appears only at the
zero-crossover endpoint.

The first no-go is:

> Formation, repeatability, nondisturbance, access, provenance, and a matched
> finite resource ledger do not choose a basis unless nondisturbance is
> evaluated against a source algebra frozen independently of the candidate
> measurement.

Every unitary-conjugate pointer circuit passes the same *self-relative*
conditions. Freezing the source \(Z\)-sector algebra breaks that orbit: the
aligned \(Z\) instrument preserves it exactly, while the sharp \(X\)
instrument does not. The source algebra is therefore load-bearing. The
pointer basis is not generated by the words “formed,” “repeatable,” or
“accessible.”

The second no-go is:

> Even a physically formed, sharp, repeatable, centrally nondisturbing record
> does not generally select a unique selective instrument.

With two-dimensional multiplicity inside each sharp sector, a Lüders
instrument and a within-sector-twisted instrument have the same PVM, archive
outcomes, repeatability, and central-sector nondisturbance. They nevertheless
send the same input to orthogonal post-measurement states. Full
within-sector action nondisturbance rejects the twist, but that stronger
condition is additional structure.

There is also an end-to-end access correction. A perfect pointer PVM followed
by a ten-percent archive bit-flip channel presents the observer with an
unsharp accessible POVM. Three-copy majority decoding improves the error but
does not make it exactly zero. Sharpness at the pointer is therefore not
automatically sharpness of the final record.

The repeatability/sharpness part is not new quantum mathematics.
[Buscemi, Kobayashi, and Minagawa](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2024-01-25-1235)
give a complete operational resource theory in which maximal sharp
finite-dimensional POVMs coincide with POVMs admitting repeatable
measurement ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07737)). Quantum-instrument
theory already separates outcome effects from selective state updates and
physical dilations. The Dynamic Unity result is the exact location of the
remaining physical-selection burden:

```text
source-selected algebra
    + formed pointer/archive coupling
    + source-relative nondisturbance
    + exact repeatability
    -> sharp aligned outcome PVM in the frozen binary family

but not
    -> generation of the coupling
    -> unique continuation inside degenerate sectors
    -> exact observer-accessible sharpness through a noisy archive
    -> public finality.
```

## Complete event order

1. prepare the system and blank pointer/archive states;
2. apply the frozen system-pointer unitary;
3. copy the pointer computational value to the archive by CNOT;
4. apply the declared archive-noise channel;
5. dephase or read the archive in its frozen basis;
6. retain the setting and sequence provenance; and
7. discard or ignore the pointer only after the archive transfer.

A repeatability assay executes the same sequence again with a new pointer and
archive. The circuit therefore charges the fresh support that a second
measurement requires.

The finite resource ledger per attempt is:

- one persistent system qubit;
- one fresh pointer qubit;
- one fresh archive-outcome qubit;
- one classical setting/provenance bit for the two-basis control;
- one system-pointer unitary;

- one archive dephasing/readout.

The model explicitly excludes macroscopic amplification, thermodynamic reset
work, long-horizon memory failure, laboratory control synthesis, and public

## The explicit pointer family

Let the candidate system basis be

\[
P_j^{(\theta)}
=W_\theta |j\rangle\langle j|W_\theta^\dagger
\]

with real qubit rotation \(W_\theta\). For
\(0\leq\epsilon\leq 1/2\), write

\[
a=\sqrt{1-\epsilon},
\qquad
b=\sqrt{\epsilon}.
\]

The two complete pointer unitaries are

\[
R_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
a & b\\
b & -a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
R_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
b & a\\
a & -b
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and the system-pointer interaction is

\[
U_{\theta,\epsilon}
=P_0^{(\theta)}\otimes R_0
+P_1^{(\theta)}\otimes R_1.
\]

The pointer begins in \(|0\rangle_P\). Reading it in the frozen
computational basis produces

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_0
  &=aP_0^{(\theta)}+bP_1^{(\theta)},\\
M_1
  &=bP_0^{(\theta)}+aP_1^{(\theta)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The outcome effects are

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_0
  &=(1-\epsilon)P_0^{(\theta)}
    +\epsilon P_1^{(\theta)},\\
E_1
  &=\epsilon P_0^{(\theta)}
    +(1-\epsilon)P_1^{(\theta)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The executable constructs the complete \(S\)-to-\(SPA\) isometry, not just
these Kraus operators, and verifies that measuring the copied archive
recovers the same selective instrument exactly.

## Theorem 1 — scoped formed-sharp equivalence

Within this binary QND pointer family with noiseless pointer-to-archive
transduction and \(0\leq\epsilon\leq1/2\), the following are equivalent:

1. the two effects are projections;
2. the probability that two immediate identical measurements disagree is
   zero for every system input;
3. the archive perfectly distinguishes the two supplied sector
   eigenpreparations; and
4. \(\epsilon=0\).

### Proof

Because the effects have eigenvalues
\(\{1-\epsilon,\epsilon\}\),

\[
\left\|E_j^2-E_j\right\|_\infty
=\epsilon(1-\epsilon).
\]

Thus they are projections exactly at \(\epsilon=0\) in the frozen interval.

For any input state \(\rho\), the joint probability that the two outcomes
differ is

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\rm mismatch}
&=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
  M_1M_0\rho M_0^\dagger M_1^\dagger
\right]
+\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
  M_0M_1\rho M_1^\dagger M_0^\dagger
\right]\\
&=2\epsilon(1-\epsilon).
\end{aligned}
\]

It vanishes on the same endpoint.

After the pointer has been copied and the pointer discarded, the conditional
archive distributions for the two sector inputs are

\[
(1-\epsilon,\epsilon)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(\epsilon,1-\epsilon).
\]

Their trace distance is

\[
D_A=1-2\epsilon,
\]

so perfect sector disclosure also occurs exactly at \(\epsilon=0\).
\(\square\)

The corresponding residual system coherence is

\[
V=2\sqrt{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)}
\]

and the circuit obeys

\[
D_A^2+V^2=1.
\]

This is standard information-disturbance structure. Its purpose here is to
prevent “formed archive” from being confused with “perfect record of the
source sector.” An orthogonal classical archive outcome exists for every
\(\epsilon\), but its proposition fidelity varies continuously.

### Collision status

This theorem is only a finite square-root-QND specialization. The broader
operational relation between sharpness and repeatable measurement is
occupied by Buscemi, Kobayashi, and Minagawa. Approximate repeatability and
intrinsic unsharpness also have an established literature. Dynamic Unity
should not promote the displayed equivalence as a new theorem of quantum
measurement.

## Theorem 2 — the frozen source algebra is load-bearing

If each candidate is allowed to define nondisturbance relative to its own
basis, then

\[
U_{\theta,\epsilon}
=
(W_\theta\otimes I)
U_{0,\epsilon}
(W_\theta^\dagger\otimes I).
\]

Every conjugate circuit has the same formation fidelity, repeatability
defect, provenance alphabet, and resource cost. Those properties admit the
orbit; they do not choose a representative.

Now freeze the source algebra independently at

\[
\mathcal A_H
=\operatorname{span}\{P_0^{(0)},P_1^{(0)}\}.
\]

At \(\epsilon=0\), the aligned \(Z\)-basis instrument obeys

\[
\sum_j M_j^\dagger P_h^{(0)}M_j=P_h^{(0)}
\]

for both \(h\). The sharp \(X\)-basis instrument instead maps each frozen
\(Z\) projector to \(I/2\); the executable maximum-entry deviation is
\(1/2\).

Therefore the source-relative nondisturbance condition rejects the named
misaligned foil. Combined with Theorem 1, it yields:

> Within the tested nondegenerate binary family, a separately selected
> source algebra, an explicitly formed aligned pointer/archive circuit, and
> exact repeatability earn the aligned sharp outcome PVM.

The conclusion is relative to the supplied source algebra and candidate
coupling family. It does not show that the source dynamics creates a pointer,
archive, or readout. It also does not prove uniqueness against every
possible instrument outside the family.

## No-go 1 — a sharp PVM does not select a sharp instrument

The multiplicity control uses

\[
\mathcal H
=\mathcal H_0\oplus\mathcal H_1,
\qquad
\dim\mathcal H_0=\dim\mathcal H_1=2
\]

with sector projectors \(P_0,P_1\).

Compare:

\[
L_j=P_j
\]

with

\[
K_0=V_0P_0,
\qquad
K_1=P_1,
\]

where \(V_0\) swaps two orthogonal states inside \(\mathcal H_0\).

Both instruments have:

- the same effects \(P_0,P_1\);
- exact trace preservation;
- exact outcome repeatability;
- exact preservation of the central sector projectors; and
- the same pointer/archive outcome kernel.

But on one sector-zero input their conditional outputs have trace distance
one. An internal sector observable is preserved by the Lüders instrument and
sent to its negative by the twisted instrument; the executable
Heisenberg-picture deviation is two.

Thus:

```text
formed sharp record
    + outcome repeatability
    + central nondisturbance
    != unique selective continuation.
```

Requiring nondisturbance of the full actionable algebra inside each sector
rejects this twist. Whether that full algebra is physically accessible and
why it should be preserved must be frozen independently; otherwise
“nondisturbance” is being strengthened after the counterexample appears.

## No-go 2 — pointer sharpness is not end-to-end record sharpness

Let the underlying pointer measurement be the exact
\(\epsilon=0\) PVM, but let the archive bit flip independently with
probability \(q\). The observer-accessible effects become

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0&=(1-q)P_0+qP_1,\\
F_1&=qP_0+(1-q)P_1.
\end{aligned}
\]

At \(q=0.1\):

- accessible sector trace distance is \(0.8\);
- effect projection defect is \(q(1-q)=0.09\).

Three independent copies followed by majority decoding reduce the effective
error to

\[
q_3=3q^2(1-q)+q^3=0.028,
\]

and the projection defect to \(0.027216\). The improvement is real, but exact
sharpness is not restored with finite noisy redundancy.

This gives a precise interface correction:

> A sharp source-to-pointer instrument and a robustly readable archive are
> separate receipts. End-to-end sharpness is relative to the observer's
> final accessible record channel and declared error tolerance.

An approximate physical theorem should therefore use an error-qualified
notion of repeatability and action sufficiency rather than silently treating
small archive error as zero.

demonstrates why finite redundancy is not by itself an exact finality law.
