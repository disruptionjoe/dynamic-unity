# Distributed/physical record collision

## Result in plain English

The distributed-systems work transfers into physics—but only through a
specific part of the architecture.

Once physics has actually formed a target-sensitive record, distributed
techniques can:

- copy it;
- move it;
- reduce readout error under an independence model;
- preserve declared provenance;
- make it usable by different regions;
- bind a receiver to the right epoch and calibration; and
- harden a rule for acting on it.

They cannot establish that the record was physically formed from the claimed
source. They also cannot make the record answer a stronger physical question
that the source record itself erased.

The exact first break is:

```text
authenticated declared origin
    !=
causal physical source binding
```

A signature, hash ancestry, quorum certificate, threshold proof, or
zero-knowledge proof can certify a declared digital relation. The same
terminal certificate can be attached to:

1. a pointer genuinely written by a gauge-boundary interaction; or
2. a pointer that was preloaded or pre-correlated without that physical
   source--pointer event.

The certificate cannot distinguish those cases because it is downstream of
the same visible value. A physical source-intervention or separately formed
coupling/provenance record can distinguish them, but that is an additional
physical input—not stronger cryptography over the old value.

The useful positive result is equally exact:

> Independent corruption of redundant archive cells can reduce the risk of
> reading an already formed flux record incorrectly. It cannot create a new
> physical distinction, and common-shock copies provide no such scaling gain.

The material gauge archive is therefore:

- exact for the frozen boundary-flux action;
- inexact for interior charge, unlogged crossing, or microscopic formation
  history;
- safely transferable only with archive availability, current epoch,
  calibrated orientation, and a still-preserved boundary action envelope;
  and
- a law-constrained, action-sufficient coarse-graining—not strong emergence
  or a new physical phase transition.

The closed metastable host remains adverse. Replicating, signing, voting on,
or hardening its terminal endpoint does not recover whether the historical
write occurred. Full-history access repairs that target only by injective
tomography and still is not selected by the host.

The Position-5 return is therefore a three-part package:

```text
SCOPED_AMPLIFICATION_WITHOUT_INFORMATION_CREATION_THEOREM
CAPABILITY_RELATIVE_REGIONAL_FINALITY_THEOREM
EXACT_DISTRIBUTED_PHYSICAL_NON_UNIFICATION
```

This is exact finite typed synthesis using known mathematics. It is not a new
distributed-systems theorem, gauge theorem, quantum law, strong-emergence
result, physics prediction, or ontology verdict.

## 3. Physical Record Downstream No-Minting Theorem

### Statement

Let:

- \(H\) be a frozen physical history/completion class;
- \(q:H\to Q\) be a physically formed record;
- \(R\) be any finite or standard measurable downstream record generated by
  a channel \(K(\cdot\mid q(h))\);
- \(C\) be any further randomized or deterministic decoder, replicated view,
  signature, commitment, proof, vote, threshold certificate, or hardened
  action label generated only from \(R\); and
- \(t:H\to T\) be a frozen physical target.

If

\[
q(h_1)=q(h_2),
\]

then every downstream law agrees:

\[
\mathcal L(C\mid h_1)=\mathcal L(C\mid h_2).
\]

Consequently, if \(t(h_1)\neq t(h_2)\) on one \(q\)-fibre, no downstream
layer can make \(t\) exactly reconstructible or statistically distinguish
those two histories.

### Proof

The frozen architecture is the Markov chain

\[
H\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow C.
\]

For histories with the same \(q\)-value, the conditional law of \(R\) is the
same by construction:

\[
K(\cdot\mid q(h_1))=K(\cdot\mid q(h_2)).
\]

Applying any common postprocessing channel from \(R\) to \(C\) preserves that
equality. Therefore \(C\) cannot distinguish the two histories. If their
target values differ, no target decoder through \(C\) exists. \(\square\)

This is a direct factorization/data-processing result. The DU value is its
unchanged use across physical replicas, protocol certificates, and the
hostile host—not mathematical novelty.

### Important correction retained from `HC-DU-047`

The theorem begins at the **complete joint admitted input**. It does not say
that individually insufficient records cannot be jointly sufficient.
Independent detectors or complementary physical records can add
target-sensitive joint information when each is formed from the source.

The gauge archive cells in this specimen are different:

```text
physical source F -> one formed pointer Q -> copied archive cells
```

Their corruption events may be independent, but their source content is
downstream of one common \(Q\). They are independent transmission/storage
channels, not independent physical sources about interior charge or
formation history.

## 4. Exact reliability boundary

Suppose an odd number \(N\) of archive cells are copied from the formed flux
record. Each cell is independently corrupted with probability \(p<1/2\).
Majority decoding has exact error

\[
P_{\rm iid}^{(N)}
=
\sum_{k=(N+1)/2}^{N}
\binom Nk p^k(1-p)^{N-k}.
\]

For \(p=1/10\):

\[
P_{\rm iid}^{(1)}=\frac1{10},
\qquad
P_{\rm iid}^{(3)}=\frac7{250}.
\]

Thus three cells materially improve the bounded-risk readout.

Under one common shock that flips all \(N\) copies together,

\[
P_{\rm common}^{(N)}=p
\]

for every \(N\). Raw replication has no scaling gain.

### Exact reconstructibility remains separate

For the separate finite full-support control used by the certificate, each
cell adds one of \(0,1,2\pmod3\), with every error value admitted on positive
support.

The received triple

\[
(0,0,0)
\]

can arise from:

- \(F=0\) with no cell errors; or
- \(F=2\) with three errors.

Therefore neither the complete noisy triple nor its majority value exactly
reconstructs \(F\) on full support. The lower majority-readout risk in the
independent binary-error model above is real; exact factorization under
overlapping noise support is still false.

At \(p=0\), ideal copies exactly preserve \(F\). They still do not recover the
interior distinction:

\[
(n_1,n_2)=(0,1)
\quad\text{versus}\quad
(1,0),
\]

because both have \(F=1\). No number of downstream copies splits that
source-record fibre.

This earns:

```text
SCOPED_AMPLIFICATION_WITHOUT_INFORMATION_CREATION_THEOREM
```

not a universal scalar “effective observer count,” phase transition, or
strong-emergence result.

### Capability enlargement

\[

\[

## 7. The exact distributed/physical non-unification

### 7.1 Matched visible-record worlds

Construct two physical processes with the same terminal record value \(q\):

1. **QND-formed world:** the boundary interaction writes the physical flux
   into the pointer.
2. **Preloaded world:** the pointer is prepared with the same value but the
   claimed source--pointer write did not occur.

Let

\[
\sigma(q)
\]

be any ideal signature, hash commitment, quorum certificate, threshold
signature, or proof whose public output is a function of the declared record
and protocol setup.

Then

\[
\sigma(q)_{\rm formed}
=
\sigma(q)_{\rm preloaded}.
\]

Therefore the terminal certificate does not reconstruct formation mode.

A physical source intervention, a retained coupling trace, or a separately
formed provenance meta-record can distinguish the processes. Adding it is a
real repair, but it expands the physical record:

\[
(q,\text{coupling witness}).
\]

It is not a consequence of applying more cryptography to \(q\).

### 7.2 Why signatures remain useful

This negative result does not make signatures or consensus useless.

Relative to a supplied membership, key, fault, and validity contract, a hard
certificate can be sufficient for:

- rejecting conflicting authorized values;
- deciding which declared value to act on;
- attributing a signer when the certificate retains signer identity;
- revealing encountered equivocation; or
- proving a declared predicate without disclosing its witness.

Those are genuine capability gains. They are downstream of the physical
input relation.

### 7.3 First noncommuting square

The attempted translation is:

```text
digital message ------------> signed message
      |                              |
      | proposed physical map        | proposed physical map
      v                              v
physical record -----------> physically sourced record
```

The upper arrow authenticates a declared message under a key/setup model. The
lower arrow would have to certify that a particular physical interaction
formed the record from the claimed source. The matched formed/preloaded
worlds show that the square does not commute.

Thus the first exact failure is:

```text
AUTHENTICATED_DECLARED_ORIGIN
    -/-> CAUSAL_PHYSICAL_SOURCE_BINDING
```

### 7.4 Dual-mesh handoff interpretation

Joe's MMO render-mesh/collision-mesh comparison gives the cleanest
operational reading of this boundary.

An MMO client can receive:

- a detailed render mesh;
- an authentic signature over that asset; and
- a current-looking regional replication packet,

while the authoritative collision mesh differs. The render artifact controls
what is displayed. The collision artifact controls whether the avatar is
blocked, passes through, takes damage, or triggers an interaction.

The exact two-row control is:

| signed render | collision mesh | action |
|---|---|---|
| `wall-visible-v7` | `solid-wall-v7` | `BLOCK` |
| `wall-visible-v7` | `ghost-wall-v7` | `PASS` |

The signed render view perfectly reconstructs which visible asset was
delivered. It does not reconstruct the collision response:

\[
\ker R_{\rm render}
\not\subseteq
\ker A_{\rm collision}.
\]

Safe handoff needs either:

\[
A_{\rm collision}=d\circ R_{\rm render},
\]

or a separately formed authoritative binding:

\[
(R_{\rm render},C_{\rm collision}).
\]

Signing the render asset more strongly cannot create that binding.

The physical reading is:

```text
certified public record / representation surface
    resembles the render mesh

causal physical source and action surface
    resembles the collision mesh
```

The analogy is structural, not ontological. Physics is not thereby an MMO.
Its value is to show why representation provenance can be exact while action
provenance remains open, and why the repair must bind the two surfaces rather
than merely authenticate one of them.
