## Executive result

Once a finite readout cut, symmetry family, post-write dynamics, and
deterministic observer channels have been declared, there is an exact
canonical quotient containing every fixed-label scalar fact that can survive
those constraints. Selection and access combine by joining their
indistinguishability relations.

In plain English:

> The dynamics does not choose a record merely because a record can be copied.
> After the relevant physical constraints have been supplied, however, the
> finest fact that survives them is computable exactly.

The more important architectural finding is that local hardening and later
pooling do not generally commute:

> Complementary raw fragments can jointly retain an invariant fact that is

## 1. Frozen finite contract

Let \(X\) be a finite set of complete histories or states at a declared
readout cut.

Freeze:

- a family \(G\) of declared representation or symmetry maps \(g:X\to X\);
- a family \(K\) of permitted post-write evolutions \(k:X\to X\);
- deterministic observer channels \(o_i:X\to Y_i\);
- a nonempty authorized-coalition family \(\mathcal Q\);
- a fixed-label scalar interpretation for candidate facts; and
- every interface, access, decoder, blank support, provenance, and resource
  assumption used to justify those objects.

Define the selection relation

\[
E_{\mathrm{sel}}
=
\operatorname{EqCl}\left(
\{(x,gx):g\in G\}
\cup
\{(x,kx):k\in K\}
\right),
\]

the least equivalence relation containing all declared equality constraints.

For observer \(i\), define

\[
E_i=\ker(o_i).
\]

Under the explicitly classical and nondisturbing tuple-readout assumption, a
coalition \(S\) has channel

\[
o_S=(o_i)_{i\in S}
\quad\text{and}\quad
E_S=\ker(o_S)=\bigcap_{i\in S}E_i.
\]

The relation for a fixed-label fact independently recoverable by every
authorized coalition is

\[
E_{\mathcal Q}
=
\bigvee_{S\in\mathcal Q}E_S.
\]

The symbol \(\vee\) is equivalence closure of the union; \(\wedge\) is
relation intersection. Partitions are ordered by refinement, so a finer
partition carries at least as many distinctions.

## 2. The finite record-selection theorem

### Theorem 1 — selection coequalizer

Let

\[
q_{\mathrm{sel}}:X\longrightarrow X/E_{\mathrm{sel}}
\]

be the canonical quotient. For any set \(A\) and map \(r:X\to A\), the
following are equivalent:

1. \(r(gx)=r(x)\) for every \(g\in G\), \(x\in X\), and
   \(r(kx)=r(x)\) for every \(k\in K\), \(x\in X\);
2. \(E_{\mathrm{sel}}\subseteq\ker(r)\);
3. there is a unique map
   \(\bar r:X/E_{\mathrm{sel}}\to A\) with
   \(r=\bar r\circ q_{\mathrm{sel}}\).

Therefore \(q_{\mathrm{sel}}\) is the canonical finest **set-valued**
invariant and durable quotient. It is nontrivial exactly when the equality
graph generated by \(G\cup K\) has more than one connected component.

### Proof

Condition 1 says that \(r\) gives equal values to every generating pair of
\(E_{\mathrm{sel}}\). Equality is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive, so
\(r\) is constant on the generated equivalence classes, which is condition 2.
Condition 2 makes

\[
\bar r([x])=r(x)
\]

well-defined and gives the factorization in condition 3. Since
\(q_{\mathrm{sel}}\) is surjective, every value of \(\bar r\) is forced, so
the factor is unique. Conversely, any factorization through the quotient is
constant on each quotient class and therefore satisfies every generating
constraint. \(\square\)

No semigroup closure assumption is needed for this Set-level statement:
equality under every declared generator already implies equality under their
compositions. A structured process theory may require a stronger congruence
or coequalizer; Section 6 preserves that limitation.

### Fixed-codomain correction

The universal object is \(X/E_{\mathrm{sel}}\), not necessarily one record
inside a preselected scalar codomain \(A\).

If \(|A|\ge |X/E_{\mathrm{sel}}|\), an injection of quotient classes into
\(A\) supplies a maximally informative representative, unique only up to
relabeling. If \(A\) is too small, a finest \(A\)-valued record need not
exist.

The smallest counterexample has three unconstrained states and a binary
codomain. Its three nonconstant two-block partitions are mutually
incomparable, so none is the unique finest binary record even though the
three-class set quotient is canonical.

## 3. Coalition-common access theorem

### Theorem 2 — pooled evidence versus a common deterministic fact

Assume all coalition tuple channels \(o_S\) are physically jointly available.
A scalar fact \(f:X\to A\) is recoverable from every authorized coalition
\(S\in\mathcal Q\) if and only if

\[
E_{\mathcal Q}
=
\bigvee_{S\in\mathcal Q}\ker(o_S)
\subseteq
\ker(f).
\]

Consequently

\[
q_{\mathcal Q}:X\longrightarrow X/E_{\mathcal Q}
\]

is the finest set-valued common deterministic factor of all authorized
coalition channels.

### Proof

For a fixed \(S\), a decoder \(d_S\) on the reachable image
\(\operatorname{im}(o_S)\) satisfying \(f=d_S\circ o_S\) exists exactly when
\(\ker(o_S)\subseteq\ker(f)\). Its values outside the reachable image, if the
declared codomain contains such values, are not canonically determined.
Requiring recoverability for every \(S\) says that \(\ker(f)\) contains every
coalition kernel, hence their equivalence join. The quotient factorization
then follows from Theorem 1's elementary argument. \(\square\)

If \(\mathcal Q\) is upward closed, its minimal authorized coalitions suffice:
a superset has at least the tuple information of its subset.

This object is best called the **coalition-common deterministic factor**. It
is not common knowledge, a Byzantine certificate, public finality, or a
physical broadcast structure.

### Theorem 3 — selected and accessible fact

A fixed-label scalar fact is both invariant/durable under the selection
contract and recoverable by every authorized coalition exactly when it
factors through

\[
q_*:
X\longrightarrow
X/(E_{\mathrm{sel}}\vee E_{\mathcal Q}).
\]

This is immediate because its kernel must contain both equivalence relations,
and therefore their join.

## 4. The hardening-order result

Let \(A=E_{\mathrm{sel}}\) and let \(B,C\) be two observer kernels. The
partition lattice always satisfies

\[
A\vee(B\wedge C)
\;\preceq\;
(A\vee B)\wedge(A\vee C),
\]

where \(\preceq\) means “refines.” The inclusion can be strict because the
partition lattice is nondistributive.

The exact three-state fixture uses

```text
A = 001
B = 010
C = 011
```

as restricted-growth partition labels. Then:

```text
raw pooling first:
    B meet C = 012
    A join (B meet C) = 001       # two classes survive

local scalar hardening first:
    A join B = 000
    A join C = 000
    (A join B) meet (A join C) = 000
                                      # only a constant survives
```

The mathematical conclusion is exact:

> Enforcing the selection equivalence separately on each marginal fragment
> and then pooling can destroy distinctions retained by pooling the fragments
> first and enforcing selection on the joint object.

The research implication is conditional but actionable:

- preserve raw, relational, or equivariant fragment information long enough
  to form the authorized coalition channel;
- carry provenance so that aliases, replay, and Sybil copies do not masquerade
  as independent evidence;
- then apply the selected invariant/finality map to the joint object; and
- compare that architecture against local-first hardening as a required null.

This is the strongest output of the wave for the intended distributed/quantum
bridge. It remains an architectural conjecture until it survives direct
coalition channels, adversaries, and quantum readouts.

## 6. What the theorem does not establish

### It does not form a record

The theorem begins at a declared readout cut and asks which facts survive
declared constraints. With identity post-write dynamics, it can return every
state distinction even though nothing was ever written. Record formation,
provenance, acquisition, and irreversible stabilization remain separate.

### It does not select the physical constraints

Changing the permitted cadence changes the quotient. A two-state flip makes
the one-step invariant quotient constant, while declaring only two-step
preservation makes the identity quotient survive. The physical readout
cadence cannot be fitted after seeing the desired answer.

### It does not turn covariance into literal invariance

A physical symmetry may transport the record codomain. The identity channel
\(X\to X\) is fully equivariant under the transported action while every
fixed-label scalar invariant under a transitive action is constant.

### It is a Set quotient, not automatically a process quotient

An orbit quotient can fail to preserve a supplied binary composition even
when the group acts by simultaneous automorphisms. Structured theories need a
congruence or a coequalizer in the appropriate category.

### It does not guarantee access

The selected quotient can contain distinctions absent from every observer
channel. The combined quotient \(q_*\), rather than
\(q_{\mathrm{sel}}\) alone, is the maximum claim licensed by this contract.

### Marginal kernels are not general coalition channels

Kernel intersection assumes a jointly available nondisturbing tuple of
classical readouts. Destructive sequential reads can defeat that tuple.
Correlated quantum histories can have identical one-fragment marginals yet
remain jointly orthogonal. Quantum and adversarial work must define the
coalition channel directly.

### It does not establish public finality

Common deterministic readability does not prevent equivocation. With
\(N=3\), quorum size \(q=2\), and one Byzantine node, two certificates can
intersect only at the faulty node. Provenance, authentication, honest locking,
fault bounds, timing assumptions, and action semantics are additional typed
requirements.

### It does not derive quantum-to-classical transition

Every scalar fact in the theorem is already commutative. The construction
cannot show why a noncommutative quantum process selects a stable
approximately classical public algebra.
