## 2. Exact response-factorization theorem

### Proposition 1 — fixed-readout Weyl response

For all admitted \(f,h\in\mathcal V_g\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\),

\[
\mathcal D_{\lambda f}(W(h))
=
e^{i\lambda\sigma(f,h)}W(h).
\]

#### Proof

The Weyl relation and antisymmetry of \(\sigma\) give

\[
W(-\lambda f)W(h)
=
e^{i\lambda\sigma(f,h)/2}W(h-\lambda f).
\]

Multiplying by \(W(\lambda f)\) on the right gives a second phase
\(e^{i\lambda\sigma(f,h)/2}\). Their product is
\(e^{i\lambda\sigma(f,h)}\), proving (1). \(\square\)

This is an operator identity. It does not depend on a state, temperature,
vacuum choice, quasifree covariance, detector noise model, or outcome
probability.

### Corollary 1 — calibrated local identification

If \(\lambda\) is calibrated and the channel phase is known on any interval
containing zero, then (2) identifies \(\sigma(f,h)\) exactly.

At one fixed nonzero amplitude \(\lambda_0\), it does not. For every
\(k\in\mathbb Z\),

\[
\sigma'(f,h)
=
\sigma(f,h)+\frac{2\pi k}{\lambda_0}
\]

gives the same phase. A single amplitude becomes sufficient only after an
independently warranted range bound selects one phase branch. Multiple
amplitudes help only relative to their calibration and a declared
unwrapping/range contract.

### Corollary 2 — state cancellation and its limit

For any state \(\omega\),

\[
F_{f,h,\omega}(\lambda)
:=
\omega\!\left(\mathcal D_{\lambda f}(W(h))\right)
=
e^{i\lambda\sigma(f,h)}\omega(W(h)).
\tag{4}
\]

When \(\omega(W(h))\neq0\), the ratio

\[
\frac{F_{f,h,\omega}(\lambda)}
     {F_{f,h,\omega}(0)}
=
e^{i\lambda\sigma(f,h)}
\tag{5}
\]

cancels the state-dependent baseline. If that baseline is zero, expectation
ratios do not expose the channel phase; one needs channel tomography or a
different calibrated readout. State independence at channel level does not
guarantee identifiability through every state-conditioned instrument.

## 3. Same causal response, different state

For a quasifree state,

\[

=
\exp\!\left[-\frac12\mu(h,h)\right],
\tag{6}
\]

where the symmetric covariance \(\mu\) satisfies the usual uncertainty

Choose two allowed covariances \(\mu_1\neq\mu_2\) on the same symplectic
space. They share:

- the same Weyl relations;
- the same \(\sigma(f,h)\);
- the same channel identity (1); and

But for some \(h\),

\[
\omega_{\mu_1}(W(h))
\neq

\]

Thus the causal commutator response does not select the vacuum, thermal
state, quasifree phase, symmetric correlations, or record carrier.

This claim is deliberately scoped. The **complete** unperturbed

covariance. The no-go applies to the displacement channel factor or causal
symplectic response by itself.

## 4. The bounded-unitary / bounded-generator correction

The initial constructive hope was that bounded Weyl operators might directly
close `HC-DU-101`'s continuum-field gap. They do not.

### Proposition 2 — Weyl displacement automorphisms are not norm-continuous

Assume \(f\) is not symplectically null and define

\[
\alpha_\lambda=\operatorname{Ad}W(\lambda f)^*.
\]

Then for every \(\lambda\neq0\),

\[
\|\alpha_\lambda-\operatorname{id}\|=2.
\tag{7}
\]

#### Proof

Choose \(h_0\) with \(\sigma(f,h_0)\neq0\), and rescale it to

\[
h
=
\frac{\pi}{\lambda\sigma(f,h_0)}h_0.
\]

Equation (1) gives

\[
\alpha_\lambda(W(h))=-W(h).
\]

Since \(\|W(h)\|=1\),

\[
\|\alpha_\lambda(W(h))-W(h)\|=2.
\]

Therefore \(\|\alpha_\lambda-\operatorname{id}\|\ge2\). The reverse
inequality holds for any two unital isometries, proving (7). \(\square\)

Consequences:

1. \(\lambda\mapsto\alpha_\lambda\) is not norm-continuous at zero.
2. It cannot have a bounded derivation as its norm generator.
3. In regular representations it may remain strongly continuous, with the
   smeared field as an unbounded generator.
4. The exact scalar dependence on each **fixed** \(W(h)\) remains analytic.

This is the correct handoff from `HC-DU-101`:

```text
bounded finite-time generator
    -> uniform factorial norm control on every bounded readout

Weyl displacement
    -> bounded unitary + unbounded generator
    -> no uniform norm-continuous channel family
    -> exact phase control only after fixing the readout label
```

The packet-specific identity is useful, but it is not a global
bounded-continuum theorem.

## 6. Conditional geometry theorem and its dependency

### Proposition 3 — full labeled causal response carries null geometry

Suppose the following are independently fixed:

1. the smooth event manifold or equivalent point/test-function identity;
2. the compact-support and localization relation;
3. the test-function pairing or distribution-density convention; and
4. the full bilinear response \((f,h)\mapsto\sigma_g(f,h)\).

Then (3) determines the causal-propagator kernel as a distribution. For a
normally hyperbolic operator on a smooth globally hyperbolic spacetime, the
wavefront relation of that kernel follows the null bicharacteristic flow.
Consequently the full response conditionally determines the null-geodesic
relation. Under standard distinguishing hypotheses, causal order determines
the topology, differentiable structure, and conformal metric.

This is a conditional Grade-3 reconstruction route. It is not a physical
record-selection theorem.

### What the proposition does not establish

- A bare abstract symplectic space does not identify which vectors are
  localized in which spacetime regions.
- A symplectic automorphism preserves all Weyl response relations while
  changing the coordinate interpretation of sources and readouts.
- If support labels were assigned using \(g\), returning \(g\)'s causal
  structure from them is representation, not record-first reconstruction.
- The wavefront/null relation by itself fixes only conformal structure.
  Amplitudes and lower-order terms may contain further information in a
  separately proved inverse theorem, but full metric scale is not earned
  here.
- The state, mass, curvature coupling, detector, observer boundary, and
  archive are not selected by the commutator.

An independently formed apparatus network could provide target-independent
source/readout identities. This result does not prove such a network
impossible. It identifies it as the load-bearing missing map.

## 9. Linear response is not the audited nonlinear inverse map

The Lorentzian inverse result previously imported into Dynamic Unity uses a
semilinear source-to-solution map. Higher-order source interactions create
new singularities that act like virtual point sources and reveal geometric
relations.

The free Weyl identity (1) is linear causal response. It does not supply:

- nonlinear wave interaction;
- higher Fréchet derivatives of a semilinear solution operator;
- virtual point-source generation; or
- the theorem's nonlinear geometric discriminator.

There are therefore two legitimate, differently typed paths:

1. **free causal-propagator path:** use a sufficiently rich, independently
   localized approximation to \(E_g\), then prove a finite stable
   causal/conformal inverse theorem for the chosen completion class;
2. **nonlinear source-to-solution path:** realize and finitely acquire enough
   of the semilinear response hierarchy to invoke the stronger inverse
   theorem.

The first is cheaper but currently lacks the finite stability/selection
result. The second has the stronger imported inverse theorem but retains the
unbounded-source, higher-instrument, and acquisition burdens.
