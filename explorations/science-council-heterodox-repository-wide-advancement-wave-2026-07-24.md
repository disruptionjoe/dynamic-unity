# Heterodox professor: repository-wide advancement wave

## Executive result

I did not select the threshold branch.

After comparing the full program, I selected the more foundational question
under `HC-DU-036`:

> When can a finite body of certified records justify the claim that no
> unrecorded causal power remains?

The answer is sharper than “records need to include history.” Even an exact,
authenticated, append-only record of every event and intervention actually
performed through an arbitrarily long but finite horizon can leave a causal
remainder completely hidden. For every finite horizon \(h\), I construct two
finite deterministic processes that:

1. return exactly the same certified transcript for **every** intervention
   sequence of length at most \(h\);
2. are first separated by one sequence of length \(h+1\);
3. return to the same endpoint after that separating sequence; and
4. require \(h+1\) behaviorally distinct memory states to hide the response
   for \(h\) steps.

This yields two complementary conclusions.

First, no fixed finite record horizon can establish unrestricted Certified
Causal Sufficiency against an unbounded rival class. A rival can always place
its physical remainder one intervention deeper.

Second, this is not a counsel of despair. If the rival process class has a
declared finite state or memory bound, product-state search terminates. It
either finds a separating intervention of bounded length or exhausts the
reachable comparison states and proves full input/output equivalence in the
declared deterministic class. Under equivalence, both systems factor through
one common minimal residual-behavior process.

The deeper heterodox correction is:

```text
certified actual history
    !=
certified counterfactual response structure.
```

A record-first theory therefore needs more than a complete archive of what
happened. It needs an independently selected **generating family of
interventions or tests**, together with a resource/model bound that says why
that family closes the relevant counterfactual possibilities. Defining a
“record” as the answer to every physically possible future experiment would
make sufficiency tautological. The viable target is a small, independently
fixed test basis whose closure can be proved.

The executable receipt passes `51/51` exact checks for horizons \(1\) through
\(8\), including exhaustive short-word agreement, shortest separating
interventions, equal endpoints, product bounds, minimal residual-state lower
bounds, a state-relabeling null, and quotient-congruence positive and negative
controls. This is a finite deterministic theorem baseline, not a new law of
physics.

## 2. Frozen typed contract

### 2.1 Process

A model is a finite deterministic Mealy process

\[
M=(S,A,Y,\delta,\lambda,s_0),
\]

where:

- \(S\) is a finite hidden process-state set;
- \(A\) is a frozen intervention alphabet;
- \(Y\) is a frozen exact record alphabet;
- \(\delta:S\times A\to S\) is the transition law;
- \(\lambda:S\times A\to Y\) is the record returned by an
  intervention; and
- \(s_0\) is the initial state.

For a word \(w=a_1\cdots a_k\), let \(\tau_M(w)\) be the complete sequence of
outputs generated by applying the interventions in that order.

Primitive precedence here is only the order of declared intervention slots.
No metric, duration, light cone, foliation, or physical spacetime order is
derived or assumed.

### 2.2 Record instrument and access

Each output symbol is treated as:

- exact;
- authenticated to the source process and intervention word;
- immediately and perfectly available to the observer;
- append-only and retained without error; and
- final in the weak, non-adversarial sense that the transcript is never
  revised.

This intentionally gives the record-first candidate every static advantage.
There is no corruption, loss, dissemination problem, threshold, or
readout uncertainty. If sufficiency still fails, it fails because actual
transcripts omit unperformed counterfactual responses.

### 2.3 Rival and adversary class

The rival class is pairs of finite deterministic Mealy processes with the
same \(A\) and \(Y\). No Byzantine adversary is present. Hidden internal
states and transition laws may differ.

The declared representation group is arbitrary relabeling of internal states
that preserves initial state, transitions, and outputs. An isomorphic
state-relabeling is the positive equivalence control.

### 2.4 Certificate and factorization target

Two initial processes are **operationally equivalent** when

\[
\tau_{M_0}(w)=\tau_{M_1}(w)
\quad\text{for every finite }w\in A^*.
\]

A separating certificate is one finite word \(w\) for which the transcripts
differ. Its complexity is its intervention length.

The factorization target is the minimal process of residual behaviors:
states are distinct maps from possible future intervention words to future
record transcripts. It identifies hidden states exactly when no future
admitted intervention can distinguish them.

### 2.5 Resource ledger

Charged:

- state count;
- intervention-word length;
- exact reset in the constructed read action;
- exhaustive product-state search; and
- the complete intervention alphabet.

Not physically priced:

- energy or time per intervention;
- implementation of memory states;
- authentication hardware;
- physical record formation;
- entropy and erasure cost;
- noise tolerance;
- quantum coherence; and
- spatial locality.

Those omissions prevent physical-law language.

### 2.6 Free choices

The delayed-remainder family uses the deliberately simple actions
`advance` and `read`. The read action resets the process. The response is
planted at one chosen delay horizon. These choices construct a counterexample;
they are not asserted to be selected by nature.

The invariant being tested is not the chosen syntax. It is:

> finite actual-transcript equality does not entail equality of the complete
> counterfactual response process without a rival-complexity bound.

## 3. Exact theorem package

### Theorem 1 — finite deterministic factorization or witness

Let \(M_0\) and \(M_1\) be finite deterministic Mealy processes with
\(n_0\) and \(n_1\) states, a common intervention alphabet, and a common output
alphabet. Exactly one of the following holds:

1. there is a separating intervention word of length at most \(n_0n_1\); or
2. their initial input/output behaviors agree for every finite intervention
   word.

In case 2, their reachable behaviors factor through the same minimal
residual-behavior process.

#### Proof

Search the synchronized product from \((s_0,t_0)\). At a reachable pair
\((s,t)\), inspect every action \(a\).

- If \(\lambda_0(s,a)\ne\lambda_1(t,a)\), the path used to reach
  \((s,t)\), followed by \(a\), is a separating word.
- Otherwise add
  \((\delta_0(s,a),\delta_1(t,a))\) to the search frontier if it has not
  already been visited.

A shortest path before the final separating action cannot repeat a product
state. There are at most \(n_0n_1\) product states, so a shortest separating
word has length at most \(n_0n_1\).

If the reachable product is exhausted without an output mismatch, induction
on word length shows that every future transcript agrees. Define the residual
behavior after a history \(w\) as the map sending a continuation \(v\) to the
output suffix generated by \(v\). Global transcript equality makes that
residual map common to both models after the same history. Quotienting
reachable states by equality of residual behavior gives one deterministic
minimal input/output process through which both reachable behaviors factor.
\(\square\)

This is an operational duality result, not an internal-state isomorphism.
Different hidden realizations can map to the same behavioral process.

### Theorem 2 — delayed physical-remainder family

For every integer \(h\ge1\), there are two deterministic processes with
\(h+1\) states each such that:

1. every intervention word of length at most \(h\) returns the same
   transcript in both processes;
2. the word
   \[
   w_h=\texttt{advance}^{h}\texttt{read}
   \]
   separates them;
3. \(w_h\) returns both processes to the same endpoint; and
4. the remainder process has \(h+1\) pairwise distinct residual behaviors, so
   no deterministic realization with fewer than \(h+1\) behavioral states can
   implement that exact delayed response.

#### Construction

Both processes have states \(0,1,\ldots,h\), beginning at \(0\).

```text
advance:
    i -> min(i+1,h), output 0

read:
    i -> 0
```

The control process outputs \(0\) on every read. The remainder process outputs
\(1\) on read exactly when the pre-read state is \(h\), and outputs \(0\)
otherwise.

#### Proof

A differing output requires a read preceded, since the previous read, by at
least \(h\) advances. That needs at least \(h+1\) actions. Hence every word of
length at most \(h\) agrees.

The declared \(w_h\) reaches state \(h\), returns \(0\) in the control and
\(1\) in the remainder process, then resets both to state \(0\). It therefore
separates equal endpoints by a history-conditioned record.

For states \(i<j\), apply

\[
\texttt{advance}^{\,h-j}\texttt{read}.
\]

Starting at \(j\), the read occurs at state \(h\) and returns \(1\). Starting
at \(i\), it occurs below \(h\) and returns \(0\). Every pair of states
therefore has different future behavior. By the residual-behavior
minimality criterion, all \(h+1\) states are necessary. \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.1 — no horizon-only sufficiency certificate

No fixed finite horizon \(h\) can certify unrestricted interventional
sufficiency against all finite deterministic rivals. The delayed family
places a remainder at horizon \(h+1\).

This closes only horizon-only certification. It does not say that finite
certification is impossible after the model/resource class is bounded.

### Corollary 2.2 — memory is epistemically load-bearing

The rival memory/state bound is not merely an implementation detail. It
determines whether equivalence is finitely certifiable and how deep a hidden
response can be.

The construction gives a linear lower bound:

\[
\text{hidden response depth }h
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{at least }h+1\text{ exact deterministic behavioral states}
\]

for this delayed-response contract.

### Theorem 3 — intervention-congruence criterion for a supplied record quotient

Let \(q:S\to Q\) be a supplied record quotient of one finite deterministic
process. A well-defined quotient process exists—meaning there are maps
\(\bar\delta\) and \(\bar\lambda\) satisfying

\[
q(\delta(s,a))=\bar\delta(q(s),a),
\qquad
\lambda(s,a)=\bar\lambda(q(s),a)
\]

for every state and intervention—if and only if, whenever \(q(s)=q(t)\),

\[
\lambda(s,a)=\lambda(t,a)
\quad\text{and}\quad
q(\delta(s,a))=q(\delta(t,a))
\]

for every \(a\in A\).

#### Proof

Necessity follows immediately by applying the quotient equations to two
states in one fiber. For sufficiency, define the quotient output and
transition using any representative of a fiber. The two fiber-constancy
conditions make both definitions independent of the representative.
\(\square\)

This is the structured-process correction to the finite Set quotient in
`HC-DU-033A`: a post-cut partition is not a causal-process quotient until it
is a congruence under every admitted intervention.

## 5. What this changes in Certified Causal Reality

### 5.1 The decisive object is a response process, not a realized archive

An archive records the branch taken. Causal capability concerns what the
system would do under interventions not taken. The latter is not generally a
function of any finite realized transcript.

The program should therefore distinguish:

```text
actual certified-history quotient
    !=
predictive state for a fixed policy
    !=
interventional response quotient
    !=
complete physical intervention grammar.
```

The finite theorem supports the third object relative to a frozen grammar and
resource bound. It does not derive the fourth.

### 5.2 Resource bounds are part of the epistemic theorem

The usual resource ledger is often read as a demand to account honestly for
implementation cost after a theorem is found. Here the state/memory bound is
logically prior: without it, no finite experiment closes the rival class.

That suggests a stronger program pattern:

```text
resource bound
    -> finite counterfactual closure radius
    -> factorization certificate or remainder witness.
```

This is more useful than searching for one universal objectivity threshold.
It tells us exactly why an experiment is complete within a class and exactly
how it can fail outside it.

### 5.3 “All possible experiments” would be a forbidden definition

One could force sufficiency by defining the certified record of a state to be
its full response to every possible future intervention. That would simply be
the operational behavior itself. It would violate the charter’s
no-sufficiency-tautology stop.

The nontrivial target is an independently justified finite generating set:

- a basis of control operations;
- a rank or memory bound;
- a closure theorem extending basis agreement to the admitted process class;
  and
- a held-out intervention that can expose basis failure.

This is where the next potentially publishable conjunction lives.

### 5.4 The ontology fork becomes operational

- **Record-first reconstruction** earns support only if a physically selected
  record/test basis is a congruence and closes the admitted response process.
- **Operational duality** occurs when distinct physical realizations share the
  same residual-behavior process under the complete admitted intervention
  family.
- **Physics-first remainder** is the shortest separating word together with
  the hidden resource that delayed or enabled it.

The delayed family is a toy physics-first remainder. It establishes the
logical shape, not a claim about nature.

### 1. Bounded Interventional Test-Basis Theorem

For a declared linear classical, quantum-comb, or process-tensor class of

### 2. Robust Record-Horizon/Memory Tradeoff

Any noisy process that hides an \(\epsilon\)-distinguishable response until
depth \(h+1\) while remaining \(\delta\)-indistinguishable through \(h\) must
carry a lower-bounded memory dimension, predictive-state rank, mutual
information, or thermodynamic support.

**Why it matters:** this is the physically promising extension of the exact
\(h+1\)-state lower bound.

**Falsifier:** a fixed-memory family with arbitrarily deep robust separation
under the same frozen grammar and error contract.

### 3. Intervention-Grammar Remainder Conjecture

If two realizations have identical certified transcripts for every commonly
admitted intervention but differ in which further interventions are
physically implementable, then the difference in intervention grammar is
itself a capability-relevant physical remainder unless that grammar can be
reconstructed from independently selected records.

**Why it matters:** the set of possible actions may be as fundamental as the
set of facts. Treating \(I\) as permanently primitive could limit a
record-first ontology even when response factorization succeeds.

### 4. Quantum Delayed-Remainder Witness

There exist two finite-memory quantum processes that agree on every
predeclared tester through \(h\) slots and have equal terminal states, yet are
separated by one coherent \((h+1)\)-slot tester; the minimum memory dimension
or process-tensor bond dimension controls the shortest witness.

**Why it matters:** this directly connects the finite theorem to coherent
proper-time histories and process-level certification.

**Risk:** standard comb tomography may already contain the core result.

### 5. Counterfactual Depth as Observer-Relative Temporal Scale

Define the distance between two certified process states as the minimum
intervention depth needed to separate their residual behaviors. Test whether
this operational depth reconstructs any stable temporal or causal metric
beyond primitive slot order.

**Why it matters:** it offers a heterodox bridge from capability to effective

**Risk:** it may be only an automata-testing metric with no physical covariance

## 12. Plain-English interpretation

Imagine two sealed machines. You can press buttons and keep a perfect,
tamper-proof log of every result. Suppose the machines agree on every button
sequence you can try in ten steps. That does **not** prove they are the same
kind of machine. One can be built to reveal its difference only on step
eleven, and after revealing it, both machines can return to exactly the same
visible state.

But hiding a difference for longer is not free. In the exact construction, a
machine that waits \(h\) steps needs at least \(h+1\) genuinely different
internal memory conditions. If we know how much memory or process complexity
the machine can have, we can search far enough to settle the question: either
find the experiment that exposes the difference or prove that no allowed
experiment can.

For Dynamic Unity, that means the deepest object is probably not “the pile of
records that already exists.” It is the compact structure that tells an
observer how the world would answer the interventions the observer can
actually perform. Records can reconstruct reality only when a small,
physically justified set of tests closes that response structure. If one
held-out intervention produces a different answer, the extra response is the
precise physical remainder the program has been looking for.
