# Antecedent-relative physical ARE selection

## Result in plain English

The swing found a clean answer to a question that had remained too vague:

> “Did physics select the record interface?” is incomplete until we say
> **which antecedent physical facts are being credited with the selection**.

A complete realization may contain a clock, detector, pointer, archive, and
observer-access path. That proves those things can physically exist together.
It does not prove that the field equation alone selected them. The same
interface can be:

- unselected by the symmetric law;
- selected only up to an orbit by structural criteria;
- point-selected after a matter state, boundary condition, or reference
  breaks the symmetry;
- physically formed only after an apparatus coupling is included; and
- accessible only after a decoder, route, resource budget, and future action
  contract are added.

The exact test is another fibre theorem.

Let \(\omega\) range over complete physical realizations. Let

\[
a(\omega)
\]

be everything admitted as the claimed antecedent—for example, law only,
law plus realized matter, or law plus apparatus—and let

\[
\xi(\omega)
\]

be the physically formed Admissible Record Envelope or interface
architecture in that realization.

The antecedent selects \(\xi\) exactly when

\[
\ker a\subseteq\ker\xi.
\]

Equivalently, any two complete realizations with the same antecedent must
form the same interface object. If they do not, there is no selector at that
explanatory level.

When point selection fails, the smallest honest output is not an arbitrary
guess. It is the set

\[
\Sigma_a(\alpha)
=
\{\xi(\omega):a(\omega)=\alpha\}.
\]

That set is the minimal sound selector envelope allowed by the antecedent.
It distinguishes four outcomes:

1. one selected interface or ARE;
2. one symmetry orbit with no selected member;
3. several genuinely plural possibilities; or
4. no nontrivial admissible interface.

Symmetry is therefore a powerful witness, not the definition of
nonselection. If a transformation preserves all antecedent facts but moves
the interface, point selection is impossible. Adding a physical
symmetry-breaking datum repairs selection only if it distinguishes every
remaining interface-different pair. In a finite exact problem with \(n\)
possible interface classes, any classical repair must carry at least \(n\)
distinguishable values; a fixed-length binary label therefore needs at least
\(\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) bits.

The result also separates two claims that were previously easy to conflate:

- physics may select the entire ARE—the menu and compatibility complex of
  physically possible interfaces—while selecting no member; and
- several selected alternatives do not become one joint record unless they
  are a simplex of the physical joint-realizability complex.

The controls give a coherent ladder:

- a supplied finite action algebra structurally selects its center as the
  finest internal nondisturbing sharp value, but not its pointer or archive;
- Minkowski law selects the full covariant orbit of time directions, not one
  observer direction;
- the material `Z3` gauge criteria select the two-member
  controlled-add/subtract formation orbit, while a material orientation
  selects one representative;
- a matter current selects a rest-frame direction but not a clock placement,
  archive, or access route;
- a supplied stabilizer-check process selects the complete syndrome class
  relative to that process, not from generic quantum dynamics; and
- a local AQFT factor admits no nontrivial exact internal nondisturbing sharp
  record at all.

So `CCR-N5-S1` does not earn a universal physical selector. It earns a
scoped selector-attribution theorem and a reusable passport that the
Einstein--matter and AQFT swings can now instantiate without changing the
question.

## 1. Why “selected” needed another type

The existing Admissible Record Envelope is defined for a frozen complete
contract

\[
\mathcal C=(M,G,P,O,A,B,F).
\]

It identifies:

- which record interfaces are physically admissible;
- how they refine one another;
- which finite families are jointly realizable; and
- which maximal or greatest records reconstruct a held-out target.

That is already sufficient to adjudicate a frozen envelope. It does not say
which earlier physical description determines \(\mathcal C\) or its ARE.

Three different questions had been sharing the word “selection”:

1. **Envelope selection:** what determines the whole admissibility poset and
   compatibility complex?
2. **Interface selection:** what determines which maximal/greatest interface
   is physically realized?
3. **Outcome actualization:** what determines one record value in a formed
   interface?

This swing addresses the first two. It does not supply an outcome or collapse
law.

Selection is therefore relative to an **explanatory cut**:

```text
law
    -> realized state and boundary
    -> apparatus and coupling
    -> formed pointer/archive and provenance
    -> observer access, decoder, actions and resources
    -> held-out reconstruction target
```

A result may close one arrow while leaving every later arrow open.

## 4. Theorem 1 — Antecedent–Selector Factorization

Let \(a:\bar\Omega\to\mathcal A\) and
\(\xi:\bar\Omega\to\mathcal X\) be maps of sets, where \(\mathcal X\) may be
the ARE isomorphism class, one interface component, or the full tagged
interface architecture.

For any set map \(f\), write

\[
\ker f
=
\{(\omega_1,\omega_2):f(\omega_1)=f(\omega_2)\}
\]

for its kernel equivalence relation.

There exists a selector

\[
s:\operatorname{im}a\longrightarrow\mathcal X
\]

such that

\[
\xi=s\circ a
\]

if and only if

\[
\ker a\subseteq\ker\xi.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
a(\omega_1)=a(\omega_2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\xi(\omega_1)=\xi(\omega_2).
\]

### Proof

If \(\xi=s\circ a\), equality of antecedent values immediately implies
equality of selector outputs.

Conversely, assume \(\xi\) is constant on every \(a\)-fibre. Define

\[
s(\alpha)=\xi(\omega)
\]

for any \(\omega\) with \(a(\omega)=\alpha\). Fibre constancy makes the
definition independent of representative, and \(\xi=s\circ a\).
\(\square\)

### Two separately graded consequences

1. **Envelope selection**

   \[
   \ker a\subseteq\ker e.
   \]

   The antecedent fixes the admissibility/refinement/compatibility object.

2. **Point or architecture selection**

   \[
   \ker a\subseteq\ker(e,k).
   \]

   The antecedent also fixes the realized maximal/greatest interface
   architecture.

The first does not imply the second. A covariant law may select a full orbit
or net of interfaces without selecting an observer-indexed member.

## 5. Theorem 2 — Minimal Sound Set-Valued Selector

For each realizable antecedent value \(\alpha\), define

\[
\Sigma_a^\xi(\alpha)
=
\{\xi(\omega):a(\omega)=\alpha\}.
\]

Then:

1. \(\Sigma_a^\xi\) is sound: the realized interface output always lies in
   the returned set;
2. it is the unique smallest sound set-valued predictor: every sound
   \(T(\alpha)\) must contain \(\Sigma_a^\xi(\alpha)\);
3. point selection holds at \(\alpha\) exactly when
   \(|\Sigma_a^\xi(\alpha)|=1\); and
4. if \(a'\) refines \(a\), meaning \(a=\pi\circ a'\), then

   \[
   \Sigma_{a'}^\xi(a'(\omega))
   \subseteq
   \Sigma_a^\xi(a(\omega)).
   \]

### Proof

Soundness follows from membership of \(\xi(\omega)\) in the image of its own
fibre. Any sound predictor must contain the interface output of every
realization in that fibre, and therefore contains the whole image.

For refinement, every \(a'\)-fibre lies inside the corresponding \(a\)-fibre,
so its image under \(\xi\) is a subset. \(\square\)

### Selector multiplicity

For finite controls define

\[
N_a^\xi(\alpha)
=
\left|\Sigma_a^\xi(\alpha)\right|.
\]

This is an uncertainty count for one frozen selector problem, not a universal
physical scalar. Additional antecedent structure can only weakly decrease it.
Changing the interface equivalence, process, or completion class retypes the
count.

## 6. Theorem 3 — Symmetry-Orbit Obstruction

Let a group \(H\) act on \(\bar\Omega\) and \(\mathcal X\). Suppose

\[
a(h\omega)=a(\omega)
\]

for every \(h\in H\), so the transformation preserves all claimed
antecedent facts, and suppose the realized interface is equivariant:

\[
\xi(h\omega)=h\xi(\omega).
\]

Then

\[
H\cdot\xi(\omega)
\subseteq
\Sigma_a^\xi(a(\omega)).
\]

Consequently:

1. a point selector at \(a(\omega)\) requires

   \[
   \xi(\omega)\in\mathcal X^H;
   \]

2. if the orbit has more than one element, the antecedent cannot point-select
   \(\xi\); and
3. the orbit class \([\xi]\in\mathcal X/H\) may still be selected even when
   no member is.

### Proof

For every \(h\), the transformed realization \(h\omega\) lies in the same
\(a\)-fibre. Its interface output is \(h\xi(\omega)\), so the full orbit lies
in the fibre image. A singleton image is possible only when every orbit
element equals \(\xi(\omega)\). \(\square\)

This recovers `HC-DU-033C` as a special case. Its new role is diagnostic:
symmetry supplies a cheap same-antecedent/different-interface witness, while
Theorem 1 also catches nonselection caused by topology, boundary
underdetermination, alternative apparatuses, resource choices, or other
nonsymmetry differences.

## 7. Theorem 4 — Minimum Additional Premise Bound

Suppose an extra physical datum

\[
b:\bar\Omega\longrightarrow\mathcal B
\]

repairs exact selection, so that

\[
\xi=f(a,b)
\]

for some \(f\).

Fix \(\alpha\in\operatorname{im}a\). If

\[
n=
\left|\Sigma_a^\xi(\alpha)\right|
<\infty,
\]

then

\[
\left|b\!\left(a^{-1}(\alpha)\right)\right|
\geq n.
\]

Therefore any exact classical accessible label sufficient to select among
the \(n\) interface classes requires at least \(n\) distinguishable values.
A fixed-length binary encoding therefore requires at least

\[
\left\lceil\log_2 n\right\rceil
\]

bits.

### Proof

If two realizations in the fixed \(a\)-fibre have the same value of \(b\),
then they have the same pair \((a,b)\) and hence the same value of
\(\xi=f(a,b)\). Therefore every distinct interface output in the fibre
requires a distinct \(b\)-value. \(\square\)

### Qualifications

- This is an exact finite classical discrimination bound, not a thermodynamic
  work law.
- A quantum reference resource is not replaced by a classical bit count.
  Symmetric quantum processing may require orthogonal orbit states or other
  asymmetry resources for exact implementation.
- A datum earns repair credit only if it is physically formed and
  target-independent. A coordinate label, fitted decoder, or choice made
  after inspecting the held-out target does not qualify.

## 8. Compatibility corollary — plurality is not a joint record

Suppose the selector output contains several interface vertices

\[
K=\{R_1,\ldots,R_m\}
\]

inside one ARE.

The set-valued selector establishes only that one of those interfaces may be
realized or that the antecedent leaves them jointly possible. It establishes
a formed joint record only if

\[
K\in\mathcal J_{\mathcal C}.
\]

If \(K\notin\mathcal J_{\mathcal C}\), the tuple

\[
(r_{R_1},\ldots,r_{R_m})
\]

is a formal product outside the physical contract. Calling it one selected
interface changes the process, observer boundary, or resource budget.

Thus:

```text
set-valued selection
    != simultaneous formation
    != joint access
    != a greatest record.
```

## 9. Naturality without naturality inflation

Factorization is evaluated on representation-invariant objects. For every
declared physical isomorphism \(u:\mathcal C\to\mathcal C'\), the selector
must commute:

\[
u_*\Sigma_{\mathcal C}
=
\Sigma_{\mathcal C'}.
\]

For a dynamically irrelevant extension with a proved forgetful map, adding
the extension must not change the selector after forgetting it.

The domain of naturality is load-bearing. It must include:

- gauge and coordinate relabeling;
- lossless record relabeling;
- inert subdivision;
- equivalent apparatus descriptions; and
- any environment extension proved irrelevant to formation, access,
  resources, and future actions.

It must not silently identify:

- different observers;
- distinct physical reference frames;
- different source or boundary data;
- new apparatuses or access routes;
- enlarged action/resource envelopes; or
- alternative acquisition strata.

Locally covariant QFT supplies a caution: naturality across all spacetimes can
be strong enough to produce no-go results for natural states in broad
theory classes. Dynamic Unity therefore demands naturality across the
declared benign equivalences of the selector problem, not an untyped demand
that one state, observer, or interface be canonical in every physical world.
