### Proposition 1 — candidate action is not lawful completion

A functional \(S\) and partial derivative expressions do not define
\(\operatorname{Sol}(S)\) until the full Euler operator, admissible domain,
boundary/junction conditions, and a nonempty solution class are fixed.

This is not a new variational theorem. It is the ordinary well-posedness
boundary applied to the cross-repository handoff.

### Proposition 2 — lawful dynamics is not source response

Even a nonempty solution class does not determine

\[
\mathcal R=C_{\rm out}\,G\,B_{\rm src}
\tag{8}
\]

without a background/linearization, Green prescription, source insertion,
and readout. Different choices can produce different response equivalence
classes while preserving the same action.

This is the point at which `HC-DU-126/127` can first become relevant. Those
results characterize completeness and probe minimality for a supplied finite
linear response object; they do not construct Equation (8) or automatically
transfer to a GU continuum/nonlinear class.

### Proposition 3 — response is not formed material record

A response law does not determine a sampler, realized outcome, material
carrier, archive policy, provenance relation, retention, observer access, or
reset semantics.

The exact finite control fixes one action on
\(\Omega=\{0,1\}\) and compares

\[
r_0(\omega)=0,
\qquad
r_1(\omega)=\omega.
\tag{9}
\]

The action is identical while the record fibres are different. A second
control stores one identical response symbol in carrier \(A\) or carrier
\(B\), preserving the entire operational response while changing material
location and provenance.

These are logical/type counterexamples, not physical models of GU. Their
purpose is only to block an invalid inference from an action formula or
response law to a selected record interface.
