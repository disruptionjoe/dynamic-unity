# Einstein--matter clock, observer, archive, and access selection ladder

## Result in plain English

Ordinary Einstein--matter theory gets farther toward a record architecture
than bare spacetime geometry, but it stops at a precise boundary.

Matter can select a local rest frame. If its stress tensor has one unique
timelike direction, that direction is not an analyst's arbitrary choice. It
is physically encoded in the realized matter. Its integral curves form a
matter congruence, and proper time accumulates along them.

Under stronger conditions—zero vorticity, geodesic flow, and no global
topological obstruction—the congruence also supplies a common family of
orthogonal time slices and a proper-time scalar up to an additive origin.
That is real physical clock structure.

It is still not a record system.

The same Einstein--matter state can contain:

- no distinguished member of the congruence;
- many possible material clocks on symmetry-related worldtubes;
- arbitrary initial data transported by a putative archive field;
- many causally permitted communication routes;
- different pointers, decoders, authentication schemes, reset rules, and
  resource budgets; and
- different physically admissible record envelopes.

Those differences can survive while the metric, stress tensor, matter flow,
and boundary data remain fixed. Therefore generic Einstein--matter dynamics
does not point-select an observer, detector, archive, access route, or
Admissible Record Envelope.


## 2. Matter-eigenflow selection theorem

Use signature \((-+++)\). A perfect-fluid stress tensor is

\[
T_{ab}
=(\rho+p)u_a u_b+p\,g_{ab},
\qquad
u^a u_a=-1.
\]

Then

\[
T^a{}_b u^b=-\rho\,u^a,
\]

while every vector \(v\) orthogonal to \(u\) obeys

\[
T^a{}_b v^b=p\,v^a.
\]

### Theorem 1 — simple timelike eigendirection selects matter flow

If:

1. \(T^a{}_b\) has one simple timelike eigenline;
2. the spacetime is time-oriented; and
3. a future-pointing normalization is required,

then the realized stress tensor point-selects one future unit timelike field
\(u\) wherever that eigendirection remains simple.

For a perfect fluid, \(\rho+p\neq0\) makes the timelike eigenvalue
\(-\rho\) distinct from the spatial eigenvalue \(p\). The future
time orientation removes the residual sign.

### Proof

The simple timelike eigenspace is one-dimensional. Normalization leaves
\(\{u,-u\}\), and the future orientation selects \(u\). No observer
worldline or material label has been selected; only the field and its
unlabelled integral-curve congruence have.

### Exact limits

The theorem does not say every stress tensor selects such a flow.

- A pure vacuum-energy form \(T_{ab}=-\rho g_{ab}\) has no distinguished
  timelike eigendirection.
- Null matter can select a null direction but no material rest frame.
- Degenerate or multi-component matter can leave several candidate frames.
- Without time orientation, the best return is the \(\mathbb Z_2\) orbit
  \(\{u,-u\}\).

Thus \(A_0\) supplies a source class, not a realized flow. \(A_1\) can select
the flow only in the declared simple-eigenline subclass.

## 3. Proper-time and synchronization theorem

Let \(u\) be a future unit timelike flow and let \(T\) be a scalar satisfying

\[
u(T)=u^a\nabla_aT=1.
\]

This equation says that \(T\) advances at proper-time rate along every flow
line. It does not synchronize the lines.

### Lemma 2 — along-flow clocks have independent origins

If \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) both satisfy \(u(T)=1\), then

\[
c=T_2-T_1
\]

satisfies

\[
u(c)=0.
\]

Conversely, \(T+c\) is another solution for every first integral
\(c\) of the flow.

Therefore proper-time accumulation along a congruence leaves one arbitrary
constant on every flow line unless an additional synchronization or anchor
condition is supplied.

### Congruence decomposition

Write

\[
\nabla_a u_b
=
\frac13\theta h_{ab}
+\sigma_{ab}
+\omega_{ab}
-u_a a_b,
\]

where \(h_{ab}=g_{ab}+u_a u_b\), \(a_b=u^c\nabla_cu_b\), and
\(\omega_{ab}\) is projected vorticity. The exterior derivative of
\(u^\flat=u_a dx^a\) is controlled by vorticity and acceleration.

If

\[
a_b=0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\omega_{ab}=0,
\]

then

\[
d u^\flat=0.
\]

### Theorem 3 — conditional proper-time descent

On a connected simply connected domain \(U\), a geodesic,
vorticity-free unit timelike flow has a scalar \(\tau\) such that

\[
u^\flat=-d\tau,
\qquad
u(\tau)=1.
\]

The scalar is unique up to one additive constant on \(U\). A physical
initial hypersurface anchor, such as \(\tau=0\) on one orthogonal slice,
fixes that origin.

### Proof

Closedness of \(u^\flat\) and \(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(U)=0\) imply
\(u^\flat=-d\tau\). Since \(u\) is unit timelike,

\[
u(\tau)=-u^a u_a=1.
\]

If \(\tau'\) has the same differential, then
\(d(\tau'-\tau)=0\), so connectedness makes the difference constant.

### Obstructions

The result separates three increasingly strong structures:

1. **proper time on each worldline:** always available for a timelike
   worldline;
2. **a hypersurface-orthogonal time parameter:** requires the Frobenius
   condition, equivalently zero vorticity for the congruence; and
3. **a global unit-lapse proper-time scalar:** additionally needs the
   geodesic/closedness and topology conditions used above.

Nonzero vorticity obstructs orthogonal synchronization. Closed but
nonexact \(u^\flat\) can also obstruct a global scalar when
\(H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(U)\neq0\). A clock origin remains an affine
\(\mathbb R\)-orbit until a physical anchor is included.

The selected object at \(A_1\) is therefore at most a proper-time and
synchronization **class**. It is not an oscillator, detector, pointer, or
archive.
