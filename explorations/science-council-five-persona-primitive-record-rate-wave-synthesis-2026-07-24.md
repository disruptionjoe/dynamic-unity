## Executive decision

The wave treated both of Joe's proposed premises as live conditional
architectures:

- **Branch L:** one universal local record-accretion rule, without a shared
  synchronization; and
- **Branch G:** one actual fundamental global update clock, split into
  `G-full`, `G-gated`, and potentially hidden or gauge versions.

The first decisive result is an underdetermination, not a rejection:

> **One observer's complete send-return echo can be exactly identical under a
> reciprocal local-clock model and a preferred global-clock model.**

The distinction appears only when the reflected message carries the remote
observer's own record, when the observers reunite, or when a moving laboratory
runs a closed-loop preferred-frame test. Those additions separate three
levels that had been running together:

```text
D0  distance-dependent propagation offset
D1  Doppler / simultaneity / reciprocal boost kinematics
D2  delay-free path-dependent elapsed records at reunion.
```

The resulting dispositions are:

1. **`G-full` delay-only is overturned as a model of relativistic elapsed
   time.** If every observer appends exactly one equivalent physical record on
   every global tick, reunited observers have equal counts. Message latency
   cannot alter records stored between shared endpoints.
2. **Branch L has a serious conditional route.** Positive rescalings of
   `1+1` null coordinates preserve causal order; a common spacetime-event
   density removes the conformal factor and yields the Lorentz boost algebra.
   That construction still needs coordinated observer chains, manifoldlike
   order, a density calibration, and a bridge from bulk event number to
   local durable records.
3. **The broader global-clock class remains live.** A global tick can be an
   update opportunity rather than a physical record. A velocity/path/field
   gate plus a universal material response can reproduce the finite reunion
   and moving-light-clock targets. Those laws are the explanation burden;
   cadence and distance alone do not select them.
4. **There is a concrete positive antecedent.** Arrighi, Facchini, and Forets
   prove exact discrete Lorentz covariance for their particular `1+1` Clock
   quantum walk and Clock QCA. The open question is no longer whether every
   globally updated discrete theory must fail. It is whether that exact
   covariance reaches an observer's durable, comparable record count rather
   than only the circuit and its construction-specific encodings.
5. **A hidden clock is not automatically gauge.** Forgetting scheduler labels
   can make multiple global layerings observationally nonidentified. Calling
   the clock gauge requires invariance or a quotient across every admitted
   intervention, not merely failure to see it in one echo.
6. **An unrestricted gated clock is empirically too flexible.** Every finite
   acyclic local-record trace has a topological ordering and can be compiled
   into global update opportunities plus event-specific gates. Finite records
   therefore cannot identify L against arbitrary `G-gated` or `G-hidden`.
   Branch G becomes explanatory only when one fixed gate law predicts
   held-out observations.

The best bounded next move is therefore a **record-clock arena**: freeze
physical record semantics, reproduce the exact Clock-QCA positive control,
and test its carried reflection record, reunion count, boost covariance, and
scheduler visibility against the Branch-L, `G-full`, and fitted `G-gated`
controls. This is a parallel, cheap foundational route. It does not erase the
separate causal-post generativity/geometry program.

No physics claim is banked or seeded.

## What the mathematics now says

### 1. One echo does not identify the clock architecture

For the exact `beta=3/5`, `gamma=5/4`, `k=2` fixture, observer A records:

```text
send_A = 60
return_A = 240
radar_time_A = 150
radar_distance_A = 90.
```

The reciprocal local-clock benchmark has B reflect at record `120`, giving
split factors `(2,2)`. `G-full` has B reflect at global record `150`, giving
split factors `(5/2,8/5)`. Both give the same A-only product `4` and the same
complete A transcript.

Exact travel-time correction makes the `G-full` reflection record equal A's
radar time, leaving no residual remote clock difference. The local benchmark's
remote record retains the declared `gamma` relation. A returned B record
therefore changes an underidentified echo into a discriminating observation.

Resynchronizing the reflection changes the one-way speeds while preserving
the round trip. Defining radar distance from the same round trip makes `c=1`
an identity in those units; a dimensional speed still needs independent
distance and tick calibration.

### 2. Reunion is the clean delay-only discriminator

Let A and B share departure and reunion events, with `100` global update
opportunities and a two-leg target at `|beta|=3/5`:

| Architecture | A records | B records | What does the work |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `G-full` | `100` | `100` | one equivalent record per observer per `K` |
| L plus declared SR proper-time map | `100` | `80` | path-dependent proper time, conditionally mapped to records |
| `G-gated` | `100` | `80` | added admission factor `1/gamma=4/5` |

There is no propagation delay at either common endpoint. `G-full` cannot
produce unequal stored counts without changing its record rule. The failure is
exactly scoped: it does not kill a global substrate clock whose updates are
only opportunities or whose physical observables quotient the scheduler.

### 3. Local order plus the right number can recover more than cadence alone

In `1+1` null coordinates, a positive linear causal-axis map has:

```text
u' = a u
v' = b v
a,b > 0.
```

Causal order alone permits every positive `a,b` and therefore leaves the
interval scale free:

```text
s'^2 = a b s^2.
```

Requiring one common spacetime-event density fixes `ab=1`. Writing
`a=k`, `b=1/k` then gives:

```text
gamma = (k+k^-1)/2
beta  = (k-k^-1)/(k+k^-1)
s'^2  = s^2.
```

This is a real conditional construction of the `1+1` Lorentz algebra. It is
not a derivation from bare record cadence. The open commuting diagram is:

```text
local durable observer records
        -> observer-chain interval
        <-> causal order + bulk event number.
```

The number-volume map uses spacetime events in a causal region. A local
observer's one-dimensional memory count is not automatically that number.

### 4. A global clock can survive only by becoming more than delay

The simplest globally clocked substrate exposes absolute motion in a two-way
moving-light-clock fixture. At `beta=3/5`, clock gating alone or rod
contraction alone does not remove the signal. Applying both:

```text

moving length     = rest length sqrt(1-beta^2)
```

restores the selected finite local round-trip count and reunion target.
This is a constructive Lorentz-ether-style completion. The global clock does
not select the quadratic gate: alternative update-budget norms produce
different rates, and a universal response of matter must be generated rather
than fitted separately.

The Clock QW/QCA antecedent makes this a buildable rather than purely verbal
question. Its exact encoded covariance is positive evidence for a special
global-update architecture, while the observer-record and higher-dimensional
maps remain open.

### 5. Nonidentification, gauge, and physical hidden structure are different

A finite causal message graph can admit many global linear extensions after
its scheduler labels are forgotten. This proves that the local transcript
does not identify one layering. It does not prove:

- that no global scheduler generated the transcript;
- that every scheduler produces the same resource use or record history; or
- that the scheduler is gauge under all possible interventions.

A genuine gauge disposition needs an explicit quotient, bisimulation, or
all-observable invariance result. If a closed-loop, boost, field, defect, or
resource intervention distinguishes two layerings, the global structure is
physical even when an ordinary echo hides it.

There is also a general finite compilation result. Every finite causal-record
DAG has a topological ordering. Assign its events successive `K` values, offer
every observer an opportunity at each `K`, and gate in only the records
attached to that event. The resulting `G-gated` model reproduces the original
local trace exactly. Consequently:

```text
finite L trace
    -> always embeddable in some G-gated schedule
    -> no ontology identification without a constrained gate law.
```

Even a reunion endpoint is underdetermining within the gated class. In the
twenty-tick, sixteen-record fixture, `binom(20,4)=4,845` different four-skip
patterns have the same endpoint count, while early, periodic, and late
patterns differ at the midpoint. Held-out intermediate records and one fixed
dynamics are therefore essential.

On a diamond causal order, two global linear extensions have the same local
record projections. A scheduler-insensitive next-event kernel factors through
the causal quotient; a kernel that changes from `0.25` to `0.75` when the
spacelike order is exchanged makes the hidden scheduler physical. This is the
operational difference between gauge and merely unobserved structure.
