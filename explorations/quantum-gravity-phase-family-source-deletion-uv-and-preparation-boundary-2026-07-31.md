# Quantum-gravity phase-family source-deletion, ultraviolet, and preparation boundary

## Executive return

The Chen--Giacomini phase proposal is not one prediction object. It contains
two differently typed phase families:

1. the wide-source term depends on two source energy-density profiles and so
   remains a serious source-dependent response candidate; but the source does
   not yet supply a physical preparation map from controllable apparatus
   settings to the nonclassical profile and then to one finite held-out joint
   outcome; and
2. the displayed nested-commutator terms depend on the probe stress but not on
   the source state, mass, or profile. They survive deletion of the source and
   therefore cannot by themselves certify source--probe mediation.

For a Gaussian-smeared rank-one probe stress, the commutator phase scales as
the inverse cube of the probe width. Its point-probe limit diverges, and the
same formal perturbative order contains the probe's omitted gravitational
self-response. A finite physical prediction therefore requires a selected
finite body profile or a renormalized composite stress operator, plus a
conserved probe/controller completion.

This is a boundary result, not a refutation of the papers. The wide-source
branch retains the umbrella candidate's conditional high ceiling. The
commutator branch remains potentially interesting as a quantized-field/probe
self-response, but it is not presently a source-pinned mediator lock.

## Proposition 1: source deletion scopes the claim

**Proposition.** For the displayed Eqs. (37)--(38), deleting the source while
retaining the free gravitational field and its probe coupling leaves
\(\Theta_1+\Theta_2\) unchanged. Consequently the term cancels from a pure
source-branch phase difference at fixed probe, while it may still distinguish
two probe-stress branches.

**Proof.** Both functionals contain only \(\kappa\), duration, and
\(T_P^{ij}\). The source state and source stress are absent. Holding the probe
branch fixed therefore gives the same value before and after source deletion.
Subtracting two source branches at the same probe cancels the common term;
changing the probe stress need not. QED.

**Earned interpretation.** The term may diagnose a gravitational-field/probe
self-response or a quantized-field correction. It does not, without an
additional source-dependent term and complete comparison, certify that the
delocalized source mediated the observed distinction.

## Proposition 2: the displayed point-probe phase is profile-sensitive

Take the normalized momentum-space fixture

\[
T_P^{ij}(k)=Q^{ij}e^{-\sigma^2|k|^2/2},
\qquad
Q^{ij}=p^ip^j.
\]

Then

\[
I_1
=
\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
T_P^{ij}(k)T^P_{ij}(-k)
=
\frac{p^4}{8\pi^{3/2}\sigma^3}.
\]

For an isotropic distribution of momentum directions, the squared
transverse-traceless projection of a rank-one tensor has angular mean

\[
\frac{I_{TT}}{I_1}
=
\frac12\left(1-\frac23+\frac15\right)
=
\frac4{15}.
\]

Thus, for the paper's unit coefficient convention,

\[
\Theta_1+\Theta_2
=
\frac{61\,\kappa t^3p^4}
{2880\pi^{3/2}\sigma^3}.
\]

Halving \(\sigma\) multiplies the result by eight. The point-probe limit
\(\sigma\to0\) diverges. Two probes with equal integrated rank-one stress but
different widths therefore give different displayed phases.

This exact fixture is not an apparatus model. It proves the conditioning
boundary: a finite target requires a physically selected form factor/body
profile or a stated regulator and renormalization contract.

## Same-order completion duty

If the probe stress is \(T_P\), its generated metric perturbation has order

\[

\]

Its gravitational self-coupling is therefore

\[

the same formal order as Eqs. (37)--(38). The 2025 calculation omits probe
backreaction while retaining this quadratic probe-stress phase. A finite
