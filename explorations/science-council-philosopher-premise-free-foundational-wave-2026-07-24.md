# Philosopher of science: premise-free foundational wave

## Executive result

The current record-clock program asks whether the exactly covariant Clock QCA
has a “representation-independent durable-record functional.” That phrase
contains two different requirements:

1. a record relation must survive physically admissible changes of
   representation; and
2. bare dynamics must select one literal scalar predicate that is unchanged
   under every possible state relabeling.

The first is coherent. The second can be impossible except trivially.

Let a finite bare theory have state space \(X\) and identity dynamics. Every
binary predicate \(r:X\rightarrow\{0,1\}\) is perfectly durable. Every such
predicate also has a reversible classical copy channel

\[
C_r(x,b)=(x,b\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}r(x)).
\]

But identity dynamics commutes with every permutation of \(X\). Its
automorphism group is therefore the full symmetric group. Since that group is
transitive, a scalar predicate invariant under every automorphism must be
constant. Thus:

> **Bare dynamics, durability, and classical copyability do not canonically
> select a nontrivial record predicate.**

The exact probe makes the gap finite:

| Fixture | Predicates | Durable | Classically copyable | Full-group invariant | Nontrivial invariant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| two-bit identity dynamics | 16 | 16 | 16 | 2 constants | 0 |
| held-out three-bit identity dynamics | 256 | 256 | 256 | 2 constants | 0 |

A first-coordinate predicate and a parity predicate are explicitly conjugate
under a bijection while identity dynamics remains unchanged. Declaring the
first-coordinate readout as a physical interface reduces the admissible group:
the surviving predicates are then exactly the four-element Boolean algebra
generated by that readout. A nonidentity two-cycle holdout shows that
nontrivial durable predicates can still be nonidentified; an asymmetric
fixed-point-plus-three-cycle control shows that asymmetric dynamics can
support a nontrivial invariant partition. The result is scoped, not a
universal denial of physical records.

The required correction is:

> **“Representation independent” should mean a natural or equivariant
> commuting relation relative to a declared physical interface and admitted
> encoding category—not literal coordinate invariance under every
> bijection.**

This changes `HC-DU-031A` before more internal Clock-QCA variables are mined.
The target is now a record **channel/algebra**, transported across the actual
Clock-QCA encodings, with its interface, codomain, copy rule, durability
horizon, and intervention access exposed. The causal-post program remains
parallel. The recursive-viability passport may help test whether an interface
has physical intervention support, but cannot supply that support by naming
it.

No observer, irreversible memory, proper time, metric, physical unit,
Clock-QCA record channel, or DU law is derived. Nothing is banked or seeded.

## 5. Exact theorem and bounded swing

### 5.1 Theorem

**Bare-dynamics record-extractor triviality.** Let \(X\) be finite with
\(|X|>1\), and let \(F=\mathrm{id}_X\).

1. Every binary predicate is durable.
2. Every binary predicate has a reversible classical copy channel \(C_r\).
3. \(\mathrm{Aut}(F)=\mathrm{Sym}(X)\).
4. Every scalar predicate invariant under \(\mathrm{Aut}(F)\) is constant.

Therefore durability, classical copyability, and the bare dynamics do not
canonically select a nontrivial record predicate.

**Proof.** Items 1 and 2 follow directly from \(F(x)=x\) and
\(C_r^2=\mathrm{id}\), with \(C_r(x,0)=(x,r(x))\). Identity commutes with
every permutation, proving item 3. For any \(x,y\in X\), the symmetric group
contains a permutation taking \(x\) to \(y\). Invariance gives
\(r(x)=r(y)\), so \(r\) is constant. ∎

This is enough to defeat any universal procedure that claims to extract a
nontrivial literal scalar record from bare dynamics alone: it fails on the
identity member of its declared domain.

### 5.2 Covariance is not canonical selection

On the two-bit space, let:

```text
r_coordinate = first bit
r_parity     = parity of both bits.
```

The probe gives an explicit bijection carrying the coordinate support
`{2,3}` to the parity support `{1,2}`. Conjugating identity dynamics by the
same bijection leaves it identity. Transporting the predicate makes the
record relation exactly covariant. Nothing in the bare dynamics chooses
coordinate over parity.

This corrects, rather than rejects, representation independence:

- **bad target:** one internal coordinate unchanged under every recoding;
- **good target:** a channel/algebra transported consistently under the
  physically admitted recodings.

### 5.3 Interface-relative positive control

Declare the first bit to be an accessible labelled readout. Restrict
automorphisms to permutations that preserve each readout block.

| Case | Full group order | Interface-preserving group order | Invariant predicate algebra |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| two bits | 24 | 4 | `0`, first bit, complement, `1` |
| three bits | 40,320 | 576 | `0`, first bit, complement, `1` |

Parity is not invariant under either interface group. The interface therefore
selects a nontrivial algebra. But the readout is an input with a provenance
burden; the calculation does not pretend to derive it from persistence.

### 5.4 Nonidentity scope controls

- Two identical flip cycles have four durable predicates, including two
  nontrivial cycle predicates. Their automorphism group has order eight and
  exchanges the cycles, so only constants are canonical. Durability still
  does not imply identification.
- A fixed point plus a three-cycle has automorphism group order three. Its
  two dynamical orbits support a nontrivial invariant partition. Bare
  asymmetric dynamics can therefore reduce the symmetry without an externally
  declared coordinate.

The second control prevents overgeneralization: the result is not “dynamics
can never select an invariant.” It is that **no selection follows from the
generic words durability and copyability**, and full-representation scalar
invariance can be trivial.

## 11. Newly visible and previously misused outcomes

1. **Circuit covariance and observable naturality are separate theorem
   surfaces.** The first can hold while a proposed record channel fails.
2. **A record can be covariant without being canonical.** Coordinate and
   parity transport exactly, but the bare identity dynamics selects neither.
3. **Copyability has two burdens.** The copy map must be physically allowed,
   and the copied algebra must already have been selected.
4. **The RVS overlay can help only after intervention handles are physical.**
   An analyst-selected controller boundary merely replaces an
   analyst-selected readout.
5. **Einselection is not a counterexample to the theorem.** The
   system-environment interaction is precisely the additional structure that
   reduces the symmetry.
6. **No-broadcasting and record nonidentification are orthogonal.** One limits
   copying; the other can persist even when copying is unrestricted.
7. **“Representation independent” should always name its group/category.**
   Without that field, a proposed invariance is either too weak to protect
   physics or too strong to admit nontrivial observables.
