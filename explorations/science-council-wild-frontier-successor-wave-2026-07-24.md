# Wild-frontier successor wave

## Result first

The most important correction is not a new analogy:

> Causal-post renormalization is an existing exact construction in classical
> sequential growth (CSG), and the factorial bare-coupling family really does
> produce an after-post effective coupling proportional to the inverse square
> root of the post's past size.

But the half-power is not selected by the post map alone.

For the admissible family

```text
t_n = u^n/(n!)^alpha,
```

the exact finite post map and a saddle calculation give

```text
T_n(P)       = sum_(k=0)^P binom(P,k) t_(n+k)
T_(n+1)/T_n ~ u^(1/(alpha+1)) P^(-alpha/(alpha+1)).
```

Thus:

```text
alpha=0       no running (transitive-percolation fixed point)
alpha=1/2     P^-1/3
alpha=1       P^-1/2
alpha=2       P^-2/3.
```

The executable finite transforms return fitted exponents
`0.000000, -0.337891, -0.498091, -0.649039`, respectively. The
factorial-family half-power is real and striking. A universal causal-post
half-power is overturned.

This changes the priority substantially. `WF-D1` is no longer a speculative
wish-list metaphor; it is a known covariant history-to-effective-law
mechanism. The next discriminator is not “can posts renormalize couplings?”
It is:

> What selects the factorial tail class, do repeated large-past posts actually
> occur in that class, and does the resulting dimensionless coupling control a
> held-out geometric or physical response?

No `Lambda` identity, unit-bearing scale, geometry recovery, selected family,
or repeated-post theorem is earned here.

## 4. Bounded evidence-producing swing

### Analytic calculation

For

```text
t_n=u^n/(n!)^alpha,
a_k=binom(P,k)u^(n+k)/((n+k)!)^alpha,
```

successive terms obey

```text
a_(k+1)/a_k
  = [(P-k)/(k+1)] u/(n+k+1)^alpha.
```

At fixed small `n` and large `P`, the dominant index is

```text
k_*~(P u)^(1/(alpha+1)).
```

The effective percolation ratio is the weighted average of
`t_(n+k+1)/t_(n+k)=u/(n+k+1)^alpha`, hence

```text
T_(n+1)/T_n
  ~ u/k_*^alpha
  ~ u^(1/(alpha+1)) P^(-alpha/(alpha+1)).
```

For `alpha=1`, the binomial approximation can also be written using modified
Bessel functions, and its large-argument ratio gives `sqrt(u/P)`.

### Exact finite transform

The probe evaluates every term of the finite binomial transform for
`P=256..65536`, physical `n=1..4`, and
`alpha in {0,1/2,1,2}`. It passes `7/7`
execution checks.

| `alpha` | Predicted `P` exponent | Fitted exponent | physical `T_2/T_1` / asymptotic at `P=65536` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `0` | `0` | `0.000000` | `1.000000` |
| `1/2` | `-1/3` | `-0.337891` | `1.007946` |
| `1` | `-1/2` | `-0.498091` | `0.999004` |
| `2` | `-2/3` | `-0.649039` | `0.975620` |

The fitted `u` exponents also match `1/(alpha+1)` within `0.0090`.
At the largest `P`, the ratios across fixed physical `n=1..4` are nearly
percolation-like; their relative spreads are `0`, `0.00063`, `0.00589`, and
`0.05108`.


No path-covariance violation, fitted epoch, or negative coupling is required.
