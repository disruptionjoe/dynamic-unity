# Operational completion-closure trichotomy

## Executive result

Dynamic Unity had repeatedly used a binary contrast:

```text
fixed-completion disclosure
    versus
productive escape
```

That contrast is incomplete. There is a third exact case:

> A realized complete history can lie outside the admitted completion class
> while every finite observer-accessible record remains compatible with some
> member of that class.

The right classifier is the **operational closure** of the completion class,
not class membership alone.

For a declared class \(C\) of completed histories and an operational topology
\(\tau_R\) generated by the observer's admitted finite-record fibres, every
realized history \(x\) lies in exactly one class:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
x\in C
& &&\text{absorbed},\\
x\in\overline C^{\,R}\setminus C
& &&\text{limit-underdetermined},\\
x\notin\overline C^{\,R}
& &&\text{finite productive escape}.
\end{aligned}}
\]

The middle case is load-bearing. It means global nonmembership without any
zero-error finite certificate of nonmembership. It is neither disclosure nor
operationally demonstrated escape.

Four consequences follow.

1. A finite record excludes \(C\) exactly when the realized history is
   outside the operational closure \(\overline C^{\,R}\).
2. Every history outside \(C\) is eventually finitely refutable exactly when
   \(C\) is closed in the operational topology.
3. The hypothesis family has at least one exact finite falsifier exactly when
   \(C\) is not dense.
4. Enlarging observer capabilities refines the operational topology and can
   shrink the limit-underdetermined region; passive records cannot receive
   credit for a distinction exposed only after that capability expansion.

An explicit bounded/unbounded control then formalizes the recurring
squeeze-to-vacuity pattern:

- in this control, every fixed finite generator budget \(k\) defines a closed
  completion class with finite counterexamples;
- the union over all finite budgets can be dense;
- once dense, the unbounded version has no exact finite counterexample at all.

Thus the budget is not evidence that a substrate exists. It is what keeps a
large family of substrate or disclosure hypotheses empirically contentful.
Retreating from every bounded class to an unrestricted finite-but-unbounded
union can remove the finite empirical content the hypothesis began with.

This is exact and useful, but the mathematical core is standard topology,
safety/liveness, monitorability, and computability. The Dynamic Unity return
is the typed application to completion classes, finite records, capability
expansion, and the disclosure/escape boundary. It is not new mathematics or
new physics.

## 1. Operational topology

Let:

- \(\Omega\) be a nonempty set of complete physical or counterfactual
  histories;
- \(C\subseteq\Omega\) be a frozen completion class admitted by the rival
  theory;
- \(R\) be the observer's frozen finite-record and finite-experiment class;
  and
- \(\mathcal B_R\) be the family of nonempty completion fibres left possible
  by individual finite records or finite intersections of jointly obtainable
  records.

Assume \(\mathcal B_R\) covers \(\Omega\) and is a basis. It generates the
**operational topology**

\[
\tau_R
=
\left\{
\bigcup\mathcal U:
\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal B_R
\right\}.
\]

This topology is not asserted to be spacetime topology. It encodes which
sets of complete histories can be separated by the admitted finite
record/action contract.

For \(x\in\Omega\), a basis element \(B\in\mathcal B_R\) with \(x\in B\)
is the residual completion fibre after some finite admissible experiment.

### Definition 1 — exact finite exclusion

A finite record **excludes** \(C\) at \(x\) when

\[
\exists B\in\mathcal B_R:
\qquad
x\in B
\quad\text{and}\quad
B\cap C=\varnothing.
\]

This is a zero-error statement. It says every completion compatible with the
record lies outside the rival class.

It is stronger than:

- low likelihood under one supplied probability measure;
- poor compression by one model;
- temporary predictive error;
- inability of one bounded agent to produce an explanation; or
- asymptotic identification after indefinitely many observations.

## 2. The completion-closure theorem

Write

\[
\overline C^{\,R}
=
\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_R}(C).
\]

### Theorem 1 — operational completion-closure trichotomy

For every \(x\in\Omega\), exactly one of the following holds:

1. **Absorbed**
   \[
   x\in C.
   \]
2. **Limit-underdetermined**
   \[
   x\in\overline C^{\,R}\setminus C.
   \]
3. **Finite productive escape**
   \[
   x\notin\overline C^{\,R}.
   \]

### Proof

Because \(C\subseteq\overline C^{\,R}\), the three sets

\[
C,\qquad
\overline C^{\,R}\setminus C,\qquad
\Omega\setminus\overline C^{\,R}
\]

are pairwise disjoint and their union is \(\Omega\). \(\square\)

The partition itself is elementary. Its scientific value is that it blocks
an invalid inference:

```text
x is not generated by C
    does not imply
some finite record proves escape from C.
```

### Theorem 2 — finite-certificate criterion

A finite admissible record excludes \(C\) at \(x\) if and only if

\[
x\notin\overline C^{\,R}.
\]

### Proof

If a basis element \(B\) contains \(x\) and is disjoint from \(C\), then
\(B\) is an open neighborhood of \(x\) disjoint from \(C\). Hence \(x\) is
not in the closure of \(C\).

Conversely, if \(x\notin\overline C^{\,R}\), the open complement
\(\Omega\setminus\overline C^{\,R}\) contains \(x\). Because
\(\mathcal B_R\) is a basis, some \(B\in\mathcal B_R\) contains \(x\) and is
contained in that complement. Therefore \(B\cap C=\varnothing\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 1 — closedness is global finite refutability

Every false complete history has a finite exclusion certificate,

\[
\forall x\notin C,\ \exists B\in\mathcal B_R:
x\in B,\ B\cap C=\varnothing,
\]

if and only if \(C\) is closed in \(\tau_R\).

### Proof

The displayed property says precisely that \(\Omega\setminus C\) is open.
\(\square\)

### Corollary 2 — nondensity is nonzero exact empirical content

There exists at least one finite record fibre excluding \(C\) if and only if
\(C\) is not dense:

\[
\exists B\in\mathcal B_R,\ B\cap C=\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\overline C^{\,R}\neq\Omega.
\]

Thus a completion class that is dense in the operational topology has no
zero-error finite falsifier. It may still carry probabilistic or asymptotic
content after additional structure is supplied.

## 3. Capability monotonicity

Let \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) be two observer contracts on the same complete
history class, with

\[
\tau_{R_1}\subseteq\tau_{R_2}.
\]

The second observer can make every finite distinction available to the first
and possibly more.

### Theorem 3 — capability-refinement monotonicity

\[
\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_{R_2}}(C)
\subseteq
\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_{R_1}}(C).
\]

Consequently, capability enlargement can turn a
limit-underdetermined history into finite escape, but it cannot turn an
already available finite escape certificate into limit underdetermination.

### Proof

Closure is the intersection of all closed supersets. A finer topology has
more open sets and therefore more closed sets, so its closure cannot be
larger. Equivalently, a separating \(R_1\)-open neighborhood is also
\(R_2\)-open. \(\square\)

This is the completion-class companion to `HC-DU-050B`. There, capability
enlargement refines action-relevant history equivalence. Here, it refines the
operational topology and can shrink the completion closure.

The attribution rule is:

```text
passive record under R1 does not prove escape
new intervention under R2 proves escape
    =>
credit the R2 capability expansion, not the old record.
```

## 4. Cantor-space specialization

Let \(\Sigma\) be a finite alphabet with at least two symbols and let

\[
\Omega=\Sigma^\omega.
\]

For a finite word \(u\in\Sigma^*\), define the cylinder

\[
[u]
=
\{x\in\Sigma^\omega:u\preceq x\}.
\]

The cylinders form the standard product-topology basis. Define the prefix
language of \(C\subseteq\Sigma^\omega\):

\[
\operatorname{Pref}(C)
=
\{u\in\Sigma^*:[u]\cap C\neq\varnothing\}.
\]

### Lemma 1 — prefix characterization

For \(x\in\Sigma^\omega\),

\[
x\in\overline C
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall n,\ x{\upharpoonright}n\in\operatorname{Pref}(C).
\]

### Proof

The cylinders generated by the finite prefixes of \(x\) are a neighborhood
basis at \(x\). The point lies in the closure exactly when every such
neighborhood intersects \(C\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3 — earliest productive escape

Finite productive escape occurs exactly when

\[
\exists n:
x{\upharpoonright}n\notin\operatorname{Pref}(C).
\]

When such \(n\) exists, the least one is the first exact escape record. When
none exists, no longer passive prefix will repair the exact certification
failure.

## 5. Bounded versus unbounded completion classes

Take \(\Sigma=\{0,1\}\). For each integer \(k\geq1\), define

\[
C_k
=
\left\{
uv^\omega:
|u|\leq k,\ 1\leq|v|\leq k
\right\}.
\]

These are eventually periodic histories with a bounded preperiod and period.

### Proposition 1 — every fixed bound is finitely refutable

Each \(C_k\) is finite and therefore closed. Hence every
\(x\notin C_k\) has a finite prefix excluding \(C_k\).

### Proof

There are finitely many binary choices for \(u\) and \(v\) under the length
bound, so \(C_k\) is finite. Finite subsets of Cantor space are closed.
Corollary 1 applies. \(\square\)

No uniform observation length is implied. A history may agree with a member
of \(C_k\) for an arbitrarily long time before diverging. The claim is only
that each false complete history eventually exposes a finite bad prefix.

Now remove the bound:

\[
C_{\mathrm{ep}}
=
\bigcup_{k\geq1}C_k,
\]

the class of all eventually periodic binary histories.

### Proposition 2 — the unbounded union is dense but proper

\[
\overline{C_{\mathrm{ep}}}
=
\{0,1\}^\omega,
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{ep}}\neq\{0,1\}^\omega.
\]

### Proof

Given any finite word \(u\), the history \(u0^\omega\) is eventually
periodic and belongs to the cylinder \([u]\). Therefore every cylinder
intersects \(C_{\mathrm{ep}}\), so the class is dense.

The class is proper because non-eventually-periodic binary histories exist;
for example, the history formed by successively concatenating
\(0^n1\) for \(n=1,2,\ldots\) has unbounded zero-run lengths and cannot become
periodic. \(\square\)

### Corollary 4 — bounded/unbounded empirical-content squeeze

For every fixed \(k\), a history outside \(C_k\) has a finite exclusion
certificate. For every \(x\notin C_{\mathrm{ep}}\),

\[
x\in
\overline{C_{\mathrm{ep}}}\setminus C_{\mathrm{ep}},
\]

so no finite prefix excludes the unbounded union.

This is the exact shape behind a recurring retreat:

```text
fixed resource bound
    -> risky finite content

null result
    -> increase the bound

union over every finite bound
    -> safe dense class
    -> no exact finite falsifier
```

The theorem does not say every unbounded physical theory is dense. A theory
may retain invariant forbidden patterns at every resource scale. It says
that **if** the enlarged completion class becomes operationally dense, the
zero-error finite empirical content is gone.

## 6. Computability and fixed-oracle controls

Let \(C_{\mathrm{comp}}\) be the set of computable binary histories.

### Proposition 3 — computable histories are dense and proper

\[
\overline{C_{\mathrm{comp}}}
=
\{0,1\}^\omega,
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{comp}}\neq\{0,1\}^\omega.
\]

### Proof

Every cylinder \([u]\) contains the computable history \(u0^\omega\), proving
density. There are countably many finite programs but uncountably many binary
histories, so noncomputable histories exist. \(\square\)

### Corollary 5 — no finite proof of noncomputability

If \(x\) is noncomputable, then

\[
x\in
\overline{C_{\mathrm{comp}}}\setminus C_{\mathrm{comp}}.
\]

No finite prefix of \(x\) proves that its complete continuation is
noncomputable.

This corrects a common overclaim:

```text
no bounded predictor explains the observed prefix
    !=
the physical source is noncomputable
    !=
the source escaped every fixed completion.
```

For a fixed program-size bound \(k\), the set of infinite histories generated
by programs of length at most \(k\) is finite and therefore closed, assuming
the generated total histories are fixed as the comparison class. Removing
every size bound produces the dense computable class.

There is a second distinction:

```text
a finite separating prefix exists
    !=
an effective monitor can find or certify it.
```

If totality or class membership is undecidable, topological finite
refutability does not automatically supply an executable assay.

### Fixed-oracle consequence

Temporal Issuance's read-only `E199` correction constructs one oracle fixed at
stage zero that contains the realized path or an entire counterfactual
response tree. `E203` then observes that unrestricted oracle coverage admits
the reflexive rival \(O=J_H\).

The present theorem independently recovers the operational boundary:

- if the admitted oracle class has an extension for every finite record, it
  is dense in the record topology;
- no finite trace can exclude it;
- fixedness at stage zero is irrelevant to density; and
- a positive physical result requires an independently justified
  degree/access, causal, future-independence, or construction restriction
  that makes some finite record fibre disjoint from the rival class.

This verifies the logic at DU's record-certification layer. It does not import
Temporal Issuance's source ontology or establish issuance.

## 7. Adaptive interventions and branch-family oracles

Let \(I\) be a finite intervention alphabet and \(O\) a finite outcome
alphabet. A complete deterministic counterfactual response object assigns
outcomes to every admitted finite intervention history. Let \(\Omega_T\) be
the resulting response-tree space.

An admitted finite experiment observes finitely many compatible
intervention-response coordinates, possibly across finitely many declared
resets. Its transcript defines a cylinder of response trees agreeing on those
coordinates. These cylinders generate an operational topology
\(\tau_{\mathrm{exp}}\).

### Theorem 4 — intervention-tree closure criterion

For \(C_T\subseteq\Omega_T\), a finite adaptive experiment excludes \(C_T\)
at realized tree \(T\) exactly when

\[
T\notin
\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_{\mathrm{exp}}}(C_T).
\]

If every finite admissible transcript has an extension in \(C_T\), then
\(C_T\) is operationally dense and no finite adaptive experiment excludes
it.

### Proof

Apply Theorem 2 to the experiment-cylinder basis. \(\square\)

This does not make intervention useless. Interventions refine the topology.
A trace-only oracle class may fail under a branch not encoded in it. But a
completed branch-family oracle is not defeated merely because the experiment
is adaptive: if it contains a response tree extending every finite admitted
transcript, the class remains dense.

The result is explicitly observer- and capability-relative:

- a single irreversible trial exposes fewer coordinates than a resettable
  experiment family;
- inaccessible rejected attempts generate a coarser topology than
  implementation-complete acquisition;
- adding a source-selective intervention may refine the topology enough to
  expose a finite escape; and
- none of those refinements proves that the rival restriction is selected by
  fundamental physics.

## 8. Collision with known theory

### Safety and liveness

Alpern and Schneider,
[“Defining Liveness”](https://hdl.handle.net/1813/6495) (1985), give the
topological characterization behind the core theorem:

- safety properties are closed sets of infinite executions; violations have
  finite bad prefixes;
- liveness properties are dense; every finite prefix can still be extended
  to a satisfying execution; and
- every property decomposes into safety and liveness components.

Theorem 2 and Corollaries 1--2 are direct specializations of that topology.
Dynamic Unity does not claim priority for them.

### Runtime monitorability

Bauer,
[“Monitorability of \(\omega\)-regular
languages”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.3638) (2010), distinguishes good, bad,
undetermined, and non-monitorable prefixes and proves monitorability results
for LTL and \(\omega\)-regular languages.

Runtime monitorability is broader than the present one-sided finite-exclusion
question. A property can be monitorable because some finite extension
eventually supplies either a positive or negative verdict even when the
current prefix supplies neither. Dynamic Unity uses the simpler exact closure
criterion because the claim under test is finite exclusion of a completion
class, not full two-sided monitor synthesis.

### Identification in the limit

Gold,
[“Language identification in the
limit”](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(67)91165-5) (1967), studies
learners that may revise hypotheses and converge after receiving an unbounded
stream.

Identification in the limit can succeed without a known finite stopping
certificate. It therefore does not repair a claim that one finite record
already proves global openness.

### Computability and algorithmic randomness

The density of computable histories and the impossibility of certifying
noncomputability from one finite prefix are elementary. Algorithmic-randomness
theory supplies much stronger infinite-sequence notions, but a passing finite
battery is evidence relative to the battery, not proof that the infinite
physical history is algorithmically random or noncomputable.

### Substrate-hypothesis consequence

families:

- a fixed budget can forbid finite patterns and carry excess empirical
  content;
- an unbounded or hypercomputational retreat may enlarge the completion class;
- if that enlargement is operationally dense, it has surrendered exact finite
  content;

  content for a different reason—it predicts the same operational fibres by
  construction.

The theorem does not prove that any game-engine, cellular-automaton, quantum-
computational, preferred-frame, or self-implementing substrate class is dense.
Each must define its completion class and operational topology.

## 11. Stop and reopener

### Stop

Do not:

- treat global nonmembership as a finite empirical result;
- certify noncomputability, algorithmic randomness, or source novelty from a
  finite trace;
- use “fixed at stage zero” as a bound on oracle content;
- enlarge a rival after every null without reporting that its operational
  closure changed;
- call a dense completion class falsifiable merely because individual members
  are; or
- build another finite source toy whose classification follows from this
  theorem.

### Reopener

A physical openness or productive-escape claim may reopen only after freezing:

1. the physical completion class \(C\);
2. the observer's operational topology \(\tau_R\), including acquisition
   visibility and admitted interventions;
3. a physically justified resource/oracle/access bound;
4. one finite record fibre \(B\) with
   \[
   B\cap C=\varnothing;
   \]
5. a realizability and no-refit argument for that record; and
6. the strongest enlarged completion class under which the same fibre remains
   disjoint.

If only \(x\notin C\) is known while
\(x\in\overline C^{\,R}\), the honest return remains
`LIMIT_UNDERDETERMINED`.

## Bottom line

> A history can be genuinely outside a completion class without ever
> producing a finite record that proves it. Finite productive escape is not
> nonmembership; it is nonmembership in the operational closure. Fixed
> resource bounds can preserve that distinction, while an unbounded union can
> erase it by becoming dense.
