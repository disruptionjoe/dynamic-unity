# HC-DU-033A record-selection quotient — novelty audit

## Verdict

The finite quotient, access meet/join law, and nondistributive hardening-order
example are mathematically known terrain. They should not be presented as new
theorems.

Dynamic Unity's defensible contribution at this stage is a **typed
specialization and research architecture**:

- it places dynamics/symmetry survival, coalition access, record formation,
  provenance, adversarial finality, and capability in distinct slots;
- it supplies exact controls showing where those slots cannot be collapsed;
  and
- it identifies a potentially useful but still
  **SEARCH-INCOMPLETE conjunction**: provenance-preserving joint
  hardening across classical adversarial and correlated quantum coalition
  channels.

No source located in this bounded audit establishes that intended full
conjunction. That is not a novelty claim; it is the reason the next search and
theorem must be claim-specific.

## Classification rule

- **KNOWN** — the claim is a standard theorem or direct statement in prior
  literature.
- **SPECIALIZATION** — the claim follows directly by instantiating known
  mathematics in the DU contract.
- **POSSIBLE CONJUNCTION** — the ingredients are known, but the precise typed
  combination may be a contribution if a nontrivial theorem or bound is
  obtained.
- **SEARCH-INCOMPLETE** — this audit does not license novelty language.

## Primary collision map

### Quotients, congruences, and information lattices

- Saunders Mac Lane, [*Categories for the Working
  Mathematician*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4757-4721-8), provides the
  general categorical universal-construction setting for coequalizers.
- Garrett Birkhoff,
  [“On the Structure of Abstract
  Algebras”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100013463), is foundational
  congruence-lattice terrain.
- Claude Shannon,
  [“The Lattice Theory of
  Information”](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1953.1188572), treats
  information elements through lattice structure.
- Hua Li and Edwin K. P. Chong,
  [“On a Connection between Information and Group
  Lattices”](https://doi.org/10.3390/e13030683), explicitly identify
  information elements with sample-space partitions and connect arbitrary
  partitions to orbit partitions of permutation actions.
- Peter Gács and János Körner,
  [“Common Information Is Far Less Than Mutual
  Information”](https://cs-web.bu.edu/faculty/gacs/papers/commoninf.pdf),
  establish the deterministic common-part lineage. The present zero-error
  coalition-common factor is in that neighborhood, not a newly discovered
  notion of public information.

These sources make the quotient and meet/join mathematics a known baseline.

### Symmetry, asymmetry, and equivariant records

- Stephen Bartlett, Terry Rudolph, and Robert Spekkens,
  [“Reference Frames, Superselection Rules, and Quantum Information”](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.79.555),
  show why lacking or supplying a reference frame changes accessible
  information and why physical covariance is not literal coordinate
  invariance.
- Gilad Gour and Robert Spekkens,
  [“The Resource Theory of Quantum Reference Frames”](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/10/3/033023),
  and Iman Marvian and Robert Spekkens,
  [“Modes of Asymmetry”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.90.062110),
  provide established machinery for symmetry-relative information and
  equivariant structure.

Therefore the DU distinction between invariant fixed labels and transported
equivariant channels is necessary but not novel.

### Copyability and quantum objectivity

- Howard Barnum, Carlton Caves, Christopher Fuchs, Richard Jozsa, and
  Benjamin Schumacher,
  [“Noncommuting Mixed States Cannot Be Broadcast”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.76.2818),
  supplies the no-broadcasting boundary.
- Wojciech Zurek,
  [“Pointer Basis of Quantum Apparatus: Into What Mixture Does the Wave Packet Collapse?”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.24.1516),
  and Harold Ollivier, David Poulin, and Wojciech Zurek,
  [“Objective Properties from Subjective Quantum States”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.93.220401),
  make interaction and environmental redundancy part of the selection and
  accessibility story.
- Jarosław Korbicz, Paweł Horodecki, and Ryszard Horodecki,
  [“Objectivity in a Noisy Photonic Environment through Quantum State Information Broadcasting”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.120402),
  characterize spectrum-broadcast structure as an objectivity mechanism.
- Thao P. Le and Alexandra Olaya-Castro,
  [“Strong Quantum Darwinism and Strong Independence Are Equivalent to Spectrum Broadcast Structure”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.122.010403),
  sharpen the relation among accessible information, independence, and
  broadcast structure.
- Fernando Brandão, Marco Piani, and Paweł Horodecki,
  [“Generic Emergence of Classical Features in Quantum Darwinism”](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms8908),
  show that many observers generically access classical information about a
  common measurement under broad channel conditions.

The finite DU scalar quotient begins after commutativity has effectively been
assumed. It cannot compete with, replace, or derive these quantum results.

### Access structures

- Mitsuru Ito, Akira Saito, and Takao Nishizeki,
  [“Secret Sharing Scheme Realizing General Access Structure”](https://archiv.infsec.ethz.ch/education/fs08/secsem/itsani87.pdf),
  and Josh Benaloh and Jerry Leichter,
  [“Generalized Secret Sharing and Monotone Functions”](https://doi.org/10.1007/0-387-34799-2_3),
  establish arbitrary monotone classical access structures.
- Daniel Gottesman,
  [“Theory of Quantum Secret Sharing”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.61.042311),
  provides the quantum-access counterpart.

Authorized coalitions are therefore known structure. A DU contribution must
arise from a new theorem linking access to formation, provenance, finality,
action, or cross-platform transfer.

### Common knowledge and Byzantine finality

- Joseph Halpern and Yoram Moses,
  [“Knowledge and Common Knowledge in a Distributed Environment”](https://doi.org/10.1145/79147.79161),
  separate distributed information from common knowledge and coordination.
- Leslie Lamport, Robert Shostak, and Marshall Pease,
  [“The Byzantine Generals Problem”](https://doi.org/10.1145/357172.357176),
  establish the adversarial agreement setting.
- Cynthia Dwork, Nancy Lynch, and Larry Stockmeyer,
  [“Consensus in the Presence of Partial Synchrony”](https://doi.org/10.1145/42282.42283),
  make timing assumptions explicit.
- Dahlia Malkhi and Michael Reiter,
  [“Byzantine Quorum Systems”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004460050050),
  characterize adversarial quorum intersection and availability.
- Maofan Yin and colleagues,
  [“HotStuff: BFT Consensus with Linearity and Responsiveness”](https://doi.org/10.1145/3293611.3331591),
  supplies a modern layered-certificate protocol benchmark.

These sources prevent the deterministic common factor from being called
agreement or finality. The next theorem must state authentication, locking,
fault, timing, provenance, and action assumptions rather than importing the
word “quorum.”

## Candidate novelty zone

The most credible major contribution is not the finite quotient alone. It is
a theorem or converse with this full typed surface:

```text
history-conditioned record formation
    + symmetry/asymmetry resources
    + direct coalition channels
    + provenance and adversarial access
    + compatible/incompatible certificate semantics
    + public finalization
    + bounded-risk action
```

The hardening-order counterexample suggests one specific hypothesis:

> A provenance-preserving joint channel can certify an invariant fact in
> regimes where every architecture that locally scalarizes before coalition
> composition necessarily loses it, with a tight converse characterizing
> when the two orders coincide.

For publication, this would need more than a partition-lattice example:

1. a nontrivial necessary-and-sufficient commutation criterion;
2. an adversarial certificate bound and converse;
3. a direct quantum-channel formulation rather than marginal-kernel analogy;
4. one common typed statement across the distributed and quantum fixtures;
5. a source search focused on that exact theorem; and
6. a physical or operational consequence unavailable from the known
   ingredients separately.
