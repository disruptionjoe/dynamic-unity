This run converts the six-card v0.1 pilot into a reusable but still bounded
method without beginning a phenomena-catalog expansion. It delivers three

1. a fail-closed v0.2 card/review/composition/release contract;

## Question and boundary

The working question was:

> Can one accepted-physics phenomenon be represented as a physically formed,
> provenance-bearing record graph; indexed by observer, interface, task,
> horizon, action menu, resources, risk, and normalization; then composed with
> GU requirements, P2C transition diagnosis, TaF capability/finality, TI
> disclosure/issuance, and Continuity relation discipline without inventing a
> missing map or promoting source evidence?

The accepted direction remains one-way:

\[
\text{accepted physics}\rightarrow\text{records}\rightarrow
\text{access}\rightarrow\text{normalized capability}\rightarrow
\text{candidate finality/structure}.
\]

Capability language does not derive accepted physics. A GU requirement row is
not a phenomenon merely because it appears in a physics ledger. A schema pass
does not validate a paraphrase scientifically.

## Contract hardening

### Card and release separation

The core card schema is independent of card count and identity. It validates
one phenomenon representation at a time. The release schema separately freezes
the exact card IDs, content digests, source-binding IDs, predecessor artifacts,
hostile controls, and non-routing posture.

This separation supports a real scalability control: a synthetic extra card
can satisfy the core schema without changing validator code, while the frozen
one-card v0.2 release rejects the extra ID. The control is test-only; no seventh
card is filed.

### Claim-level source grounding

Every binding declares a `claim_role`, physics-grounding status, independence
from the atlas mapping, revision, locator, selector, and SHA-256 digest.
Accepted-physics completeness requires at least one referenced binding that is:

- `PHYSICS_GROUNDING`;
- `INDEPENDENT_ACCEPTED`;
- independent of the atlas mapping; and
- paired with adequate domain review.

Method notes, transition classifiers, GU theory requirements, TaF capability
adjudications, TI completion returns, and atlas-authored mappings can constrain
or interpret a card. They cannot bootstrap an accepted-physics description.

### Review and record topology

The v0.2 review object preserves reviewer relation, evidence, limitations,
prior exposure, blindness, and per-field adjudication. The v0.2 card preserves
both:

- the seven-level status projection using `PRESENT`, `ABSENT`, `CONDITIONAL`,
  `UNKNOWN`, and `NOT_APPLICABLE`; and
- a typed record DAG whose nodes and edges can distinguish an occurrence, two
  retained endpoints, a common-occurrence relation, an estimate, a certificate,
  custody, calibration, and action support.

The v0.1 migration is explicit: `ESTABLISHED` maps to `PRESENT` and `FAILED`
maps to `ABSENT`. Legacy terms fail when placed directly in a v0.2 card.

### Projection and challenge typing

Projection witnesses declare whether a witness was supplied, whether visible
states are equal, whether capabilities are equal or different, and whether the
capability spread is zero or nonzero. The validator rejects contradictory
combinations rather than parsing a sentence prefix.

Evidence challenges are split into:

- strongest absorber;
- representation kill;
- claim falsifier;
- regime reopener; and
- held-out discriminator with opposed outcomes.

This prevents a repair—such as adding a physical common-event tag—from being
misreported as a falsifier of a theorem about the untagged packet.

### Source-first reconstruction

The phenomenon is a family-level dual-phase-xenon transduction and
event-association architecture, not one numerically frozen apparatus. An
interaction can form prompt S1 and delayed S2 traces, each of which can be
retained independently. The load-bearing target is the separate relation

\[
L_{ij}=\text{“S1}_i\text{ and S2}_j\text{ share one occurrence.”}
\]

An event builder estimates (L); it does not create physical provenance by
choosing a software partition. Exact eventwise reconstruction requires a
retained common tag or a justified unique-matchability condition.

The natural record topology is a DAG:

```text
underlying interaction
  ├─forms→ S1 trace ─retained_as→ S1 endpoint
  └─forms→ S2 trace ─retained_as→ S2 endpoint

(S1 endpoint, S2 endpoint)
  ├─candidate_for→ compatibility edge
  ├─related_by→ hidden common-occurrence relation L_ij
  └─estimated_by→ event-builder relation L_hat

(L_ij or warranted unique match, calibration)
  └─supports→ conditional depth/class certificate
```

The source does not select an observer, institutional right, public-final
profile, or action class. The v0.2 card marks its DAQ-archive and calibrated
event-builder profiles as atlas-selected rather than source facts.

### Exact failed square

The physical and counterfactual frames do not form one commuting square:

```text
same ring, N at T>Tc  -- physical COOL -->  same ring, S at Tf<Tc
        |                                      |
        | no admitted identity- and            |
        | budget-preserving map                 |
        v                                      v
reference N at Tf  ------ matched_with ---->  ring S at Tf
```

The top edge preserves material identity but is not final-temperature/resource
matched. The bottom edge is the budget-matched capability comparison but joins
counterfactual systems and is not physical evolution. No square preserves all
four of material identity, common final temperature, matched
`COOL/THREAD/PROBE` budget, and a physical transduction edge.

Accordingly, `COOL`, `matched_with`, `formed_by`, `held_by`, and
`material_continuity` remain different relation types. The record DAG forbids
`same_occurrence`, `same_system`, or `evolves_to` across the matched
counterfactual pair.
