## 2. General orbit-separation criterion

Let a group \(G\) act on a state space \(X\). Let

\[
\mathcal R\subseteq X
\]

be the states declared to contain a record and
\(\mathcal N=X\setminus\mathcal R\) those declared not to contain one.

A state-only representation-invariant record classifier is a map

\[
F:X\rightarrow Y
\]

such that:

1. \(F(gx)=F(x)\) for every \(g\in G\); and
2. no value assigned to a record state is assigned to a nonrecord state.

### Theorem 1 — orbit-separation criterion

Such an \(F\) exists at the set-theoretic level if and only if no \(G\)-orbit
meets both \(\mathcal R\) and \(\mathcal N\).

**Proof.**

If \(x_{\mathrm r}\in\mathcal R\) and
\(x_{\mathrm n}=g x_{\mathrm r}\in\mathcal N\), invariance gives

\[
F(x_{\mathrm r})=F(x_{\mathrm n}),
\]

contradicting separation.

Conversely, if every orbit lies wholly in \(\mathcal R\) or wholly in
\(\mathcal N\), the characteristic function of the induced subset of the
orbit space \(X/G\), composed with the quotient map, is invariant and
separates the classes. \(\square\)

The theorem is elementary group-action mathematics. Its value here is to
make the ontology burden exact: a record predicate must descend through the
declared representation quotient. If record and nonrecord specimens occupy
one orbit, no clever scalar fixes the problem.

## 3. Quantum two-bit obstruction

### 3.1 State-only full representation invariance

Let

\[
\mathcal H=\mathcal H_S\otimes\mathcal H_R
\cong\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2.
\]

A bare state-only functional that is independent of all global Hilbert-space
coordinates satisfies

\[
F(U\rho U^\dagger)=F(\rho)
\qquad
\text{for every }U\in U(4).
\]

Two density operators are globally unitarily equivalent exactly when their
spectra, including multiplicities, agree. Therefore every such \(F\) factors
through the spectrum.

Both specimens have spectrum

\[
\operatorname{spec}(\rho_{\mathrm{rec}})
=
\operatorname{spec}(\rho_{\mathrm{none}})
=
\left(\frac12,\frac12,0,0\right).
\]

One explicit permutation unitary sends the occupied basis pair
\(\{|00\rangle,|11\rangle\}\) to
\(\{|00\rangle,|01\rangle\}\), so the two states lie on one \(U(4)\) orbit.

But relative to the declared \(S|R\) split,

\[
I(S:R)_{\rho_{\mathrm{rec}}}=1\ \text{bit},
\qquad
I(S:R)_{\rho_{\mathrm{none}}}=0.
\]

The first is the ideal classical copy form for one binary value. The second
is a fixed system state beside independent record-register noise.

### Corollary 1 — no intrinsic state-only record scalar

No globally unitary-invariant functional of \(\rho\) alone can distinguish
even this minimal perfect record correlation from no system--record
correlation.

The conclusion is scoped. Once the observable algebras or subsystem split are
part of the physical contract, global unitaries that move that structure are
not mere representation changes. Local-unitary invariants such as mutual
information can then diagnose correlation. But the extra algebra/factorization
is doing essential physical work.

## 4. Classical four-state control

Let the same four labels be treated as a classical joint state space
\(\{00,01,10,11\}\). Define

\[
p_{\mathrm{rec}}(00)=p_{\mathrm{rec}}(11)=\frac12
\]

and

\[
p_{\mathrm{none}}(00)=p_{\mathrm{none}}(01)=\frac12.
\]

Both probability vectors have the unordered multiset

\[
\left\{\frac12,\frac12,0,0\right\}.
\]

A permutation of the four microstates maps one to the other. Thus any
functional invariant under all microstate relabelings gives the same value,
while mutual information relative to the declared two-coordinate schema is
one bit versus zero.

This control matters. The obstruction is not Bell nonlocality,
contextuality, collapse, or the quantum measurement problem. It is the more
general fact that relational structure cannot be recovered from an object
after quotienting away the relations that define it.

## 5. Why factorization is necessary but insufficient

Adding a physically meaningful system--record split permits a correlation
functional. It does not yet establish a **formed** or **accessible** record.

### 5.1 Endpoint does not identify formation

The same correlated terminal state can arise through:

1. a source-controlled copy into a blank register;
2. a preloaded correlated register followed by identity evolution;
3. an erase-and-rewrite path returning to the same endpoint; or
4. persistence of a record written before the declared epoch.

No terminal-state functional separates those histories. This is the general
form of the `HC-DU-043/044` occurrence-tokenization boundary:

\[
\text{correlated endpoint}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{write occurrence or provenance}.
\]

A formed-record claim therefore needs a process/history object, blank or
epoch condition, and retained occurrence distinction.

### 5.2 Reduced dynamics does not identify the archive

The same reduced dephasing channel can be implemented by:

- coupling the system to an environment that retains a pointer record; or
- applying a random unitary from a controller whose random variable is not
  the claimed pointer value.

The reduced system channel alone does not select which environmental
observable, route, or history is a record. This is the dilation/unraveling and
archive-relocation boundary already encountered by `HC-DU-033F/054/061`.

### 5.3 Latent correlation does not identify access or capability

Even a formed physical correlation need not be independently readable or
action-enabling for a given observer. Removing the access link while
preserving the remote carrier state changes capability without changing the
candidate remote record value.

Thus the minimum typed ladder is:

```text
state
  + selected algebra/factorization
    -> correlation candidate
  + process, blank/epoch, and retained provenance
    -> formed record candidate
  + observer access, decoder, actions, horizon, and resources
    -> operational record/certificate/finality candidate.
```

The arrows remain claims to test. The theorem says the omitted types cannot
all be reconstructed from a bare invariant state scalar.

## 7. What this changes for the North Star

The North Star asks whether independently selected certified causal records
reconstruct observer-accessible physics. The theorem makes
“independently selected records” stricter and more coherent.

A future record-first theory may use:

- a net of local observable algebras rather than a fixed tensor product;
- dynamically selected subsystems or reference frames;
- causal/process histories rather than instantaneous states;
- environment fragments or material archives;
- observer-relative access maps and action envelopes; or
- another physical relational structure.

But it must derive or independently justify that structure. It cannot claim
representation independence while silently using a preferred split, pointer
basis, epoch, archive, or access map to decide what counts as a record.

This also prevents an overcorrection. The global wavefunction, density
operator, Hamiltonian, QFT net, or causal process may still be a complete
physical description. The theorem does not decide whether physics or records
are fundamental. It says a record ontology cannot be obtained from the
instantaneous state **alone** while discarding all relational typing.
