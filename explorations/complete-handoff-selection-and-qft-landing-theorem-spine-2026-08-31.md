# 2. Spine part A — selection up to physical gauge

## Theorem A

There exists a deterministic selector

\[
s:A\to H/G
\]

such that

\[
q\circ\xi=s\circ a
\]

exactly when

\[
\ker a\subseteq\ker(q\circ\xi).
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, every two completions sharing the claimed antecedent must carry
the same complete handoff orbit.

## Proof

If the factorization exists, equality of antecedents implies equality of the
selected orbit. Conversely, if the orbit is constant on every antecedent
fibre, define \(s(\alpha)\) as the common orbit on that fibre. It is
well-defined on \(\operatorname{im}a\). \(\square\)

This is the existing `HC-DU-033F/137` factorization theorem applied to the
corrected relational handoff of `HC-DU-204`. Its mathematics is elementary.
Its physical burden is not: the quotient \(q\) must be fixed independently
and every declared response, provenance, access, timing, reliability,
resource, reset, and intervention field must descend. Endpoint equality is
not enough to declare gauge.

# 3. Spine part B — equivariant selection and residual symmetry

Suppose a group also acts on antecedents and handoffs. An equivariant selector
obeys

\[
s(ga)=g\,s(a).
\]

For an antecedent value \(\alpha\), let \(G_\alpha\) be its stabilizer.

## Theorem B

Every equivariantly selected point over \(\alpha\) must be fixed by
\(G_\alpha\). Thus:

- an empty stabilizer fixed-point set forbids a deterministic equivariant
  point selector;
- several fixed candidates leave covariance insufficient for uniqueness; and
- an invariant probability law is a different type and still does not select
  its sampler or realized record.

The proof is the one-line identity

\[
s(\alpha)=s(g\alpha)=g\,s(\alpha)
\]

for every \(g\in G_\alpha\). This is already banked in `HC-DU-156/207` and is
standard homogeneous-space reasoning. It belongs second in the spine because
one must first know which complete handoff object and physical quotient are
being selected.

# 4. Spine part C — matched descent does not remove the handoff

For a finite record wire \(R\), write a producer and consumer as

\[
K(r\mid x),\qquad C(y\mid r),
\]

with closed response

\[
H(y\mid x)=\sum_r C(y\mid r)K(r\mid x).
\]

A bijection of the internal record labels preserves \(H\) when producer and
consumer co-transform. This is `HC-DU-204`'s diagonal descent theorem.
Absolute label spelling can therefore be representational.

The converse shortcut is false. `HC-DU-205` proves that one producer and
formed record algebra support many record-preserving consumers with different
continuations. An inverse write erases the record and selects none of them.
Therefore:

```text
closed response descent
  does not imply
source selection of producer, archive, consumer or alignment.
```

The exact finite control exhausts all sixteen deterministic binary
producer-consumer pairs. Every matched label swap preserves the closed
response. The same writer with identity versus flip consumers produces
different responses and distinct handoff orbits. Adding an independently
physical alignment coordinate separates the antecedent fibres and repairs
selection; adding the desired consumer after seeing the target does not.

# 5. Spine part D — QFT landing and selection surplus

Let \(\mathcal Q_I(d)\) be the frozen incumbent QFT completion family after
training data and physical antecedents \(d\) are fixed. Let an upstream theory
select, before the held-out result, a nonempty family
\(\mathcal Q_S(d)\). Let \(\tau\) be a held-out target.

The following types must be kept separate.

## QFT expressibility

\[
\mathcal Q_S(d)\subseteq\mathcal Q_I(d).
\]

The upstream construction lands inside QFT. This is normally required for a
healthy low-energy limit; it is not redundancy.

## Structural selection surplus

\[
\varnothing\neq\mathcal Q_S(d)\subsetneq\mathcal Q_I(d).
\tag{2}
\]

The upstream theory derives or selects some QFT input that the frozen
incumbent family left open. Examples could include field content, symmetry
breaking orbit, parameter relation, sector, boundary condition, or coupling
sign. Equation (2) can hold even when the chosen target cannot yet see the
difference.

## Target classifications

Write

\[
I_\tau=\tau(\mathcal Q_I(d)),
\qquad
S_\tau=\tau(\mathcal Q_S(d)).
\]

For an expressible upstream family:

1. **predictive absorption:** \(S_\tau=I_\tau\);
2. **source-selected sharpening:**
   \(\varnothing\neq S_\tau\subsetneq I_\tau\); or
3. **rival-excluding excess:** the challenger locks a value outside
   \(I_\tau\).

When (2) holds but \(S_\tau=I_\tau\), the result is
`STRUCTURAL_SELECTION_ONLY` for that target. It may be explanatory and may
enable another prediction, but it is not target excess.

This adds one useful type to `HC-DU-185/186`; the underlying set mathematics
is not new. It stops two opposite errors:

```text
QFT can express the result
  therefore the upstream theory did nothing                    [false]

the upstream theory selected one QFT realization
  therefore it predicted every target better                   [false]
```

After-fact selection receives no credit. The incumbent QFT family, upstream
map, nuisance class, matching scale, and held-out target must be frozen before
reveal.
