The first-droplet proposal is more interesting than a generic statement that
cloud-chamber droplets amplify microscopic interactions. It offers a concrete
candidate for the missing direction seed: a randomly formed, barely
subcritical vapor cluster whose near-singular ionization response is driven
supercritical by the emitted particle.

The proposal nevertheless does not derive a new source of single-run
actuality. It explicitly assumes a classical stochastic description of
subcritical droplets, calls the underlying system deterministic, and treats
the apparent randomness as thermal. Once the particular chamber microstate is
included in the completion, the first track is selected by ordinary
asymmetric initial data. The detector then amplifies and retains that seed.
This is a valuable physical mechanism, but it is **disclosure from a more
complete microstate**, not a derivation of an ontic sample from a symmetric
unitary state.

The proposal's suggested low-amplitude cutoff is a separate matter. If a fixed
apparatus really responds according to

\[
f_c(q)=\max(q-c,0),\qquad q=\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho),\quad c>0,
\tag{1}
\]

then its unconditional click probability is not affine in the density matrix.
No fixed POVM effect can realize (1). That gives a genuine conditional delta
from standard quantum mechanics, but the existing data and model do not yet
choose between a density-level nonlinear rule, a branchwise hidden-state rule,
ordinary detector response, selection bias, or an incomplete attempt census.

## 1. What the proposal actually supplies

Schonfeld's 2021 mechanism uses four ingredients:

1. a slowly leaking emitted-particle wavefunction;
2. a classical picture of subcritical vapor clusters formed randomly by
   thermal fluctuations;
3. a near-degeneracy in Coulombic ionization when polarization energy nearly
   offsets molecular binding energy; and
4. rapid nucleation and later Mott collimation after one exceptional cluster is
   ionized.

The proposed first-droplet rate has the form

\[
\lambda(x,t)\simeq \rho_c A v\tau |\psi(x,t)|^2,
\tag{2}
\]

where the cluster population, singular cross-section coefficient, projectile
speed, and cluster lifetime are apparatus variables. Equation (2) is a
microphysical explanation for a position-space Born-shaped response. It is
not a derivation of the general projection postulate.

The source is unusually explicit about its boundary:

- the detector model is partly classical and partly quantum;
- subcritical clusters are assumed to form randomly;
- the randomness is taken to be thermal rather than intrinsically quantum;
- the complete system is said to be deterministic; and
- negligible residual amplitude is treated operationally as no longer
  significant rather than proved to vanish.

Those admissions make the DU typing possible without reading against the
author. The mechanism can explain **where the first asymmetry is carried**. It
does not yet explain **why one value is ontically realized** under a fully
quantum description.

## 2. First-droplet actualization trilemma

Let `D` be a transitive orbit of candidate track directions, `S` a microscopic
chamber-seed space, and

\[
A:S\longrightarrow R
\tag{3}
\]

the metastable amplifier from a first critical seed to a retained track.
Within the source classes actually under discussion, three cases must be kept
separate.

### 2.1 Pre-existing actual microstate

If one particular `s in S` is part of the complete initial physical state,
then `A(s)` can be one definite track. The symmetry of a coarse thermal
distribution over `S` is irrelevant to that run. The omitted microstate was
the selector.

This is not a failure. It is a deterministic hidden-configuration account of
the outcome. But relative to a completion class that already contains `s`, the
later track discloses and amplifies prior asymmetry. Calling the ensemble
"random" does not turn it into issuance or derive a sampler.

### 2.2 Objective stochastic innovation

A law can instead include ontic stochastic increments that generate one new
`s` or one direction during the process. That supplies a genuine one-run
actualization antecedent. GRW/CSL-type theories demonstrate that this option is
coherent physics.

But a probability kernel is not yet its sample. `HC-DU-156` proves that even a
unique invariant kernel need not select a physical sampler, realized label, or
archive. The objective status of the innovation and its coupling to the
chamber must be part of the law, not inferred from the observed frequency.
Whether such an innovation is source issuance belongs to Temporal Issuance;
DU credits only the actualization and record arrows it physically earns.

### 2.3 Closed unitary quantum dynamics

If the chamber, clusters, emitted particle, and environment are all described
unitarily from a symmetric antecedent, the output can be an entangled carrier

\[
|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{d\in D} c_d|d\rangle|R_d\rangle|E_d\rangle.
\tag{4}
\]

Decoherence can make the reduced track statistics mixture-like. It does not by
itself choose one global member. A branch-relative account, Bohmian actual
configuration, collapse noise, or operational outcome contract adds the
missing interpretation or ontology.

The three cases exhaust this **scoped model family**, not every logically
possible theory. Their reusable statement is:

> A metastable amplifier can select an outcome only relative to a physically
> actual seed. A fixed microstate makes the selection inherited disclosure;
> an ontic innovation makes it an added stochastic law; and closed unitary
> dynamics retains correlated alternatives unless an outcome ontology is
> separately supplied.

## 3. Amplification does not establish provenance

Suppose the amplifier is injective on direction seeds. Then one ideal track
can reconstruct the seed direction. That does not reconstruct what made the
seed actual.

The routes

```text
pre-existing thermal microstate
objective stochastic innovation
monitored conditional trajectory
branch-relative unitary record
```

can induce the same retained track alphabet and probabilities. Their endpoint
supports overlap. By `HC-DU-226`, no endpoint decoder can certify the route.
A route marker in a complete multi-time packet can restore provenance, but
that is a physical record refinement, not information latent in straightness.

Metastability therefore closes two real duties—amplification and finite
retention—while leaving seed selection and source lineage separate.

## 4. The convex-affinity gate

For every fixed quantum effect `0 <= E <= I`, the unconditional probability

\[
p_E(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho)
\tag{5}
\]

is affine:

\[
p_E(a\rho_1+(1-a)\rho_2)
=a p_E(\rho_1)+(1-a)p_E(\rho_2).
\tag{6}
\]

This remains true for an inefficient but fixed detector: replace `E` by
`eta E`. Dark counts, loss outcomes, and coarse graining can also be included
in a larger fixed instrument without breaking (6).

The offset rule (1) fails. Let `0<c<=1/2` and choose diagonal states with

\[
q_0=0,\qquad q_2=2c,\qquad q_m=c={q_0+q_2\over2}.
\tag{7}
\]

Then

\[
f_c(q_m)=0,
\qquad
{f_c(q_0)+f_c(q_2)\over2}={c\over2}.
\tag{8}

Therefore no one fixed POVM effect reproduces the cutoff at all three states.
This is exact and parameter-independent apart from `c>0`.

### 4.1 Two readings, two risky consequences

If (1) is applied to the density operator itself, it predicts a direct convex-
mixture violation. Two operationally equivalent preparations of `rho_m` must
give different click frequencies depending on whether the device receives
`rho_m` directly or a randomized `rho_0/rho_2` ensemble.

If the cutoff is applied separately to hidden pure ensemble members, it makes
different decompositions of the same density matrix observable. For
`rho=I/2` and `c=1/4`:

- the equal `Z`-basis decomposition has mean response `3/8`; and
- the equal `X`-basis decomposition has mean response `1/4`.

That is preparation contextuality or extra ontic structure. Generic nonlinear
quantum extensions also face well-known no-signalling constraints; a complete
proposal must show how the local rule composes with entanglement.

### 4.2 What would not count

The following can look nonlinear without violating a fixed quantum
instrument:

- probability of at least one event across a changing number of trials;
- saturation or dead time in a detector;
- conditioning on accepted events;
- a source distribution correlated with chamber conditions;
- a detector efficiency that changes with position, energy, or time; and
- missing rejected attempts or classifier-dependent track inclusion.

They alter resources, the instrument, the admitted completion, or the sample
selection. The decisive observable is the **unconditional all-attempt
frequency for preparation-equivalent density operators under one frozen
apparatus**, with calibration and no-refit analysis.

## 5. Status of the evidence

Schonfeld's 2022 track-origin analysis used publicly available pedagogical
video, visual track identification, effectively two-dimensional geometry, and
an apparatus not designed for the question. The cutoff and offset curves were
fit after seeing an apparent deficit beyond about 22.5 mm. The paper itself
lists uncontrolled thermal, geometric, and classification alternatives and
calls the cutoff speculative.

The revised 2022/2025 normalization analysis compares rough cloud-chamber data
with extrapolated chemical-physics estimates and now reports order-unity
agreement, while retaining the caveat that the experiment and cluster model
are uncontrolled. The 2025 review continues to present the mechanism as a
unitary, emergent, approximate Born account and asks for purpose-built tests.
The 2025 superconducting-qubit paper extrapolates the cloud-chamber cutoff
through an impressionistic model of an analog-to-digital converter and says
its numerical estimates are rough and likely too small to matter practically.

These sources justify an **imported conditional prediction seam**. They do not
establish anomalous data, a universal detector law, or a DU prediction.

## 6. `PRED-DU-006` — exact conditional discriminator

The registered seam is:

> If one frozen detector has a nonzero state-amplitude offset response
> `f_c(q)=max(q-c,0)` at the all-attempt level, then it violates convex affinity.
> Preparation-equivalent density operators will produce a nonzero frequency
> contrast; no fixed standard-quantum POVM predicts that contrast.

It is not yet hardened. Before any empirical credit it needs:

1. a physical rule saying whether the cutoff acts on density operators,
   ensemble members, or a larger system--apparatus state;
2. an independently fixed nonzero cutoff and scaling law;
3. preparation-equivalent states with no hidden source--detector correlation;
4. a frozen apparatus, classifier, exposure, resource, and attempt census;
5. calibration of ordinary nonlinear electronics, dead time, saturation,
   dark counts, drift, and spatial efficiency;
6. a no-signalling and entangled-composition analysis; and
7. a preregistered contrast, uncertainty rule, falsifier, and retreat cost.

The cheap kill is exact: if a single fixed enlarged instrument and its
selection process reproduce the all-attempt data, the claimed Born-rule delta
is absorbed as detector physics. If the source cannot specify how mixtures
transform, the prediction remains underdefined.
