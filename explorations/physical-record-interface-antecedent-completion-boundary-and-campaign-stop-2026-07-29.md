## 1. Why the original cheapest kill had to be corrected

Swing 1 preregistered the following as its cheapest naturality obstruction:

> A physical automorphism preserves the entire antecedent and future-response
> structure while moving every candidate interface outside its operational
> equivalence class.

That cannot be the kill for the quotient as defined.

For a fixed future action family \(\mathcal A\), let a complete interface
implementation \(B\) have response signature

\[
\Sigma_{\mathcal A}(B)
\in
\prod_{a\in\mathcal A}\mathsf P(Y_a).
\]

Operational equivalence is:

\[
B\sim_{\mathcal A}B'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Sigma_{\mathcal A}(B)=\Sigma_{\mathcal A}(B')
\]

up to the declared physical isomorphisms of action and response spaces.

If an automorphism preserves the **complete future-response structure**, it
preserves this signature up to exactly those isomorphisms. It therefore maps
\(B\) into the same operational equivalence class. It cannot simultaneously
move \(B\) outside that class.

Symmetry remains a valid obstruction to selecting one raw coordinate,
pointer orientation, or microscopic implementation. It is not an
obstruction to the response quotient when the quotient has already divided
out every response-preserving symmetry.

The correct operational nonselection witness is:

\[
A(m)=A(m')
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Sigma_{\mathcal A}(B(m))
\neq
\Sigma_{\mathcal A}(B(m')).
\tag{1}
\]

In words: two complete realizations share the claimed antecedent but differ
under one already-admitted future action. That is the exact kernel failure
proved abstractly in `HC-DU-056`.

This correction is substantive for approach. A search for a
response-preserving automorphism outside response equivalence would never
return a legitimate witness. The finite swing must instead vary the physical
interface across one source-antecedent fibre.

\[

\[

## 6. Antecedent-completion theorem

Let:

- \(M\) be complete physical realizations;
- \(a_0:M\to\mathsf A_0\) be a claimed physical antecedent;
- \(B:M\to\mathsf B\) be the complete interface implementation;
- \(\mathcal A\) be a frozen future action family; and
- \(R_{\mathcal A}=\Sigma_{\mathcal A}\circ B\) be the complete response
  signature.

Then exactly the following selector dispositions are available.

### 1. Antecedent-relative selector

If

\[
\ker a_0\subseteq\ker R_{\mathcal A},
\]

the unique response-class selector exists on \(\operatorname{im}a_0\).

### 2. Physical premise completion

If the inclusion fails, an independently fixed physical premise
\(p:M\to\mathsf P\) repairs it exactly when

\[

\]

The selector is then real relative to the richer antecedent, and the
explanatory credit belongs to \(p\). Examples could include material
apparatus dynamics, a selected boundary condition, a physical reference, or
an autonomously selected access route.

### 3. Response-coded completion

The formal repair

\[
p_{\min}=R_{\mathcal A}
\]

always works. If it is introduced only after the desired action responses are
known, it merely copies the answer into the antecedent. It is action-coded
repair, not an independently physical selector.

### Proof

The first two statements are the factorization theorem of `HC-DU-056`
applied before and after antecedent refinement. The third follows by
identity:

\[
\ker(a_0,R_{\mathcal A})\subseteq\ker R_{\mathcal A}.
\]

The scientific distinction is the provenance of \(p\), not the elementary
set factorization. \(\square\)

This theorem is not a universal ban on physical record selection. It gives
